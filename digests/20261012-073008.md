### lung-key-evidence / nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-top-journals
- **KEYNOTE-010 at 5 years: pembrolizumab vs docetaxel in previously treated PD-L1+ NSCLC (phase 2/3, n=1,034).** OS HR was 0.70 (95% CI 0.61–0.80) for TPS ≥1% and 0.55 (0.44–0.69) for TPS ≥50%. 5-year OS was 15.6% vs 6.5% and 25.0% vs 8.2%, respectively. Of the 79 patients who completed 2 years, 83% were alive about 3 years later, and 11/21 responded to a second course. This confirms a standard already in place. Herbst RS, Garon EB, Kim DW et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34048946/
- **ORIENT-12: sintilimab or placebo plus gemcitabine-platinum in first-line squamous NSCLC (double-blind phase 3, n=357, China).** IRC-assessed PFS HR was 0.536 (95% CI 0.422–0.681) at a median follow-up of 12.9 months. Grade ≥3 TEAEs were 86.6% vs 83.1%. OS is not reported, and chemotherapy alone is no longer the standard comparator. Zhou C, Wu L, Fan Y et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34048947/
- **RATIONALE 304: tislelizumab plus pemetrexed-platinum vs chemotherapy in first-line nonsquamous NSCLC (open-label phase 3, n=332, 2:1, China).** Median PFS was 9.7 vs 7.6 months (HR 0.645, 95% CI 0.462–0.902), and responses were higher and more durable. OS is immature and the comparator is outdated, but the trial adds to the evidence base for tislelizumab, a PD-1 inhibitor. Lu S, Wang J, Yu Y et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34033975/
- **Neoadjuvant durvalumab with or without SBRT (8 Gy × 3) in stage I–IIIA NSCLC (single-centre randomised phase 2, n=60, Lancet Oncol).** Major pathological response was 53.3% with SBRT vs 6.7% without (OR 16.0, 95% CI 3.2–79.6), and half of the responders had a pCR. Grade 3–4 AEs were 20% vs 17%. This is a striking signal, but from a small single-centre trial, and survival outcomes are not yet reported. Altorki NK, McGraw TE, Borczuk AC et al. Lancet Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34015311/
- **AIRO GRADE recommendation on sequential chemotherapy plus hypofractionated RT vs concurrent CRT in unresectable stage III NSCLC.** Five studies were eligible. OS and late lung toxicity looked worse with the sequential/hypofractionated approach, and the panel recommends against it as an alternative to concurrent CRT. The recommendation confirms, rather than changes, the standard. Merlotti A, Bruni A, Borghetti P et al. Radiol Med 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33954898/

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen / nsclc-drivers
- **ACTIVE (CTONG1706): apatinib or placebo plus gefitinib in first-line EGFR-mutant NSCLC (double-blind phase III, n=313).** IRRC-assessed PFS was 13.7 vs 10.2 months (HR 0.71, p=0.019), and OS is immature. Grade ≥3 hypertension was 46.5% and proteinuria 17.8%. A gefitinib-based backbone is no longer the first-line standard. Zhao H, Yao W, Min X et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34033974/
- **IFCT-1503 ACE-Lung: afatinib with or without cetuximab in first-line EGFR-mutant NSCLC (randomised phase II, n=117).** The 9-month TTF rate was 59.3% vs 64.9% (median 11.1 vs 12.9 months), and the trial stopped for futility. PFS and OS were not improved. Higher baseline EGFR ctDNA allele frequency predicted shorter PFS. Cortot AB, Madroszyk A, Giroux-Leprieur E et al. Clin Cancer Res 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34031056/
- **Intrathecal pemetrexed plus dexamethasone for TKI-failed leptomeningeal metastases from EGFR-mutant NSCLC (single-arm phase 1/2, JTO; 30 patients at the 50 mg recommended dose).** The clinical response rate was 84.6% (22/26 evaluable) and median OS 9.0 months. Myelosuppression (30%) was the most frequent AE. The trial is small, single-arm, and uses clinical rather than radiological response. Fan C, Zhao Q, Li L et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33989780/
- **Capmatinib plus erlotinib in MET-positive NSCLC (phase I/II).** The RP2D was capmatinib 400 mg BID with erlotinib 150 mg. ORR was 50% in the EGFR-mutant TKI-resistant cohort (n=12) and 75% in EGFR wild-type (n=5). Rash (63%) was the most common AE. These are small, early data on a MET-directed post-TKI strategy. McCoach CE, Yu A, Gandara DR et al. JCO Precis Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34036220/
- **Lazertinib: First Approval (Drugs profile).** Lazertinib, a brain-penetrant third-generation EGFR TKI, received its first approval (Korea, January 2021) for T790M-positive NSCLC after prior EGFR TKI. This is a journal profile of the decision, not the decision itself. Dhillon S Drugs 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34028784/
- **FDA Approval Summary: pralsetinib (Clin Cancer Res).** Accelerated approval was granted on 4 September 2020 for metastatic RET fusion-positive NSCLC, and later for RET-altered thyroid cancers, based on the single-arm ARROW trial (ORR 57%, 95% CI 46–68, after platinum). The label warns of pneumonitis, hypertension and hepatotoxicity. This is a summary of an earlier decision. Kim J, Bradford D, Larkins E et al. Clin Cancer Res 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34045295/

### sclc
- **ALTER1202: anlotinib vs placebo as third-line or later treatment for SCLC (double-blind randomised phase 2, n=120, 2:1, China).** Median PFS was 4.1 vs 0.7 months (HR 0.19, 95% CI 0.12–0.32) and OS 7.3 vs 4.9 months (HR 0.53, 0.34–0.81). It is a Chinese placebo-controlled trial. Cheng Y, Wang Q, Li K et al. Br J Cancer 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34006926/
- **Once-daily 65 Gy/26 fractions vs twice-daily 45 Gy/30 fractions concurrent with EP in LS-SCLC (multicentre randomised phase II, n=182, China).** Median PFS was 17.2 vs 13.4 months (p=0.031), and OS 39.3 vs 33.6 months (p=0.137, not significant). Grade ≥3 lymphopenia was higher (71.7% vs 40.2%), but oesophagitis and pneumonitis were similar. The authors call for a phase 3, so this is relevant to the dose/fractionation question raised by the Nordic 60 Gy BID trial. Qiu B, Li Q, Liu J et al. Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33992717/
- **Veliparib plus carboplatin-etoposide, then veliparib maintenance, in first-line ES-SCLC (randomised phase 2, n=181).** PFS HR was 0.67 (80% CI 0.50–0.88; median 5.8 vs 5.6 months), but OS was numerically worse (HR 1.43, 80% CI 1.09–1.88). Grade 3–4 AEs were 82% vs 68%. This is another PARP-inhibitor failure in unselected SCLC. Byers LA, Bentsion D, Gans S et al. Clin Cancer Res 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33947690/
- **Prexasertib (CHK1 inhibitor) in relapsed ES-SCLC (phase II, platinum-sensitive n=58, platinum-resistant n=60).** ORR was 5.2% and 0%, and median OS 5.4 and 3.2 months. Haematological toxicity was common. The drug is not active enough for further development as monotherapy. Byers LA, Navarro A, Schaefer E et al. Clin Lung Cancer 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34034991/

### lung-local-therapy / lung-nordic / lung-nonpharm
- **Overall survival after radiotherapy for NSCLC brain metastases (population-based, South-Eastern Norway Regional Health Authority, n=2,140, 2006–2018).** SRT use rose from 19% to 45%. Median OS was 7.0 months after SRT and 3.0 after WBRT, and 50% of WBRT patients died within 90 days. Survival improved over time only in the SRT group. This is a Norwegian real-world study that questions WBRT use; it is retrospective and subject to selection bias. Karlsson AT, Hjermstad MJ, Omdahl T et al. Acta Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34032536/
- **Upfront vs deferred brain RT with carboplatin-pemetrexed ± bevacizumab for asymptomatic brain metastases (open-label phase III, n=91, stopped early for slow accrual).** Median PFS was 4.7 vs 4.8 months and OS 8.5 vs 8.3 months. Only 20% of the deferred arm ever received brain RT. The trial is underpowered, but suggests upfront brain RT is not mandatory. Monnet I, Vergnenègre A, Robinet G et al. Ther Adv Med Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33948123/
- **Bilateral mediastinal lymphadenectomy via an added cervical incision vs systematic node dissection (randomised, n=102, stage I–IIIA).** 5-year survival was 72% vs 53% (OR 2.33, 95% CI 0.95–5.69, p=0.062, not significant). A subgroup benefit appeared for left lower lobe tumours, and operative time was longer. The trial is small, and the subgroup finding is exploratory. Kużdżał J, Trybalski Ł, Hauer Ł et al. Lung Cancer 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33962764/
- **RVlob: robotic vs video-assisted thoracoscopic lobectomy (single-centre RCT, n=320, short-term results).** Hospital stay and complications were similar. Robotic surgery harvested more nodes (11 vs 10) and stations, but cost more ($12,821 vs $8,009). Long-term oncological outcomes are pending. Jin R, Zheng Y, Yuan Y et al. Ann Surg 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33938492/
- **PUREAIR: perioperative (pre- and postoperative) pulmonary rehabilitation vs standard care for lung resection (single-centre RCT, Italy).** 6MWT at 6 months improved by 48.9 m vs a 7.5 m decline (difference 56.4 m, 95% CI 29.6–83.0), and the decline at 1 month was smaller. There were no differences in complications, length of stay or QoL. Tenconi S, Mainini C, Rapicetta C et al. Eur J Phys Rehabil Med 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34042410/
