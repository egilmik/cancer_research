## Practice-changing

- **US Preventive Services Task Force: Screening for lung cancer, recommendation statement (JAMA, March 2021; replaces the 2013 statement).** It is informed by a commissioned systematic review (7 RCTs, n=86,486; NLST lung cancer mortality IRR 0.85, NELSON IRR 0.75) and by modelling of 1,092 screening strategies. The USPSTF now recommends annual LDCT for adults aged 50–80 with ≥20 pack-years who smoke or quit within the past 15 years (B recommendation). The 2013 criteria were age 55–80 and ≥30 pack-years. Screening stops after 15 years without smoking, or when health limits life expectancy or the ability to have curative surgery. In the modelling, the new criteria raise the share of the population ever eligible from 14.1% to 20.6–23.6% and the lung cancer deaths averted from 381 to 469–558 per 100,000. The cost is more false positives, more overdiagnosis (83–94 vs 69 per 100,000) and more radiation-related deaths. It is a US recommendation that changes who is eligible in the US, not a Norwegian decision. The open questions are implementation, equity and uptake, which the editorials in this run discuss. US Preventive Services Task Force, Krist AH, Davidson KW et al. JAMA 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33687470/

## High

### lung-screening
- **USPSTF modelling study (JAMA): benefits and harms of 1,092 LDCT strategies in the 1950 and 1960 US birth cohorts, using 4 natural-history models.** The 2013 criteria were not efficient for the 1960 cohort. Annual strategies with a 20 pack-year minimum and start at age 50 or 55 increased deaths averted and life-years gained, at the cost of more false positives, overdiagnosis and radiation deaths. The study assumes full uptake and adherence. Meza R, Jeon J, Toumazis I et al. JAMA 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33687469/
- **USPSTF evidence report and systematic review (JAMA; 223 publications, 7 RCTs).** NLST: lung cancer mortality IRR 0.85 (95% CI 0.75–0.96), number needed to screen 323. NELSON: IRR 0.75 (0.61–0.90), number needed to screen 130. False positives led to 17 invasive procedures per 1,000 screened in NLST, and overdiagnosis estimates ranged widely (0–67%). Trial participants were healthier than the eligible US population. Jonas DE, Reuland DS, Reddy SM et al. JAMA 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33687468/
- **IFCT/SPLF/SIT statement on lung cancer screening (France).** The experts renew their recommendation for individual screening in France under defined conditions, and call for rapid funding of prospective studies towards an organised national programme. This is expert consensus, not a national decision. Couraud S, Ferretti G, Milleron B et al. Diagn Interv Imaging 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33648872/
- **China guideline for screening and early detection of lung cancer (2021, Beijing; National Cancer Center, commissioned by the National Health Commission).** It gives WHO-method recommendations on target population, technology and procedures. The abstract gives no eligibility criteria. He J, Li N, Chen WQ et al. Zhonghua Zhong Liu Za Zhi 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33752304/

### sclc / lung-nordic / lung-local-therapy
- **60 Gy vs 45 Gy twice-daily thoracic RT with platinum-etoposide in limited-stage SCLC (open-label randomised phase 2, n=170, 22 hospitals in Norway, Denmark and Sweden).** 2-year OS was 74.2% vs 48.1% (OR 3.09, 95% CI 1.62–5.89; p=0.0005), at a median follow-up of 49 months. Grade 3–4 neutropenia was 81% in both arms, and the abstract excerpt does not report other toxicities. This is a large survival signal from a Norwegian-led trial that directly bears on Nordic practice. As a randomised phase 2 with 2-year OS as the primary endpoint, it needs confirmation before 60 Gy becomes standard. Grønberg BH, Killingberg KT, Fløtten Ø et al. Lancet Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33662285/
- **CheckMate 451: nivolumab + ipilimumab, or nivolumab alone, vs placebo as maintenance after first-line chemotherapy in ED-SCLC (double-blind phase III, n=834).** OS did not improve with the combination (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.75–1.12; median 9.2 vs 9.6 months). Nivolumab alone gave OS HR 0.84 (0.69–1.02). PFS improved with both (HR 0.72 and 0.67). Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs were 52.2% with the combination. The trial is negative. Owonikoko TK, Park K, Govindan R et al. J Clin Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33683919/
- **FDA approval summary: atezolizumab (March 2019) and durvalumab (March 2020) with platinum-etoposide for first-line ES-SCLC.** The approvals rest on IMpower133 (OS HR 0.70; 12.3 vs 10.3 months) and CASPIAN (OS HR 0.73; 13.0 vs 10.3 months). This is a journal summary of earlier decisions. CASPIAN results were also posted on ClinicalTrials.gov this run (NCT03043872). Mathieu L, Shah S, Pai-Scherf L et al. Oncologist 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33687763/
- **PRoT-BM: PCI vs standard care in high-risk metastatic NSCLC (EGFR, ALK or high CEA) without progression after first-line therapy (randomised phase 2, n=84, Mexico).** 24-month brain metastasis incidence was 7% vs 38% (HR 0.12, 95% CI 0.035–0.42). OS was 64.5 vs 19.8 months (HR 0.41, 95% CI 0.22–0.78). The trial is small and single-country, and patients largely lacked access to CNS-penetrant third-generation TKIs, as the authors note. With this sample size, the large OS difference needs cautious interpretation. Neurocognitive outcomes are reported separately. Arrieta O, Maldonado F, Turcott JG et al. Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33640422/
- **WJTOG0105 10-year follow-up: mitomycin/vindesine/cisplatin vs irinotecan/carboplatin vs paclitaxel/carboplatin with 60 Gy thoracic RT in unresectable stage III NSCLC (phase 3, n=440).** 10-year OS was 13.6%, 7.5% and 15.2%, with no significant differences. Grade 3–4 late toxicity was 3–4%. These are long-term data from the pre-immunotherapy era that support paclitaxel/carboplatin CRT. Zenke Y, Tsuboi M, Chiba Y et al. JAMA Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33734289/

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-drivers / nsclc-resistance-nextgen
- **Furmonertinib (AST2818) in T790M-positive NSCLC (single-arm phase 2b, n=220, 46 Chinese centres).** ORR by central review was 74% (95% CI 68–80). Treatment-related grade ≥3 AEs occurred in 11%, with diarrhoea in 5% and rash in 7%, all grade 1–2. Median follow-up was 9.6 months, and PFS is not given in the abstract. This is a third-generation TKI used mainly in China. Shi Y, Hu X, Zhang S et al. Lancet Respir Med 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33780662/
- **Entrectinib in ROS1-positive NSCLC: updated integrated analysis of three phase I/II trials (n=161).** ORR was 67.1%, median duration of response 15.7 months and median PFS 15.7 months. Intracranial ORR was 79.2% in 24 patients with measurable CNS metastases. These are durable single-arm data, and there is no randomised comparison with crizotinib. Dziadziuszko R, Krebs MG, De Braud F et al. J Clin Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33646820/
- **Crizotinib in MET-amplified NSCLC (PROFILE 1001 cohort, n=38).** ORR was 38.1% with high amplification (MET/CEP7 ≥4), 14.3% with medium and 33.3% with low. Median PFS was 6.7, 1.9 and 1.8 months. There were no responses with concurrent KRAS, BRAF or EGFR mutations. Activity is confined to high-level amplification, and the subgroups are small. Camidge DR, Otterson GA, Clark JW et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33676017/
- **Sacituzumab govitecan, final IMMU-132-01 phase I/II basket results (safety population n=495).** ORR was 17.7% in SCLC (11/62) and 22.2% in endometrial cancer. Results for the NSCLC cohort are not given in this abstract. Bardia A, Messersmith WA, Kio EA et al. Ann Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33741442/
- **NCCN Guidelines Insights: NSCLC version 2.2021.** It summarises recent updates on targeted therapies, immunotherapies and biomarkers. The abstract does not name which recommendations changed. ### lung-key-evidence / lung-repurposed / lung-toxicity
- **TFINE (CTONG-0904): docetaxel continuation maintenance vs best supportive care after docetaxel-cisplatin (open-label phase III; 375 in the first randomisation, 184 in the second).** PFS improved with maintenance (5.8 vs 3.0 months; HR 0.57), but OS did not (12.3 vs 13.7 months). Docetaxel 60 mg/m² was as effective as 75 mg/m² with less diarrhoea. The trial is from the chemotherapy-only era, with no immunotherapy. Zhang L, Lu S, Cheng Y et al. Ann Transl Med 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33708965/
- **AMPLCaRe: adjuvant melatonin 20 mg nightly vs placebo for 1 year after NSCLC resection (RCT, n=709, 8 centres).** 2-year DFS did not differ (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.83–1.22), nor did 5-year DFS (HR 0.97). A subgroup with stage III/IV disease showed HR 0.75 for 5-year DFS, which is exploratory only. There was no effect on quality of life, symptoms or immune markers. The trial is negative. Seely D, Legacy M, Auer RC et al. EClinicalMedicine 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33681747/
- **Probiotics and anti-PD-1 monotherapy in NSCLC (retrospective, 3 Japanese centres, n=294, IPTW-adjusted).** Probiotic use was associated with longer PFS, higher disease control and higher response. OS was not significantly different. The study is retrospective, with likely confounding and healthy-user bias, and the authors call for prospective validation. Takada K, Shimokawa M, Takamori S et al. Int J Cancer 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33720422/
- **Nintedanib vs placebo as prophylaxis against radiation pneumonitis during chemoradiation in unresectable NSCLC (randomised double-blind phase 2, JTO letter).** There is no abstract, so the results cannot be summarised from this item. It is relevant as one of the few randomised pneumonitis-prophylaxis trials. Dy GK, Prasad D, Kumar P et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33641724/
