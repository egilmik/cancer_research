### nsclc-drivers

- **Sotorasib (960 mg or 240 mg) once daily in patients with previously treated KRAS G12C-mutated advanced NSCLC.** Randomised open-label phase 2 (CodeBreaK 100 dose comparison, n=209): sotorasib 960 mg vs 240 mg daily in previously treated KRAS G12C NSCLC gave ORR 32.7% vs 24.8%, median PFS 5.4 vs 5.6 months and OS 13.0 vs 11.7 months, with similar toxicity. Supports keeping 960 mg (an FDA post-marketing question); not powered for formal comparison. Hochmair MJ, Vermaelen K, Mountzios G et al. Eur J Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39029295/
- **Phase II study of brigatinib in patients with ROS1 fusion-positive non-small-cell lung cancer: the Barossa study.** Barossa single-arm phase II (Japan, n=47 ROS1+ NSCLC): brigatinib 180 mg gave confirmed ORR 71.4% (95% CI 51.3-86.8) in ROS1-TKI-naive patients and 31.6% (12.6-56.6) after crizotinib; median PFS 12.0 months in the TKI-naive cohort. Adds an option for ROS1+ NSCLC; small, single-arm. Niho S, Goto Y, Toyozawa R et al. ESMO Open 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39018589/

### lung-key-evidence / nsclc-immunotherapy

- **Robotic-assisted versus video-assisted lobectomy for resectable non-small-cell lung cancer: the RVlob randomized controlled trial.** RVlob single-centre randomised non-inferiority trial (Shanghai, n=320, median follow-up 58 months): robotic-assisted vs video-assisted lobectomy for resectable NSCLC gave 3-year OS 94.6% vs 91.5% (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.33-1.28), meeting the non-inferiority margin of -5%. Supports robotic lobectomy as an oncologically equivalent option; single centre, survival a co-primary endpoint with few events. Niu Z, Cao Y, Du M et al. EClinicalMedicine 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39105193/
- **Management of Central Airway Obstruction: An American College of Chest Physicians Clinical Practice Guideline.** ACCP clinical practice guideline on central airway obstruction (systematic review of 31 studies, modified Delphi): one good-practice statement and 10 graded recommendations, all on very-low-certainty evidence, supporting multimodality therapeutic bronchoscopy (rigid bronchoscopy, debridement, ablation, dilation, stenting) for malignant and non-malignant CAO. A new society guideline for a common complication of lung cancer. Mahmood K, Frazer-Green L, Gonzalez AV et al. Chest 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39029785/

### lung-screening / lung-nonpharm / lung-nordic

- **Patient- vs Physician-Initiated Response to Symptom Monitoring and Health-Related Quality of Life: The SYMPRO-Lung Cluster Randomized Trial.** SYMPRO-Lung multicentre stepped-wedge cluster randomised trial (Netherlands, n=515, stage I-IV lung cancer): weekly PRO-CTCAE symptom monitoring with alerts (to clinician or patient) improved the EORTC QLQ-C30 summary score vs usual care at 15 weeks (mean difference 5.22, 95% CI 2.72-7.73), 6 months (6.28, ES 0.40) and 1 year (3.97). Supports routine ePRO monitoring in lung cancer; open design and an imbalance in stage IV patients between arms. Billingy NE, van den Hurk CJG, N M F Tromp V et al. JAMA Netw Open 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39186274/
- **[It's finally here - BMUV-Guideline for lung cancer screening].** Rofo commentary (no abstract) on the German BMUV ordinance that permits LDCT lung cancer screening, a national screening-policy decision in Europe. The item itself gives no detail of eligibility criteria or start date, so it is reported as commentary on the decision rather than the decision itself. Barkhausen J. Rofo 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39146727/
- **Smartphone application versus written material for smoking reduction and cessation in individuals undergoing low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) screening for lung cancer: a phase II open-label randomised controlled trial.** Finnish randomised phase II trial (Oulu, n=201 heavy smokers undergoing LDCT screening): a smartphone cessation app vs written material raised self-reported smoking cessation at 3 months (19.8% vs 7.1%, OR 3.18, 95% CI 1.28-7.90) and 6 months (18.8% vs 7.1%, OR 2.85, 1.14-7.13). Relevant to integrating cessation support into a Nordic screening programme; single centre, self-reported quitting without biochemical verification, industry co-funded. Iivanainen S, Kurtti A, Wichmann V et al. Lancet Reg Health Eur 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39070744/
- **Evaluation of risk prediction models to select lung cancer screening participants in Europe: a prospective cohort consortium analysis.** Lung Cancer Cohort Consortium validation (Lancet Digit Health; 240,137 ever-smokers aged 45-80 in four prospective cohorts from nine European countries, including HUNT and EPIC-Norway): most of ten risk models (PLCOm2012, LCRAT, LCDRAT, Bach, HUNT, OWL, UCLD, UCLI) were reasonably calibrated with AUCs 0.68-0.83, while LLP v2 overpredicted risk and LLP v2/v3 discriminated less well. Thresholds matched to USPSTF-2021 and NELSON numbers were derived. Directly informs how a European (and Norwegian) programme could select participants; cohorts not screened, so it predicts incidence/mortality only. Feng X, Goodley P, Alcala K et al. Lancet Digit Health 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39179310/
- **Surveillance of subsolid nodules avoids unnecessary resections in lung cancer screening: long-term results of the prospective BioMILD trial.** BioMILD screening trial: conservative annual-LDCT surveillance of 1,085 subsolid nodules in 758 participants found cancer in 3.7% (non-solid) and 7.1% (part-solid), 87.5% stage I, no cancer deaths; supports surveillance over resection; single trial, observational subgroup. Balbi M, Sabia F, Ledda RE et al. ERJ Open Res 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39193379/
- **The Development and Performance of Alternative Criteria for Lung Cancer Screening.** Model-based US study: alternative screening eligibility (≥40 smoking-years, or age 60–80 with ≥40 pack-years) had higher sensitivity (91% vs 78%) and specificity (86% vs 84%) than USPSTF criteria for identifying high-benefit people; modelling only, not trial-validated. Kearney LE, Belancourt P, Katki HA et al. Ann Intern Med 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39159457/
- **The HUNT lung-SNP model: genetic variants plus clinical variables improve lung cancer risk assessment over clinical models.** Norwegian HUNT2 (n=30,749 ever-smokers) model adding 22 SNPs to the HUNT Lung Cancer Model improved risk ranking and detection rate (42 vs 48 screened per case), externally validated in Tromsø (n=2,663); relevant to Norwegian screening selection; few events (160 cancers in HUNT2), not prospectively tested. Nguyen OTD, Fotopoulos I, Nøst TH et al. J Cancer Res Clin Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39129029/
- **Educational disparities in cancer incidence, stage, and survival in Oslo.** Cancer Registry of Norway data 2013–2021 for Oslo: the low-education area had higher lung cancer incidence, more distant-stage diagnoses and lower 5-year relative survival overall; aggregated ecological district-level data, no individual adjustment. Al-Rammahy A, Fadum EA, Nilssen Y et al. Res Health Serv Reg 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39177868/

### lung-local-therapy / lung-toxicity

- **Stereotactic Ablative Radiation for Oligoprogressive Cancers: Results of the Randomized Phase 2 STOP Trial.** STOP randomised phase 2 (8 Canadian centres, n=90 with 1-5 oligoprogressive metastases, 44% lung primaries): SABR to all progressing lesions plus standard therapy vs standard therapy did not improve the primary endpoint PFS (median 8.4 vs 4.3 months, curves crossing, P=.91) or OS (31.2 vs 27.4 months, P=.22), but improved lesional control (70% vs 38%). Protocol adherence in the control arm was poor (35% received ablative therapy or withdrew). A negative trial for routine SABR in oligoprogression. Schellenberg D, Gabos Z, Duimering A et al. Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39168356/
