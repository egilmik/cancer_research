## Practice-changing

### ASCO living guideline: stage IV NSCLC without driver alterations (JCO, 2022)
This is the first living-guideline update of the 2020 ASCO and Ontario Health (CCO) recommendations, based on a rolling systematic review of RCTs from 2018 to 2021; five randomised trials form the new evidence base. New options for PS 0–1:
- With PD-L1 ≥50% (non-squamous or squamous), clinicians may offer single-agent atezolizumab.
- At every PD-L1 level, clinicians may offer nivolumab plus ipilimumab, alone or with chemotherapy (two cycles of platinum chemotherapy in squamous disease).
- After first-line chemo-immunotherapy in non-squamous disease, clinicians may offer second-line paclitaxel plus bevacizumab.
- Third-line options (pemetrexed, docetaxel, paclitaxel-bevacizumab) are specified.

All new options are "may offer" (weaker) recommendations. They widen the menu rather than replace pembrolizumab-based standards. The guideline adds no new efficacy data. In Norway, nivolumab plus ipilimumab regimens depend on Nye Metoder decisions. Singh N, Temin S, Baker S et al. J Clin Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816668/

### ASCO living guideline: stage IV NSCLC with driver alterations (JCO, 2022)
This updates the 2021 ASCO and Ontario Health (CCO) guideline using two new randomised studies (2020–2021). For untreated ALK-positive NSCLC at PS 0–2, clinicians *should* offer alectinib, brigatinib or lorlatinib; lorlatinib is added to the preferred first-line options. Ceritinib or crizotinib are now only for when those three are unavailable. For RET fusions, clinicians *may* offer selpercatinib or pralsetinib first line, or second line if not previously given. Limitations: the abstract gives no effect sizes; the RET recommendation is a weaker "may offer". Singh N, Temin S, Baker S et al. J Clin Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816666/

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen / nsclc-drivers
- **CHALLENGE: first-line icotinib for clinically diagnosed advanced lung cancer with plasma EGFR mutations but no pathology (single-arm phase 2, n=116, China, JAMA Oncol).** Patients were selected by ctDNA testing on three platforms. ORR was 52.6%, PFS 10.3 months and OS 23.2 months. It supports ctDNA-only treatment decisions when biopsy is impossible. It is single-arm with an older TKI. Xu J, Liu Z, Bai H et al. JAMA Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35862035/
- **Poziotinib in EGFR exon 20 NSCLC (phase II, n=50, Cancer Cell).** Confirmed ORR was 31–32% and PFS 5.5 months. Insertion location predicted activity: near-loop insertions (A767–P772) had ORR 46% vs 0% for far-loop. Resistance came through T790M, MET amplification and EMT. It is small. Elamin YY, Robichaux JP, Carter BW et al. Cancer Cell 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35820397/
- **POSITION20: osimertinib 160 mg in EGFR exon 20 NSCLC (single-arm phase II, n=25, Netherlands, Lung Cancer).** ORR was 28%, DoR 5.3 months, PFS 6.8 months and OS 15.2 months, with diarrhoea in 72%. Activity is modest. Zwierenga F, van Veggel B, Hendriks LEL et al. Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35777160/
- **Nazartinib first line in EGFR-mutant NSCLC (single-arm phase 2, n=45, Eur J Cancer).** BIRC ORR was 69% and PFS 18 months. In patients with brain metastases, ORR was 67% and PFS 17 months. It is single-arm. Tan DSW, Kim SW, Ponce Aix S et al. Eur J Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35810553/
- **SH-1028 (oritinib) in T790M-positive NSCLC (single-arm phase 2, n=286, China, JTO).** IRC ORR was 60.4% and PFS 12.6 months (part B). No ILD and little rash were reported. It is the post-first/second-generation T790M setting. Xiong A, Ren S, Liu H et al. J Thorac Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35798241/
- **BLU-945, a reversible wild-type-sparing EGFR inhibitor (discovery paper, J Med Chem).** It is potent against T790M and T790M/C797S, retains activity on L858R, and has entered phase 1/2 trials. The data are preclinical only. Eno MS, Brubaker JD, Campbell JE et al. J Med Chem 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35838760/
- **SAFIR02-Lung/IFCT 1301: genomics-guided maintenance in EGFR/ALK-wild-type NSCLC (randomised phase II, France, Clin Cancer Res).** Targeted therapy matched to genomic alterations did not improve PFS vs standard care (2.7 vs 2.7 months, HR 0.97; n=175). Durvalumab maintenance was not significant overall (HR 0.86; n=183) but appeared active with PD-L1 ≥1% (HR 0.29, n=29, subgroup). The trial is negative for genome-matched maintenance. Barlesi F, Tomasini P, Karimi M et al. Clin Cancer Res 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35802649/
- **J-ALEX final OS: alectinib vs crizotinib in ALK-positive NSCLC (phase III, n=207, Japan, ESMO Open).** OS did not differ (HR 1.03; 5-year OS 60.9% vs 64.1%), confounded by 78.8% crossover from crizotinib to alectinib. The prior PFS benefit (HR 0.37) stands, and alectinib remains a standard. Hotta K, Hida T, Nokihara H et al. ESMO Open 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35843080/
- **Lorlatinib for ROS1 NSCLC with CNS-only progression on crizotinib (phase 2, n=16, JTO Clin Res Rep).** Intracranial DCR at 12 weeks was 100% and intracranial ORR 87%, with intracranial PFS 38.8 months. Dose reductions were needed in 69%. It is small. Schneider JL, Muzikansky A, Lin JJ et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35815322/

### lung-key-evidence / sclc / lung-local-therapy / lung-toxicity
- **STOMP: olaparib maintenance after response to first-line therapy in SCLC (randomised double-blind phase II, n=220, UK, Lung Cancer).** PFS was 3.7 and 3.6 months on the two olaparib schedules vs 2.5 months on placebo (HR 0.76 and 0.86, not significant). There was no OS benefit, and PARP-inhibitor maintenance is not supported in unselected SCLC. Woll P, Gaunt P, Danson S et al. Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35872530/
- **Proton craniospinal irradiation vs photon involved-field RT for leptomeningeal metastases from NSCLC and breast cancer (randomised phase II, n=63, JCO).** CNS PFS was 7.5 vs 2.3 months (p<0.001) and OS 9.9 vs 6.0 months (p=0.029) at a planned interim analysis, with no excess grade 3–4 toxicity. It is single-centre, phase II, and the technique has limited availability. Yang JT, Wijetunga NA, Pentsova E et al. J Clin Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35802849/
- **ERAS plus pulmonary rehabilitation vs ERAS alone for VATS lung cancer surgery (multicentre pragmatic RCT, n=374, China, Thorax).** Postoperative pulmonary complications at 14 days were 18.7% vs 33.2% (HR 0.524, 95% CI 0.347–0.792). Drain time and length of stay did not differ. It supports integrating rehabilitation into ERAS, but it is open-label in a Chinese setting. Zheng Y, Mao M, Li F et al. Thorax 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35835552/
- **Durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy with baseline grade 1 radiation pneumonitis (prospective multicentre cohort, n=35, Japan, Lung Cancer).** Grade ≥2 pneumonitis occurred in 31% (one fatal), and PFS was 11.4 months. Durvalumab appears feasible in this group, but the risk is real. It is small and single-arm. Sugimoto T, Fujimoto D, Sato Y et al. Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35863254/
- **Chinese national guidelines for diagnosis and treatment of lung cancer, 2022 (National Health Commission, Chin J Cancer Res).** It is a national guideline. The abstract names no changed recommendation, so it is rated high. Health Commission of the PRC. Chin J Cancer Res 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35873889/

### lung-screening / lung-nonpharm
- **NCCN Guidelines Insights: Lung Cancer Screening version 1.2022 (JNCCN).** It summarises recent updates to selection criteria and nodule follow-up. - **Yorkshire Lung Screening Trial: participation (randomised invitation, 44,943 invited, Eur Respir J).** 50.8% responded to telephone risk assessment, and 86.8% of those eligible attended a community Lung Health Check with mobile CT. Current smoking (aOR 0.44) and deprivation (aOR 0.58) reduced response. It is a relevant implementation model for a Norwegian screening programme. Crosbie PAJ, Gabe R, Simmonds I et al. Eur Respir J 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35777775/

They give a basis for risk-based screening of never-smokers. Discrimination in never-smokers is modest. Wang F, Tan F, Shen S et al. Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35900139/
- **Intensive vs minimal telephone smoking cessation in the lung screening setting (NCI SCALE RCT, n=818, JNCI).** Three-month bio-verified abstinence was 9.1% vs 3.9% (OR 2.70), but the difference was not sustained at 6–12 months. Absolute quit rates were modest. Taylor KL, Williams RM, Li T et al. J Natl Cancer Inst 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35818122/
