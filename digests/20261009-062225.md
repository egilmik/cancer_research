## High

### lung-toxicity
- **Pneumonitis with combined thoracic RT and immunotherapy: multicentre prospective cohort.** 380 patients with lung cancer receiving thoracic RT were prospectively enrolled at 3 centres, and pneumonitis was graded by a multidisciplinary team. Over a median 28.2 months, 35.0% developed grade ≥2 pneumonitis. ICI exposure raised the risk (HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.20–2.39). Among 170 ICI-treated patients, the rate was 47.2% when ICIs came before RT and 39.5% when they came after. With ICI first, an ICI–RT interval under 21 days and higher lung V20 were risk factors; V20 mattered in both sequences. This is useful for planning consolidation and salvage RT after immunotherapy. The sequence was not randomised, so selection could explain part of the difference, and grade ≥3 rates are not given in the abstract. Xiao L, Li Q, Li Y et al. *J Natl Compr Canc Netw* 2026. [PMID 42805242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42805242/)
- **Pre-existing ILD and first-line ICI-based therapy: 15-centre Japanese cohort.** 1,340 patients with advanced or recurrent NSCLC; 158 (11.8%) had pre-existing ILD on centrally reviewed HRCT. ILD raised the risk of any-grade pneumonitis (adjusted sHR 2.64, 95% CI 1.91–3.65) and grade ≥3 pneumonitis (sHR 2.18, 1.37–3.47), and shortened OS (HR 1.36, 1.12–1.66). Grade ≥3 pneumonitis was followed by a higher risk of death (HR 2.25). Within ILD, a probable UIP/UIP pattern marked the worst group (pneumonitis sHR 2.18; OS HR 2.88). This supports a pretreatment HRCT review of ILD pattern before choosing ICI-based therapy. It is retrospective, and patients with ILD who were not offered ICIs are not captured. Matsumoto K, Yamamoto Y, Shiroyama T et al. *Eur J Cancer* 2026. [PMID 42349343](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42349343/)
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis and postoperative pneumonia after lung resection.** Prospective, double-blind comparison of cefazolin vs amoxicillin-clavulanic acid in 200 patients undergoing lung resection for primary lung malignancy, with preoperative bronchial colonisation assessed. Postoperative pneumonia occurred in 23.5% overall: 21.5% with cefazolin vs 25.8% with amoxicillin-clavulanate (not significant). Rates did not differ by colonisation status (22.7–25.0%), and 30-day mortality was 0.5%. The authors conclude cefazolin is appropriate and routine colonisation screening may be unnecessary. It is a single-centre study, the abstract does not say whether allocation was randomised, and it was not powered for equivalence; few patients carried potentially pathogenic organisms. Petrovic S, Beovic B, Tomic V et al. *Ann Med Surg (Lond)* 2026. [PMID 42583275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42583275/)

### lung-repurposed
- **NSAIDs vs acetaminophen during ICI therapy: Hong Kong region-wide cohort.** CDARS data covering all Hong Kong public hospitals: 560 patients with newly diagnosed NSCLC who received ≥2 ICI cycles (2018–2020) with a concomitant non-opioid analgesic (419 acetaminophen, 141 NSAIDs). Long-term NSAID/aspirin users and patients with inflammatory disease were excluded. After IPTW, median OS was 15.5 vs 10.5 months with NSAIDs vs acetaminophen (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46–0.98), over a median follow-up of 37.8 months. The difference was concentrated in ICI without chemotherapy (HR 0.61, 0.41–0.92); with chemotherapy it was not significant and pointed the other way (HR 1.50, 0.88–2.53). The active comparator reduces confounding by indication. However, exposure was counted up to 21 days after the last ICI dose, so patients treated longer had more chance of exposure, which is a form of immortal-time bias. Subgroups are small. A short communication; the abstract was missing, and details were taken from the PMC full text. Wang SC, Ye ZH, Zhou H et al. *J Natl Cancer Cent* 2026. [PMID 42007217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42007217/)
- **PPIs during ICI therapy: active-comparator design changes the answer.** Japanese claims cohort of 6,360 patients with advanced NSCLC starting first-line ICI-based therapy who received a PPI (5,409) or an H2 blocker (951) within 30 days before or at the start. With overlap weighting, 3-year mortality did not clearly differ between PPI and H2RA users (HR 1.06, 95% CI 0.93–1.20). Compared with patients on neither drug, PPI users had higher mortality (HR 1.13, 1.05–1.20). This suggests much of the reported PPI–ICI harm reflects the underlying need for acid suppression rather than the PPI itself. Claims data, all-cause mortality only, no PD-L1 or performance status. Ihara Y, Okada N, Mizutani M et al. *Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf* 2026. [PMID 42736173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42736173/)
- **Antibiotics, probiotics and immunotherapy outcomes: multicentre Chinese cohort.** 524 driver-negative NSCLC patients on immunotherapy; fecal samples from 22. Antibiotics alone were linked to poorer PFS and OS. Patients on antibiotics who also took probiotics, for any duration, had PFS and OS similar to those who took neither. In antibiotic-naive patients, probiotics for more than 15 days were linked to better PFS. Bifidobacterium and Roseburia enrichment went with better PFS. This bears on whether probiotics should be given with antibiotics during ICI therapy, but it is retrospective, and duration-based exposure groups invite immortal-time bias. Effect sizes are not given in the abstract, and probiotic products varied. Luo WC, Huang ZJ, Mei SQ et al. *J Transl Med* 2026. [PMID 42432744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432744/)
- **Faecal microbiota transplantation with anti-PD-1 in PD-L1-negative NSCLC.** A single-arm trial in advanced PD-L1-negative NSCLC reported that FMT plus anti-PD-1 improved efficacy and PFS. A strain-tracking analysis of over 2,000 metagenomes found that strains within the same species can have opposite effects, and that engraftment of particular beneficial strains correlated with clinical benefit; 38 candidate species were identified for next-generation products. This is the first FMT clinical signal in NSCLC in this monitor. The abstract gives neither the number of patients nor PFS figures, and without a control arm "improved" rests on historical comparison. Chen K, Liu Y, Rong J et al. *Cell Host Microbe* 2026. [PMID 41747725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41747725/)
- **Denosumab and survival in lung cancer with bone metastases: TriNetX cohort.** International EHR network; 52,521 eligible patients, 2,735 propensity-matched pairs with or without denosumab. Two-year mortality was lower with denosumab (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.69–0.80; median survival 467 vs 292 days), as were skeletal-related events (HR 0.42, 0.33–0.53). The SRE reduction is consistent with trial data. The mortality benefit is much larger than randomised evidence supports and is likely inflated by immortal-time bias (patients must live long enough to start denosumab) and by healthier patients being more often treated. Shiau BW, Hsu WH, Wei YF et al. *Clin Oncol (R Coll Radiol)* 2026. [PMID 41707577](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41707577/)

### lung-nonpharm
- **Current smoking and outcomes after lung cancer resection: STS database.** 85,124 resections (2018–2023) in the Society of Thoracic Surgeons General Thoracic Surgery Database; 28% were current smokers (within 1 month of surgery). Pulmonary complications were more frequent in current smokers (34.6% vs 30.5%), and current smoking independently predicted them, but operative/30-day mortality was the same (1.0% vs 1.0%). The authors conclude that smoking status alone should not decide whether a patient is a candidate for resection. Large and recent, but retrospective, with current smokers younger and healthier, and with no data on cessation support or long-term survival. Kim H, Viana SW, Pinkston C et al. *J Am Coll Surg* 2026. [PMID 42047383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42047383/)
