## Practice-changing

- **FLAURA2: first-line osimertinib with or without platinum-pemetrexed in EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC (open-label randomised phase 3, NEJM 2023).**
  - **Design:** 557 untreated patients with exon 19 deletion or L858R were randomised 1:1 to osimertinib plus pemetrexed and cisplatin or carboplatin, or osimertinib alone. The comparator, osimertinib monotherapy, was the first-line standard.
  - **Primary endpoint (investigator PFS):** HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.49–0.79, P < .001). 24-month PFS was 57% vs 41%, and BICR PFS was consistent (HR 0.62, 0.48–0.80). ORR was 83% vs 76%, and median response duration 24.0 vs 15.3 months.
  - **Toxicity:** Grade ≥3 adverse events were more frequent with the combination, driven by known chemotherapy toxicities.
  - **Still open:** OS was immature in this report; the final OS (HR 0.77) is already in the index from a later run. The trade-off is more toxicity and infusion burden for longer PFS. EU approval and Norwegian reimbursement status are not covered by this item.
  - Planchard D, Jänne PA, Cheng Y et al. N Engl J Med 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37937763/
- **American Cancer Society 2023 lung cancer screening guideline update (CA Cancer J Clin 2024).**
  - **What changed:** Annual LDCT is now recommended for asymptomatic people aged 50–80 who currently or formerly smoked, with ≥20 pack-years (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence). Years since quitting is no longer a criterion to start or stop screening. The 2013 ACS guideline covered ages 55–74 with ≥30 pack-years and quitting within 15 years.
  - **Other recommendations:** Shared decision-making should come before screening. People who smoke should get cessation counselling. People with comorbidity that substantially limits life expectancy should not be screened.
  - **Evidence:** It rests on the USPSTF 2021 systematic review, a new review of risk by years since quitting, CISNET modelling and benefit-to-radiation-risk analyses. The companion papers in this run (PLCO/NLST modelling, the years-since-quitting review and the radiation-risk analysis) are rated high or medium.
  - **Still open:** It is a US guideline. Norway had no national screening programme at the time, so it is relevant mainly as a reference for eligibility criteria.
  - Wolf AMD, Oeffinger KC, Shih TY et al. CA Cancer J Clin 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37909877/
- **IND.227/KEYNOTE-483: pembrolizumab plus platinum-pemetrexed in untreated advanced pleural mesothelioma (open-label randomised phase 3, Lancet 2023).**
  - **Design:** 440 patients at 51 hospitals in Canada, Italy and France were randomised 1:1 to up to 6 cycles of cisplatin or carboplatin with pemetrexed, with or without pembrolizumab (up to 2 years).
  - **Primary endpoint (OS):** Median OS was 17.3 vs 16.1 months (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.64–0.98, P = .0324), and 3-year OS 25% vs 17%. Median follow-up was 16.2 months.
  - **Toxicity:** Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs occurred in 27% vs 15%, and hospital admission for related SAEs in 18% vs 6%.
  - **Still open:**
    - The absolute median gain is about 1 month.
    - The trial gives no head-to-head comparison with nivolumab-ipilimumab (CheckMate 743), the other first-line immunotherapy option.
    - EU approval and Norwegian reimbursement status are not covered by this item.
  - Chu Q, Perrone F, Greillier L et al. Lancet 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37931632/

CheckMate 227 Part 2 and SKYSCRAPER-02 are rated high but are negative trials. PERLA is a randomised phase 2.

## High

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen / nsclc-drivers

- **UNICORN (single-arm phase 2, n=40, JAMA Oncol).** First-line osimertinib in uncommon EGFR mutations (G719X 50%, S768I 25%, L861Q 20%; exon 20 insertions excluded) gave ORR 55.0% (90% CI 40.9–68.5) and DCR 90%. Median PFS was 23.0 months for solitary mutations. Grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 27.5%, and ILD in 12.5%. It is small and nonrandomised, but it supports osimertinib as an option for these mutations. Okuma Y, Kubota K, Shimokawa M et al. JAMA Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37991747/
- **Dacomitinib in EGFR-mutant NSCLC with untreated brain metastases (single-arm phase II, n=30, ESMO Open).** Confirmed intracranial ORR was 96.7%, with intracranial CR 63.3%, 12-month iPFS 78.6% and median PFS 17.5 months. Grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 16.7%, and 83.3% needed dose reduction. It is small and from a single country, with no comparison with osimertinib. Jung HA, Park S, Lee SH et al. ESMO Open 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38016250/
- **EGFR C797X allelic context after osimertinib (multicentre cohort, n=365, J Thorac Oncol).** Four subtypes were defined: in cis 75.3%, in trans 6.4%, cis&trans 10.4% and C797X-only 7.9%. The cis&trans subtype had the worst outcomes (PFS on subsequent treatment 1.0 month, OS 3.9 months). In-cis tumours did best with brigatinib plus cetuximab (median PFS 11.0 months). It is retrospective and Chinese-only, and treatments were heterogeneous. Lu C, Wei XW, Wang Z et al. J Thorac Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37981218/
- **Osimertinib resistance on paired tissue and plasma (prospective single-centre, n=65, Br J Cancer).** Of 25 patients with post-progression samples, 80% had an identified mechanism: MET amplification (8), C797S (3) and SCLC transformation (2). Tissue–plasma concordance for resistance mechanisms was only 22.7%, which argues for tissue rebiopsy when feasible. The progression cohort is small. Leonetti A, Verzè M, Minari R et al. Br J Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37938348/
- **VISION Cohort B: tepotinib in high-level MET amplification detected by liquid biopsy (phase 2, n=24, Cell Rep Med).** ORR was 41.7% (95% CI 22.1–63.4) and median DoR 14.3 months. Oedema occurred in 58.3%. The cohort was halted before its planned 60 patients, so the estimates are imprecise. Le X, Paz-Ares LG, Van Meerbeeck J et al. Cell Rep Med 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37944528/
- **FLAURA longitudinal ctDNA modelling (exploratory post hoc, n=320, Clin Pharmacol Ther).** A Bayesian joint model of EGFR-mutant ctDNA dynamics predicted PFS in a validation set (17.7 vs 9.1 months, osimertinib vs comparator), close to the observed RECIST PFS. It is a modelling paper, not a ctDNA-guided intervention. Johnson M, Serra Traynor C, Vishwanathan K et al. Clin Pharmacol Ther 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38010260/
- **Sunvozertinib: First Approval (Drugs).** This profiles the conditional approval in China (August 2023) for EGFR exon 20 insertion NSCLC after platinum. It rests on ORR and DoR from a single-arm phase 2, with confirmatory phase 3 trials ongoing. Dhillon S. Drugs 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37962831/
- **Iruplinalkib: First Approval (Drugs).** This profiles the June 2023 approval in China of the ALK/ROS1 TKI for ALK-positive NSCLC after crizotinib. Keam SJ. Drugs 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38008856/

### lung-key-evidence / lung-top-journals / nsclc-immunotherapy

- **CheckMate 227 Part 2 (phase III, n=755, ESMO Open).** First-line nivolumab plus chemotherapy vs chemotherapy did not improve OS in nonsquamous NSCLC, the primary endpoint (18.8 vs 15.6 months, HR 0.86, 95.62% CI 0.69–1.08). Descriptive OS in all randomised patients was HR 0.81, and in squamous disease HR 0.69 (exploratory). It is negative for its primary endpoint. Borghaei H, O'Byrne KJ, Paz-Ares L et al. ESMO Open 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37988950/
- **PERLA (randomised double-blind phase II, n=243, Nat Commun).** Dostarlimab vs pembrolizumab, each with pemetrexed-platinum, first line in non-squamous NSCLC gave ORR 45% vs 39%, PFS HR 0.70 (0.50–0.98) and OS HR 0.75 (0.53–1.05). It is phase II and not powered for superiority, so it shows similar activity of two PD-1 antibodies. Lim SM, Peters S, Ortega Granados AL et al. Nat Commun 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37951954/
- **SPRINT (phase 2, n=25, J Clin Oncol).** In stage III NSCLC with PD-L1 ≥50%, induction pembrolizumab, then risk-adapted 20-fraction thoracic RT without chemotherapy, then pembrolizumab to 1 year gave 1-year PFS 76% and 2-year OS 76%. It is small and single-arm, a chemotherapy-free hypothesis for frail patients. Ohri N, Jolly S, Cooper BT et al. J Clin Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37988638/
- **Chinese expert consensus on RNA-based NGS for fusion detection in NSCLC (Zhongguo Fei Ai Za Zhi).** It sets out application scenarios and quality control for RNA-NGS alongside DNA-NGS. It is a national consensus and does not change treatment. Society of Cancer Precision Medicine of the Chinese Anti-Cancer Association et al. Zhongguo Fei Ai Za Zhi 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37985137/

### sclc

- **SKYSCRAPER-02 (phase III, n=490, J Clin Oncol).** Adding tiragolumab (anti-TIGIT) to atezolizumab-carboplatin-etoposide in ES-SCLC did not improve PFS (HR 1.11; 5.4 vs 5.6 months) or OS (13.1 months in both arms, HR 1.14). It is negative, a further setback for TIGIT. Rudin CM, Liu SV, Soo RA et al. J Clin Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37976444/
- **Danish randomised phase III, platinum-etoposide vs platinum-topotecan first line in ES-SCLC (Acta Oncol, Nordic).** PubMed has no abstract, so the results cannot be summarised here. The chemotherapy-only design predates chemo-immunotherapy. Mau-Sørensen M, Gerner-Rasmussen J, Hansen O et al. Acta Oncol 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37934081/

### lung-ctdna-mrd

- **ctDNA plus CT tumour volume after resection of early-stage NSCLC (n=85 plus 32 validation, Ann Oncol).** Detectable ctDNA at baseline or after treatment, and failure to clear it, were associated with worse outcome. Adding 3D tumour volume improved risk stratification. The cohort is small and observational, and the findings do not yet guide adjuvant decisions. Tran HT, Heeke S, Sujit S et al. Ann Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37992871/

### lung-screening / lung-nonpharm / lung-nordic

- **I-ELCAP 20-year follow-up (prospective cohort, 89,404 screened, Radiology).** Of 1,257 screen-detected first primary lung cancers, 81% were clinical stage I. 20-year lung cancer-specific survival was 81% (95% CI 78–83), and 95% for pathological T1aN0M0. It has no unscreened control, so lead-time and overdiagnosis bias cannot be excluded. Henschke CI, Yip R, Shaham D et al. Radiology 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37934099/
- **HUNCHEST-II final results (Hungarian national LDCT programme, n=4,215, Eur Radiol).** 1.8% were diagnosed with lung cancer, 60.7% at stage I and 16.4% at stage IV, a shift compared with unscreened practice cohorts. The positive predictive value was 58%. It has no mortality endpoint. Kerpel-Fronius A, Megyesfalvi Z, Markóczy Z et al. Eur Radiol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37921926/
- **Risk beyond 15 quit-years (PLCO/NLST/NHIS modelling, Cancer).** Absolute lung cancer risk rose 8.7%/year after 15 quit-years. Removing the quit-year criterion would make 4.9 million more Americans eligible and raise the share of preventable deaths prevented from 63.7% to 74.2%. It is the modelling basis for the ACS update. Landy R, Cheung LC, Young CD et al. Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37909885/
- **Lung cancer beyond 15 years since quitting (systematic review, CA Cancer J Clin).** Moderate evidence shows that risk declines gradually without a threshold at 15 years and may stay elevated for 2–3 decades compared with never-smokers. It is the evidence review behind the ACS update. Kondo KK, Rahman B, Ayers CK et al. CA Cancer J Clin 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37909870/
- **Norwegian cohort of 358,551 people aged 40–43 (followed 1985–2018, Tob Control).** Quitters who had smoked >20 years had lung cancer mortality HR 6.77 (men) and 5.75 (women) vs never-smokers, while all-cause mortality among quitters approached that of never-smokers. Smoking is self-reported once at baseline. It is relevant for Norwegian screening-eligibility debates. Tverdal A, Selmer R, Thelle DS. Tob Control 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37963773/
- **Personalised tobacco treatment in screening-eligible smokers (two-phase RCT, n=188, J Thorac Oncol).** Gain-framed messaging added to counselling plus nicotine patch did not improve 8-week verified quit rates (16.1% vs 16.8%). Biomarker feedback was tested for cigarette reduction at 6 months. It is negative for cessation. Cartmel B, Fucito LM, Bold KW et al. J Thorac Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37977486/
- **Preoperative home-based exercise before lung cancer surgery (multicentre RCT, n=41, Ann Surg Oncol).** Global QoL was better before (MD 13.5 points) and 1 month after surgery (MD 12.4, 95% CI 1.3–23.4). Clinical QoL deterioration occurred in 30% vs 71.4%. It is small and short-term, but a cheap intervention. Machado P, Pimenta S, Garcia AL et al. Ann Surg Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37934383/
