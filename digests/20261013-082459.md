### Guidelines and consensus statements

- **ESR Essentials: practice recommendations for LDCT lung cancer screening (ESTI).** It recommends offering screening at age 50–75 with at least 20 pack-years, keeping the effective dose below 1 mSv, using deep-learning volumetry for nodule detection and growth, and short-term follow-up at 3–6 months for solid nodules of 100–500 mm³. Most subsolid nodules should be watched rather than treated, and reporting of incidental findings should be limited. These are expert practice recommendations relevant to Nordic screening pilots, not new trial evidence. Revel MP, Biederer J, Nair A et al. Eur Radiol 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40848139/
- **French (SFRO-affiliated) recommendations on preventing and managing radiation-induced lung injury.** They cover pneumonitis, fibrosis, organising pneumonia and radiation recall. Risk factors are dosimetric, patient- and tumour-related, and new challenges include SBRT, combinations with TKIs and immunotherapy, and pre-existing ILD. The abstract describes scope rather than specific new recommendations. Cravéreau O, Bourbonne V, Vaugier L et al. Cancer Radiother 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40774102/
- **SITC guideline addendum 1: immunotherapy for lung cancer and mesothelioma.** It updates the Society for Immunotherapy of Cancer clinical practice guideline. No abstract text is available, so this run cannot say which recommendations changed. Check the full text before relying on it. J Immunother Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40681175/
- **Clinical practice guideline on interventions to improve function in lung cancer (54 studies).** Combined aerobic plus resistance and/or breathing exercise is recommended to improve mobility during prehabilitation and during and immediately after treatment. Multimodal exercise plus education may improve function during treatment. Evidence is moderate and concentrated on mobility in early phases; durability of benefit is unclear. Vargo M, Gerber LH, Gilchrist LS et al. Cancer Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40613665/
- **ESTI nodule-management recommendation defining positive, indeterminate and negative LDCT screening results.** Solid nodules are assessed mainly by volumetric growth and subsolid nodules by the size of the solid component. The stated goals are fewer follow-up scans without a major stage shift and less overtreatment. A companion paper (medium) gives the rationale for the follow-up intervals. It is a consensus recommendation; the abstract gives no performance data. Snoeckx A, Silva M, Prosch H et al. Eur Radiol 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593170/

### lung-key-evidence (trials and pooled analyses)

- **G-oncoCOACH RCT: geriatric assessment with extensive patient coaching in older adults with cancer (n=217, 19% lung cancer).** At 6 months, global health status QoL was 10.9 points higher with the intervention (95% CI 3.7–18.0), and more geriatric recommendations were implemented (65% vs 45%). It was a single-country trial of mixed tumour types, so the lung-specific effect is not reported. Kenis C, Peeters L, Laethem L et al. Lancet Healthy Longev 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40818480/
- **RCT of a novel airway balloon cryoablation system vs a conventional CO2 cryoprobe for malignant central airway obstruction (n=198).** Airway patency at 6 weeks was 78.5% vs 60.9%, a difference of 17.6 percentage points (95% CI 4.4–30.8), which met non-inferiority and then superiority. Procedures were shorter and bleeding rates were similar. It is a device trial from China; symptom scores did not differ between arms. Liu J, Li C, Gu Y et al. Thorax 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40796274/
- **MORPHEUS-Lung (randomised phase 1b/2, interim analysis): atezolizumab plus bevacizumab ± SBRT vs docetaxel in non-squamous NSCLC after platinum and PD-(L)1 therapy (n=121).** Confirmed ORR was 16.7% with SBRT, 20.0% without SBRT and 12.8% with docetaxel. Grade ≥3 AEs occurred in 47.6%, 45.0% and 64.1%. There was no clear signal that adding SBRT helped, and the arms are too small for firm comparisons. Ghiringhelli F, Lawrence YR, Felip E et al. J Immunother Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40759444/
- **Phase 3 placebo-controlled RCT of 12 weeks of dexamethasone for fatigue during palliative chemotherapy (n=126 randomised, 101 evaluable; 16% lung cancer).** Fatigue scores at 6 and 12 weeks did not differ. Placebo had better role functioning, and dexamethasone caused more constipation. This is a negative trial that argues against routine prolonged steroids for fatigue; mixed tumour types and dropout limit precision. Radhakrishnan V, Gopalakrishnan V, Vihari GV et al. Support Care Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40739403/

### nsclc-drivers

- **CodeBreaK 200 exploratory CNS analysis: sotorasib vs docetaxel in pretreated KRAS G12C NSCLC.** Among 69 patients with baseline treated CNS metastases, CNS PFS was 9.6 vs 4.5 months (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.20–0.92). CNS shrinkage of ≥30% occurred in 33% vs 15% of those with lesions ≥10 mm. It is a post hoc subgroup analysis of a trial with only modest overall PFS benefit and no OS benefit, so it is hypothesis-supporting only. Dingemans AC, Syrigos K, Livi L et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40774040/

### sclc

- **PAVE (phase 2, Hellenic Cooperative Oncology Group): intercalated avelumab with platinum chemotherapy in ES-SCLC (n=55).** ORR was 69.1%, median PFS 5.8 months and median OS 10.3 months, with grade 3–4 AEs in 56%. QoL improved, and liver metastases and RB1/TP53 co-mutations predicted poor outcome. Survival is not better than approved chemo-immunotherapy regimens; it is single-arm with short follow-up. Linardou H, Papadopoulou K, Korfiatis N et al. Eur J Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40857837/
- **Single-arm phase 2: camrelizumab plus carboplatin and nab-paclitaxel first line in ES-SCLC (n=60).** The 6-month PFS rate was 52.2%, meeting the primary endpoint; median PFS was 7.1 months, median OS 18.1 months and ORR 73.3%. Exploratory multiomics linked MUC17 alterations and high NEUROG1 to shorter survival. It is single-arm and from China, and it uses a non-standard chemotherapy backbone. Yu J, Cai D, Zhao S et al. MedComm (2020) 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40727251/

### nsclc-immunotherapy

- **TD-NeoFOUR phase 2, 3-year follow-up: neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy plus anti-angiogenic therapy in resectable NSCLC (n=45).** The 3-year EFS was 84.3% and 3-year OS 86.7%, better in patients with pCR/MPR, and no new late toxicities emerged. It is a small single-arm study without a comparator, so the added value of the anti-angiogenic drug is unproven. Gao Z, Mao Y, Sun Y et al. Thorac Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40842286/
- **CANOPY-N (randomised phase 2): neoadjuvant canakinumab, pembrolizumab or both in resectable NSCLC (n=88).** MPR was 2.9% with canakinumab alone, 17.1% with the combination and 16.7% with pembrolizumab alone. Adding the IL-1β antibody did not improve on pembrolizumab, and 86% had surgery within the planned 6-week window. This is a negative result for canakinumab; two cycles of IO without chemotherapy is not current standard. Lee JM, Pujol JL, Zhang J et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40810132/
- **NEOMUN (phase 2): two cycles of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab alone in resectable NSCLC (n=29).** The resection rate was 100%, MPR 24.1% and pCR 13.8%, and response rose with PD-L1 TPS and TMB ≥10. Both DFS (75.9%) and OS (82.7%) were the same at 2 and 3 years. It is small and single-arm; IO monotherapy is below what perioperative chemo-immunotherapy achieves. Eichhorn ME, Niedermaier B, Charoentong P et al. J Immunother Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40759442/
- **Phase 2, two-arm: neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus chemotherapy, or camrelizumab plus apatinib in PD-L1-positive patients, for resectable stage IIA–IIIA NSCLC.** MPR was 25.0% with chemo-IO and 60.0% with camrelizumab-apatinib (95% CI 26–88), and 24-month EFS was 53.6% vs 70.0%. Grade ≥3 TRAEs were 25% vs 10%. The apatinib arm is very small (wide CIs), and the arms are not randomised against each other. Ji S, Sheng Z, Bian D et al. BMC Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40682106/

### lung-nonpharm / lung-toxicity

- **RCT of patient navigation with symptom monitoring in advanced lung cancer, India (n=150).** Broad-panel molecular testing rose (44% vs 10%), time to treatment was shorter (19 vs 28 days), unplanned visits were fewer (67% vs 88%) and compliance was higher. QoL, PFS and OS did not differ. It improves care processes, not outcomes; it was run in India, a resource-limited setting. Prabhash K, Noronha V, Patil V et al. Eur J Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40850317/
- **TD-WELLBEING RCT: ePRO-based collaborative symptom management after lung cancer surgery (n=355).** At 12 weeks, symptom scores were lower (adjusted difference −0.53, 95% CI −0.79 to −0.27) and global health QoL was higher (+6.8 points), with effects beyond 6 months. It is a single-country (China) trial; the size of the effect is modest. Jing P, Liang Y, Tan Z et al. Br J Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40775446/
- **Take a Breath pilot RCT: cognitive-behavioural treatment for breathlessness in lung cancer (n=45).** Five weekly sessions improved dyspnoea-related functioning (d=0.82) and dyspnoea anxiety (d=0.87) vs standard care, with gains in depression and QoL. Accrual was only 25.6% and 60% completed all sessions. It is a feasibility-stage trial needing a full-scale RCT. Lo SB, Arrato NA, Presley CJ et al. Ann Am Thorac Soc 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40772929/
- **PRE-HIIT RCT: preoperative high-intensity interval training before lung or oesophageal cancer surgery.** In a mostly very deconditioned cohort, VO2 peak rose from 18.7 to 21.7 ml/kg/min with HIIT vs no change with standard care (between-group difference 6.6%, p=0.05), and sit-to-stand improved. It is a fitness endpoint only; postoperative complications were not the primary outcome. Smyth E, O'Neill LM, Kearney N et al. Ann Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40767629/
- **RCT of a digital smoking-cessation and screening intervention vs brief intervention in high-risk adults.** Biochemically verified 7-day abstinence was 35.1% vs 15.4% at 3 months and 24.3% vs 10.3% at 6 months, and screening uptake at 6 months was 58.1% vs 29.5%. It is a US trial in a limited population; long-term abstinence is unknown. Cooley ME, Kim SS, Xiong N et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40669310/

### lung-screening

- **Heterogeneity of screening effectiveness in NELSON (n=14,808) and NLST (n=53,405) individual data.** Mortality reduction varied by pack-years, smoking status and sex, mainly because histology differed: benefit was larger for adenocarcinoma and other types than for SCLC, and squamous results conflicted between trials. The authors suggest relaxing smoking-based eligibility might increase effectiveness. It is a secondary analysis with wide subgroup CIs and is relevant to eligibility debates. Welz M, van der Aalst CM, Alfons A et al. Nat Commun 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40877276/
- **Radiologist's perspective on the Australian National Lung Cancer Screening Program, launched 1 July 2025.** It describes the radiology workflow, reporting and quality requirements of a new national programme. It is useful as an implementation model for Nordic programmes; it is a narrative perspective with no outcome data. Ellis SJ, McCusker MW, Melsom S et al. J Med Imaging Radiat Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40817706/
- **CISNET modelling of screening intervals for moderate-risk people.** Biennial screening at age 50–60, then annual, preserved 95.9% of lung cancer deaths prevented compared with annual screening, using 20.6% fewer CT scans. Annual screening from 50 to 80 was not cost-effective at $100,000/QALY. All cost-effective strategies started biennially and switched to annual screening at age 60 or 30–40 pack-years. It is model-based (US costs), but directly relevant to designing resource-limited programmes. de Nijs K, de Koning HJ, Cao P et al. JAMA Netw Open 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40705331/

### lung-local-therapy

- **DUART (phase 2): durvalumab after radiotherapy in unresectable stage III NSCLC ineligible for chemotherapy (n=102, median age 79).** Grade 3/4 possibly related AEs within 6 months occurred in 9.8%, median PFS was 9.2 months and median OS 21.1 months. ctDNA positivity on treatment predicted shorter PFS. It is single-arm, supporting durvalumab after sequential or RT-alone approaches in frail patients. Filippi AR, García Campelo MR, Paoli JB et al. ESMO Open 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40840233/
- **Single-arm phase 2: consolidative SRT to oligo-residual disease after first-line chemo-immunotherapy in driver-negative metastatic NSCLC (n=59; PLoS Med).** Median PFS was 29.0 months, meeting the primary endpoint, and 2-year OS 88.9%; grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 22%. A propensity-matched comparison with systemic therapy alone gave adjusted PFS HR 0.29 and OS HR 0.23. It is single-centre (China, 95% male) and non-randomised, so randomised trials are needed. Chen H, Yu L, Liang F et al. PLoS Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40748918/
- **FORCE (phase 2): palliative RT (5×4 Gy) added to nivolumab in pretreated non-squamous NSCLC (n=41, with 60 parallel controls).** ORR was 8.3%, so the primary endpoint was not met; parallel controls had 23.8%, but they had better baseline condition. Outcomes tracked performance status and PD-L1, not RT. It is a negative result for RT-induced immune priming in this setting. Bozorgmehr F, Chung I, Fischer JR et al. Clin Exp Metastasis 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40702361/
- **PEMBRO-RT translational analysis (Nat Cancer): SBRT before pembrolizumab in metastatic NSCLC.** Patients with immunologically cold tumours (low TMB, PD-L1 null or Wnt mutations) had longer PFS with SBRT. Interferon and antigen-presentation programmes rose in non-irradiated sites, and new T-cell clones expanded in tumour and blood. It is hypothesis-generating biomarker work from a randomised phase 2 trial of modest size. Huang J, Theelen WSME, Belcaid Z et al. Nat Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40696153/
- **LUN16-081 (randomised phase 2): consolidation nivolumab vs nivolumab plus ipilimumab after chemoradiation in stage IIIA/IIIB NSCLC (n=105).** The 18-month PFS was 65.5% vs 66.3%, both compared against historical controls at p<0.1. Grade ≥3 TRAEs were 18.5% vs 29.4%. Adding ipilimumab increased toxicity without clear benefit, and the trial was not powered to compare the arms. Durm G, Mamdani H, Althouse S et al. J Immunother Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40664454/
- **COAST update (randomised phase 2): durvalumab alone or with oleclumab or monalizumab after chemoradiation (n=189).** Confirmed ORR was 35.0% with oleclumab and 40.3% with monalizumab vs 23.9% alone, but the CIs for the differences crossed zero after 30 months of follow-up. Phase 3 PACIFIC-9 is testing these combinations; this update does not change practice. Aggarwal C, Martinez-Marti A, Majem M et al. JAMA Netw Open 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40663352/

### lung-nordic

- **Norwegian analysis of ProGRP and NSE within a randomised phase 2 trial of twice-daily thoracic RT (45 vs 60 Gy) in limited-stage SCLC (89 of 170 patients).** Elevated ProGRP at baseline or after chemoradiotherapy predicted shorter PFS and OS. Patients whose ProGRP normalised after CRT did better (median PFS 49.3 vs 7.8 months), whereas NSE was not prognostic. It is a secondary biomarker analysis in about half the trial population. Grønberg M, Aanerud M, Halvorsen TO et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40763415/
- **Norwegian population-based study of radiotherapy use in lung cancer, 2000–2020 (n=55,048).** Curative RT rose from 10% to 22%, mainly from SBRT (11% in 2020). Palliative RT peaked at 42% in 2010 and fell to 30% by 2020, with less palliative thoracic and whole-brain RT. Median OS improved from 6.2 to 14.0 months. It is registry data, so the survival trend cannot be attributed to RT changes alone. Graabak G, Halvorsen TO, Grønberg BH et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40850042/
- **Finnish five-hospital real-world study of operability and surgical outcomes in 2018 (n=545).** Of 545 patients, 156 were potentially operable and 77 had surgery, with 2-year OS of 79% after surgery. Finnish patients had poorer health, more advanced stage and lower surgical rates than other Nordic countries. The authors link delayed diagnosis and less surgery to Finland's poorer survival. It covers a single year and the comparison with Nordic rates is indirect. Paappanen V, Tikka J, Jukkola A et al. Acta Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40697179/
- **ICBP population study of chemotherapy and RT use in lung cancer, 2012–2017, in Australia, Canada, the UK and Norway.** Chemotherapy use ranged from 23% (Northern Ireland) to 45% (Norway). Norway had a relatively flat RT pattern by age in stage 1–3, unlike the UK's steep decline, and the UK had longer times to treatment. Differences in non-surgical treatment plausibly contribute to survival gaps. It is observational, and the reasons for variation are not established. Barclay ME, McPhail S, Johnson SA et al. BMJ Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40662158/

### thoracic-other

- **MesoNet real-world cohort: first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab in pleural mesothelioma (n=135, 12 German centres).** Median OS was 13.1 months overall and 15.5 months in CheckMate 743-eligible patients (16.7 non-epithelioid vs 10.0 epithelioid). irAEs occurred in 37%, 58% of them grade 3/4, and irAEs were associated with longer survival. Real-world survival is shorter than in CheckMate 743, especially for epithelioid disease; the study is retrospective. Shah R, Buchmeier EL, Kopp HG et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40779889/
- **Parallel phase 2 trials of selinexor in advanced thymoma (n=16) and thymic carcinoma (n=15).** ORR was 12.5% in thymoma and 6.7% in thymic carcinoma (one CR), with stable disease in most patients. Nausea (84%) and vomiting were frequent. It shows modest activity with considerable GI toxicity in very small cohorts. Kim C, Aggarwal V, Daugaard G et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40740966/
- **Retrospective study: anti-PD-1 plus taxane-platinum vs taxane-platinum first line in metastatic thymic carcinoma.** PFS was 9.4 vs 6.3 months (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.25–0.65). OS (52.3 vs 32.8 months, HR 0.44, CI crossing 1) and ORR (44.7% vs 46.7%) did not differ significantly. Grade 3–4 TRAEs were 23% vs 32%. It is non-randomised and from China, so randomised trials are needed. Liu M, Zhao J, Liu Z et al. Clin Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40713214/
- **CONFIRM (phase 3, placebo-controlled) translational analysis of nivolumab in relapsed mesothelioma.** The trial met its PFS endpoint but not OS; ORR was 10.3% and grade ≥3 related AEs 20.4% vs 7.2%. Responders did not differ in mutation or neoantigen burden but had activated CD8+ T cells and B cells organised in tertiary lymphoid structures. It is exploratory biomarker work in a small responder group. Fennell DA, Hill K, Zhang M et al. Nat Commun 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691143/
