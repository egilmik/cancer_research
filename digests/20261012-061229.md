### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen / nsclc-drivers
- **INSIGHT: tepotinib + gefitinib vs chemotherapy in EGFR-mutant, T790M-negative NSCLC with MET overexpression or amplification after EGFR-TKI (randomised phase 1b/2, Asia, n=55 in phase 2).** The trial was terminated early for slow accrual, so all analyses are exploratory. Overall PFS was similar (4.9 vs 4.4 months, HR 0.67, 90% CI 0.35–1.28), as was OS (17.3 vs 18.7 months). The benefit was concentrated in MET amplification (n=19: PFS 16.6 vs 4.2 months, HR 0.13; OS 37.3 vs 13.1 months, HR 0.08) and MET IHC3+ (n=34: PFS HR 0.35). Grade ≥ 3 treatment-related AEs were mainly amylase/lipase rises. It supports MET-amplification-selected combination strategies after EGFR TKI, the basis for later osimertinib + MET-inhibitor trials. Subgroups are very small, and the backbone is a first-generation TKI. Wu YL, Cheng Y, Zhou J et al. *Lancet Respir Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32479794/
- **Clonal evolution of osimertinib resistance (multi-region WES/RNA-seq including rapid autopsies, NCI phase II cohort).** A likely resistance mechanism was found in every patient, and most had two or more, concurrently or in sequence. MET amplification occurred in 6 of 9 patients treated first-line, was spatially heterogeneous and was associated with early progression. Other mechanisms included neuroendocrine differentiation without histological transformation, PD-L1 and KRAS amplification, and new BRAF and ESR1 fusions. It argues for combination strategies and caution about single-biopsy profiling at progression. The sample is small. Roper N, Brown AL, Wei JS et al. *Cell Rep Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32483558/
- **Canadian expert consensus: systemic treatment algorithm for advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC.** Because of the OS result and CNS activity, osimertinib is the preferred first-line treatment for common EGFR mutations, including patients with brain metastases. Later lines depend on the first-line agent or T790M status. For uncommon mutations, evidence is mainly retrospective. It is rated high: it is an expert panel, not a formal guideline body, and it confirms a standard already adopted elsewhere. Melosky B, Banerji S, Blais N et al. *Curr Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32489263/
- **Esomeprazole and milk with erlotinib (randomised crossover PK study, Netherlands).** Esomeprazole reduced erlotinib AUC24 by 47% (95% CI 34–57; n=9) and Cmax by 56%. Taking erlotinib with milk instead of water did not change exposure, with or without the PPI (AUC −3% and 0%). It supports avoiding PPIs with erlotinib; the authors' milk hypothesis did not hold. It is a small PK study with no clinical outcomes. Veerman GDM, Hussaarts KGAM, Peric R et al. *Clin Pharmacokinet*, 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32557346/
- **Taletrectinib (DS-6051b/AB-106), ROS1/NTRK TKI (first-in-human phase I, USA, n=46).** The MTD was 800 mg once daily, and DLTs were grade 3 transaminase rises at 1200 mg. The main AEs were nausea (48%) and diarrhoea (44%). Confirmed ORR was 33% in six crizotinib-refractory ROS1+ NSCLC patients. A cabozantinib-sensitive ROS1 L2086F mutation was identified as acquired resistance. It is early data for a next-generation ROS1 TKI (the phase 2 registration appeared in the previous chunk). The efficacy sample is tiny. Papadopoulos KP, Borazanci E, Shaw AT et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32591465/
- **Selpercatinib: First Approval (*Drugs*) and *Cancer Discov* news.** Selpercatinib received FDA approval (May 2020) for RET fusion-positive NSCLC and RET-altered thyroid cancers, based on the phase I/II LIBRETTO-001 trial. The news item notes that pralsetinib had been submitted. These are journal reports of the approval, not the decision itself. Markham A. *Drugs*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32557397/ ; *Cancer Discov*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32493697/
- **Capmatinib: First Approval (*Drugs*).** Capmatinib received FDA approval in May 2020, its first approval anywhere, for metastatic NSCLC with METex14 skipping detected by an FDA-approved test. This is a journal profile. Dhillon S. *Drugs*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32557339/

### lung-key-evidence / lung-nordic / nsclc-immunotherapy
- **Switch-maintenance pemetrexed vs observation with pemetrexed at progression (Norwegian randomised phase III, n=105).** After four cycles of carboplatin–vinorelbine, immediate pemetrexed gave longer PFS (3.1 vs 1.9 months; adjusted HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.35–0.80). OS was numerically longer (12.0 vs 10.0 months, p=0.10; adjusted HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.42–1.01), and toxicity and HRQoL did not differ. This trial tested what earlier maintenance trials lacked: 73% of the observation arm received pemetrexed at progression, and PS 2 and elderly patients were included. It is underpowered, and the setting is chemotherapy-only, which is now uncommon. Halvorsen TO, Stokke K, Killingberg KT et al. *Acta Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32543258/
- **FALUCA: fruquintinib vs placebo third-line in non-squamous NSCLC (phase III, China, n=527).** OS did not improve (8.9 vs 10.4 months, HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.82–1.28), although PFS did (3.7 vs 1.0 months, HR 0.34) and ORR was 13.8% vs 0.6%. Grade ≥ 3 hypertension occurred in 21%. A post hoc OS gain in patients without later therapy is hypothesis-generating only. It is a negative trial. Lu S, Chen G, Sun Y et al. *Lung Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32592986/
- **ETOP/EORTC SPLENDOUR: denosumab added to first-line platinum doublet (phase III, n=514, closed early).** OS was 8.2 vs 8.7 months (HR 0.96, 95% CI 0.78–1.19), with no benefit in patients with or without bone metastases. Denosumab was well tolerated. This closes the hypothesis, raised by a retrospective analysis, of an anticancer survival effect of denosumab in NSCLC. The trial closed for slow accrual and used chemotherapy only. Peters S, Danson S, Hasan B et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32565388/
- **WJOG7512L: S-1 maintenance vs best supportive care after carboplatin–S-1 in squamous NSCLC (phase 3, Japan, n=131 randomised).** PFS improved (HR 0.548, 95% CI 0.374–0.802), with mainly mild toxicity. The abstract gives no OS data. It is relevant only to chemotherapy-only practice and S-1, which is not used in Norway. Tanaka K, Morita S, Ando M et al. *Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32484914/
- **IASLC Pathology Committee grading system for invasive pulmonary adenocarcinoma.** Grade is based on the predominant pattern plus the proportion of high-grade patterns (solid, micropapillary, complex gland), with a 20% cut-off: grade 1 lepidic, grade 2 acinar or papillary with < 20% high-grade, grade 3 ≥ 20% high-grade. It was developed in 284 stage I tumours and validated in independent cohorts (n=212 and n=300), with AUC for RFS 0.69–0.75 and OS 0.74–0.79 and good interobserver agreement (κ 0.617). It is a practical prognostic grade relevant to adjuvant decisions. The cohorts are retrospective. Moreira AL, Ocampo PSS, Xia Y et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32562873/
- **TERAVOLT: COVID-19 in patients with thoracic cancers (international registry, first 200 patients, 8 countries).** Most patients (76%) had NSCLC and 74% were on active treatment. Of the 200, 76% were hospitalised and 33% died; only 10% of those meeting ICU criteria were admitted. Age > 65, smoking and chemotherapy (alone or combined) were associated with higher mortality in univariable analysis. It is an early registry with selection bias towards hospitalised cases. Garassino MC, Whisenant JG, Huang LC et al. *Lancet Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32539942/
- **ESMO adapted recommendations for lung cancer management in the COVID-19 era.** This is a structured tier 1–3 prioritisation of diagnosis, staging and treatment, based on Cancer Care Ontario, Huntsman and ESMO-MCBS criteria. It is temporary pandemic guidance, not a permanent change in recommendations. Passaro A, Addeo A, Von Garnier C et al. *ESMO Open*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32581069/
- **ESMO eUpdate of the early and locally advanced NSCLC guideline (letter).** The item has no abstract, so the changed recommendation cannot be stated from it, and it is rated high, not practice-changing. Pentheroudakis G, ESMO Guidelines Committee. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502714/

### sclc
- **FIRE-SCLC: first-line SRS vs WBRT for SCLC brain metastases (multicentre cohort, n=710 SRS, 28 centres).** After SRS, median OS was 8.5 months (11.0 months for 1 lesion, 5.5 months for ≥ 11) and 12-month local failure 7.0%. Distant CNS failure was 41.6% and neurological death 12.4%. In propensity-matched comparison, WBRT improved time to CNS progression (HR 0.38) but not OS (6.5 vs 5.2 months, favouring SRS) or CNS PFS. It challenges WBRT as the default in SCLC brain metastases. It is retrospective and observational. Rusthoven CG, Yamamoto M, Bernhardt D et al. *JAMA Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32496550/
- **SUKSES: biomarker-driven phase 2 umbrella in recurrent SCLC (Korea).** Of 286 screened, arms were assigned by biomarker: adavosertib for MYC amplification or CDKN2A/TP53, vistusertib for RICTOR amplification, and adavosertib or AZD2811 for non-selected patients. There were no objective responses in any arm, and PFS was 1.2–1.6 months. It is a negative trial; biomarker selection did not rescue these agents. The arms were very small (4–21 patients). Park S, Shim J, Mortimer PGS et al. *Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32584426/

### lung-screening / lung-nordic
- **Psychosocial consequences of false positives in the Danish Lung Cancer Screening Trial (nested matched cohort, n=130 positives).** False positives had significantly more negative psychosocial consequences at 1 week (7 outcomes) and 1 month (3 outcomes) than controls. There were no long-term effects. It quantifies a short-term harm of screening relevant to programme design in Norway. The cohort is small and from one trial. Rasmussen JF, Siersma V, Malmqvist J et al. *BMJ Open*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32503869/
- **Inclusion in cancer patient pathways (pakkeforløp) in Norway 2015–2016 (registry linkage, n=30,747).** Inclusion for lung cancer rose from 79.0% to 87.3%, exceeding the 70% national target. Older age and low income (OR 1.52, 95% CI 1.16–1.99) were associated with non-inclusion of lung cancer patients, and there was regional variation. It is directly relevant to equity of access in Norway. It covers the first years only. Nilssen Y, Brustugun OT, Eriksen MT et al. *BMC Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32473650/
- **LDCT screening in never-smokers (Korean single-centre cohort, 17,968 never-smokers vs 19,468 ever-smokers).** Among never-smokers, 16.2% had positive nodules, 0.77% had biopsies and 0.47% had cancer, 89% of them stage I and 98% adenocarcinoma. False-positive biopsy and complication rates did not differ from ever-smokers. It is relevant to never-smoker screening debates, but participants were self-referred health-check attendees at a single centre, with no mortality endpoint. Kim YW, Kang HR, Kwon BS et al. *Eur Respir J*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32482786/

### thoracic-other
- **REMORA: lenvatinib second-line in thymic carcinoma (phase 2, Japan, n=42).** ORR by central review was 38% (90% CI 25.6–52.0) and stable disease 57%, at 15.5 months' median follow-up. Grade 3 hypertension occurred in 64%, and there were no treatment-related deaths. It established lenvatinib as a second-line option in a rare disease with no standard. It is single-arm and Japanese only. Sato J, Satouchi M, Itoh S et al. *Lancet Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502444/
