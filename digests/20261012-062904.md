## Practice-changing

### ESMO recommendations for NGS in metastatic cancers (ESMO Precision Medicine Working Group, 2020)
This is ESMO's first society-level recommendation on next-generation sequencing. It grades NGS use into three levels. It recommends routine tumour NGS in advanced non-squamous NSCLC, as well as prostate and ovarian cancer and cholangiocarcinoma. Large multigene panels are acceptable where they add little cost over small panels. Off-label genomically matched drugs should be used only within national or regional access programmes. For lung cancer, this moves multigene NGS from an option to the recommended default for non-squamous disease. Squamous NSCLC is not covered by the routine recommendation. The recommendation is consensus-based and not linked to outcome data from a randomised comparison of NGS against sequential single-gene testing. Its impact in Norway depends on laboratory capacity and on how hospital trusts fund panel testing. Mosele F, Remon J, Mateo J et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32853681/

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-drivers / nsclc-resistance-nextgen
- **AURA3 final OS: osimertinib vs platinum-pemetrexed after EGFR-TKI in T790M-positive NSCLC (phase 3, n=419).** OS was a secondary endpoint. HR 0.87 (95% CI 0.67–1.12; P=0.277), median 26.8 vs 22.5 months, and 73% of the chemotherapy arm crossed over to osimertinib. The crossover-adjusted HR was 0.54 (0.18–1.6). Time to first subsequent therapy or death strongly favoured osimertinib (HR 0.21). The result confirms an established second-line standard, and with first-line osimertinib now standard, this setting has shrunk. Papadimitrakopoulou VA, Mok TS, Han JY et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32861806/
- **RELAY patient-reported outcomes: ramucirumab + erlotinib vs placebo + erlotinib first-line in EGFR-mutant NSCLC (phase 3).** The primary result was PFS 19.4 vs 12.4 months, HR 0.59. Time to deterioration of the LCSS total score did not differ (HR 0.96, 0.69–1.34), and EQ-5D was similar. Blood in sputum worsened with ramucirumab (HR 1.99, 1.21–3.28). This shows the PFS gain did not cost quality of life. The comparator, erlotinib alone, is no longer standard since osimertinib. Yoh K, Atagi S, Reck M et al. *Curr Med Res Opin*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32780643/
- **ALTA-1L second interim analysis: brigatinib vs crizotinib first-line in ALK-positive NSCLC (phase 3, n=275).** With 24.9 months median follow-up, BIRC-assessed PFS was 24.0 vs 11.0 months (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.35–0.68). Time to worsening of global QoL was delayed (HR 0.70, 0.49–1.00), and no new safety signals emerged. OS is immature. The comparator is crizotinib, which is no longer the first-line standard now that alectinib is used, so this is high rather than practice-changing. Camidge DR, Kim HR, Ahn MJ et al. *J Clin Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32780660/
- **LIBRETTO-001: selpercatinib in RET fusion-positive NSCLC (phase 1–2, NEJM).** Among 105 platinum-pretreated patients, ORR was 64% (95% CI 54–73) and median DoR 17.5 months. Among 39 untreated patients ORR was 85% (70–94), and intracranial ORR was 91% in 11 patients with measurable CNS disease. Grade ≥3 toxicities were mainly hypertension (14%) and transaminase rises. Only 2% discontinued for toxicity. It is single-arm, with no randomised comparison against chemo-immunotherapy. Drilon A, Oxnard GR, Tan DSW et al. *N Engl J Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32846060/

### nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-key-evidence / lung-top-journals
- **ORIENT-11: sintilimab + pemetrexed-platinum vs placebo + chemotherapy first-line in nonsquamous NSCLC without EGFR/ALK (phase 3, China, n=397).** IRRC-assessed PFS was 8.9 vs 5.0 months (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.36–0.64), and ORR was 51.9% vs 29.8%. Grade ≥3 AEs were 61.7% vs 58.8%. Median follow-up was only 8.9 months, and OS was immature. It reproduces KEYNOTE-189 with a PD-1 antibody not available in Europe. It is relevant mainly for price competition and later FDA review. Yang Y, Wang Z, Fang J et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32781263/
- **JCOG0707: adjuvant S-1 vs UFT after complete resection of node-negative stage I NSCLC >2 cm (phase 3, n=963).** S-1 was not superior for RFS (HR 1.06, 0.82–1.36), and OS was HR 1.10 (0.81–1.50). The 5-year RFS was 79% in both arms, and grade ≥3 toxicity was about 15% in both. The result is negative, and UFT is a Japanese standard not used in Norway. Kunitoh H, Tsuboi M, Wakabayashi M et al. *JTCVS Open*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36004301/
- **Atezolizumab in pretreated NSCLC with idiopathic interstitial pneumonia (phase 2, Japan).** The trial was stopped early after 17 of a planned 38 patients. Pneumonitis occurred in 29.4% (grade ≥3 23.5%, one grade 5). It occurred in 4 of 7 patients with honeycombing vs 1 of 10 without. The study is small, but it provides a clear safety signal for a group excluded from pivotal ICI trials. Honeycombing appears to be the key risk marker. Ikeda S, Kato T, Kenmotsu H et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32858235/
- **The effect of advances in lung-cancer treatment on population mortality (SEER, NEJM).** Incidence-based NSCLC mortality in men fell 6.3% a year from 2013 to 2016, faster than incidence (3.1% a year). Lung cancer-specific survival in men rose from 26% (2001) to 35% (2014), which coincides with targeted-therapy approvals. SCLC mortality fell only with incidence, and survival did not improve. It is an ecological, registry-based analysis, so treatment attribution is inferential. Howlader N, Forjaz G, Mooradian MJ et al. *N Engl J Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32786189/

### lung-screening
- **ECLS: EarlyCDT-Lung autoantibody blood test followed by CT vs usual care (RCT, Scotland, n=12,208).** At 2 years, 58.9% vs 73.2% of lung cancers were stage III/IV at diagnosis (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.41–0.99). Lung cancer and all-cause mortality did not differ. Only 127 cancers occurred. The comparator was symptomatic presentation, not LDCT screening, so the test's incremental value over LDCT is unknown. Sullivan FM, Mair FS, Anderson W et al. *Eur Respir J*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32732334/

It is a secondary analysis, and lead-time and overdiagnosis bias inflate comparisons with SEER. Gierada DS, Pinsky PF. *Chest*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822676/

It is a news item about a draft, not the final recommendation. *Cancer Discov*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32747378/
