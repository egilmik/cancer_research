### egfr-nsclc

- **Dacomitinib With or Without Dose Titration in Treatment-Naive Advanced EGFR-Mutated NSCLC - Primary Analysis of the ATORG-003 Phase 2 Trial.**  
  ATORG-003 was a single-arm, investigator-initiated phase 2 trial (118 treatment-naive stage IIIB–IV EGFR-mutant patients, five Asian countries) of dacomitinib 30 mg with optional titration to 45 mg. The primary endpoint (12-month PFS in patients without CNS metastases, vs a historical control) was met: 61.2% (95% CI 50.5–70.3); median PFS 16.1 months, ORR 77%, intracranial ORR 54% (n=24). Grade 3/4 TRAEs occurred in 25%; T790M was found in 30% (plasma) and 52% (tissue) of resistant cases. Limitations: no randomised comparator, historical control, immature OS, and it does not address osimertinib-era standards.  
  Tan AC, Kim TM, Zhou S et al. et al., JTO Clin Res Rep, 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42871070/

### lung-key-evidence

- **Vietnamese expert consensus on the diagnosis and multidisciplinary management of resectable non-small cell lung cancer.**  
  Vietnamese multidisciplinary Delphi-informed consensus (32 experts, Aug 2025–Jan 2026) with 35 statements across staging, surgery, perioperative and adjuvant therapy for resectable NSCLC; all reached ≥75% agreement (82–100%). It mainly adapts international evidence and flags implementation gaps (PET/CT, mediastinal staging, molecular testing). No specific changed recommendation is stated in the abstract.  
  Do QK, Pham DX, Nguyen HTT et al. et al., Transl Lung Cancer Res, 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42870861/

- **Camrelizumab plus chemotherapy as induction therapy followed by concurrent chemoradiotherapy and camrelizumab consolidation in limited-stage small cell lung cancer: a randomized phase II trial.**  
  Randomised phase II (ChiCTR2000032275; n=41) of induction camrelizumab plus etoposide-platinum, then CRT and camrelizumab consolidation vs induction chemotherapy and CRT alone in LS-SCLC. 1-year PFS was 65.5% vs 47.4% (P=0.029); median PFS 24.0 vs 11.8 months; median OS not reached vs 23.9 months (P=0.042) at 32.1 months' median follow-up; severe irAEs 4.8%. Relevant to the SCLC criteria, but the sample is very small, the comparator lacks consolidation immunotherapy (the current standard), and biomarker analyses are exploratory.  
  Liu D, Yang L, Hu M et al. et al., Signal Transduct Target Ther, 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42869886/

- **American Radium Society Appropriate Use Criteria: Accelerated hypofractionated radiotherapy in the multidisciplinary management of locally-advanced non-small cell lung cancer ineligible for concurrent chemotherapy.**  
  American Radium Society appropriate-use criteria from a 15-member panel (modified Delphi) on accelerated hypofractionated RT in locally advanced NSCLC ineligible for concurrent chemotherapy; literature review found 14 prospective studies and only one randomised phase III. Consensus: consolidative PD-L1 immunotherapy after RT is appropriate, concurrent is not; conventional 60 Gy/30 fractions remains appropriate; limit doses to 45–55 Gy in 15 fractions near the oesophagus/brachial plexus. Consensus statement based on limited evidence; ARS is not a major guideline body under the rubric, so not rated practice-changing.  
  Chun SG, Iyengar P, Rimner A et al. et al., Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys, 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42869299/


### nsclc-drivers

- **Second- and third-line outcomes after selective RET inhibitor failure in RET fusion-positive metastatic lung cancer: A real-world analysis from the RET-MAP registry.**  
  International RET-MAP registry (52 centres): of 207 patients on first-line selective RET inhibitor, 81 progressed and 65 received later therapy. Second-line platinum-pemetrexed ± immunotherapy gave ORR 41% (12/29), rwPFS 6.1 months (95% CI 4.8–11.4) and OS 13.7 months; chemo vs chemo-immunotherapy was similar (rwPFS HR 1.05). Third-line outcomes were poor (rwPFS 1.5 months, no responses). Retrospective, small subgroups, no randomised comparison.  
  Miliziano D, Rotow JK, Lomibao M et al. et al., Clin Cancer Res, 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42873584/
