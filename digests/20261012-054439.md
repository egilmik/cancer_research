### Radiation therapy for small cell lung cancer: an ASTRO clinical practice guideline
This is a new American Society for Radiation Oncology (ASTRO) guideline, published March 2020. It is built on a systematic review with graded evidence and graded recommendation strength, prompted by recent phase III trials that challenged traditional radiotherapy practice in SCLC. It covers four questions.

**Limited-stage SCLC:**
- Definitive thoracic RT once or twice daily, early in the course of treatment (strong).
- Involved-field RT to post-chemotherapy volumes with conformal techniques (strong).
- Adjuvant RT after resection with positive margins or nodes (conditional).

**Stage I–II node-negative disease:** SBRT or conventional fractionation, with chemotherapy before or after (strong).

**Prophylactic cranial irradiation:**
- LS-SCLC stage II–III responders: PCI strongly recommended.
- LS-SCLC stage I: PCI conditionally *not* recommended.
- Patients at higher neurocognitive risk: a shared decision.
- ES-SCLC: radiation-oncology consultation to weigh PCI against MRI surveillance (strong). This change reflects the Japanese phase III trial.

**ES-SCLC thoracic RT:** strongly recommended in selected patients after chemotherapy, and conditionally after chemo-immunotherapy.

The abstract gives no effect sizes. Several recommendations rest on conditional, lower-certainty evidence, especially for thoracic RT combined with immunotherapy. Applicability in Norway depends on the national guideline (Helsedirektoratet) and MRI capacity for surveillance.
Simone CB, Bogart JA, Cabrera AR et al. *Pract Radiat Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32222430/

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen / nsclc-drivers
- **TREM-study: osimertinib in T790M-positive and -negative NSCLC (Nordic, single-arm phase 2).** This investigator-initiated trial ran in five Northern European countries, including Norway (Helland Å), with 199 patients after progression on ≥ 1 EGFR TKI.

  | | T790M-positive | T790M-negative |
  |---|---|---|
  | ORR | 60% | 28% (95% CI 15–41) |
  | Median PFS | 10.8 months | 5.1 months (HR 0.62) |
  | Median OS | 22.5 months | 13.4 months (HR 0.55) |

  It confirms osimertinib after a first/second-generation TKI and shows clinically relevant activity in some T790M-negative patients. It is single-arm, and that setting has largely disappeared now that osimertinib is given first-line. Eide IJZ, Helland Å, Ekman S et al. *Lung Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32200138/
- **TATTON (multi-arm phase Ib): osimertinib plus selumetinib, savolitinib or durvalumab.** 77 patients progressing on an EGFR TKI were enrolled. ORR was 42%, 44% and 43% in the three arms. Osimertinib plus durvalumab was not feasible because of interstitial lung disease. Osimertinib plus selumetinib or savolitinib was tolerable at the identified doses. The combination with savolitinib went on to further development, and the ILD warning for EGFR TKI plus PD-L1 still stands. It is a small dose-finding study. Oxnard GR, Yang JC, Yu H et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32139298/
- **Trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-expressing/mutant solid tumours (phase I expansion, n=60).** In HER2-mutant NSCLC (n=11), ORR was 72.7% and median PFS 11.3 months (95% CI 8.1–14.3). Overall ORR was 28.3%. Two drug-related deaths occurred, and interstitial lung disease is a known risk. This is an early signal for the ADC that later became standard in HER2-mutant NSCLC. The NSCLC cohort is very small. Tsurutani J, Iwata H, Krop I et al. *Cancer Discov*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32213540/
- **Resistance to third-generation EGFR TKIs (osimertinib, olmutinib).** This retrospective tissue sequencing study included 98 patients, 36 with a post-treatment biopsy. EGFR-dependent mechanisms (C797S, L718Q) were found in 28%, EGFR-independent mechanisms in 58%, and T790M loss in 39%. T790M loss was linked to EGFR-independent resistance. It supports re-biopsy and is relevant to post-osimertinib strategies. It is a small single-country series. Lee J, Kim HS, Lee B et al. *Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32154925/
- **IFCT-1003 LADIE: EGFR TKI ± fulvestrant in postmenopausal women (randomised phase II, n=379).** Adding fulvestrant did not improve outcomes:
  - EGFR-mutant: PFS 9.9 vs 9.4 months, OS 22.1 vs 28.6 months
  - EGFR wild-type: PFS 2.0 vs 1.8 months

  This is a negative trial that closes the antiestrogen combination approach in unselected patients. The comparators (gefitinib, erlotinib) are outdated. Mazieres J, Barlesi F, Rouquette I et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32144133/

### nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-key-evidence / lung-top-journals
- **KEYNOTE-189 updated analysis (phase 3).** At 23.1 months' median follow-up, median OS was 22.0 vs 10.7 months (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.45–0.70) and PFS 9.0 vs 4.9 months (HR 0.48). PFS2 was 17.0 vs 9.0 months (HR 0.49). The benefit was seen regardless of PD-L1 level and with liver or brain metastases. Grade 3–5 AEs occurred in 71.9% vs 66.8%. It confirms the first-line standard already established. No alpha was assigned to this update. Gadgeel S, Rodríguez-Abreu D, Speranza G et al. *J Clin Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32150489/
- **PACIFIC by tumour PD-L1 expression (exploratory).** 451 of 713 patients (63%) had assessable PD-L1. The PFS benefit of durvalumab was seen in all subgroups. The OS benefit was seen in most subgroups but not at TC < 1% (HR 1.14, 95% CI 0.71–1.84). This underlies the EMA label restriction to PD-L1 ≥ 1%. The analysis is post hoc on archival pre-CRT samples, with small subgroups. Paz-Ares L, Spira A, Raben D et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32209338/
- **ARCTIC: durvalumab ± tremelimumab third-line or later (phase III).** The trial had two sub-studies:
  - **Study A (PD-L1 ≥ 25%, n=126):** durvalumab vs standard of care, OS 11.7 vs 6.8 months (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.42–0.93). This comparison was descriptive only.
  - **Study B (PD-L1 < 25%, n=469):** durvalumab + tremelimumab vs standard of care, OS 11.5 vs 8.7 months (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.61–1.05; not significant).

  Treatment-related grade 3/4 AEs were lower with immunotherapy. ARCTIC is now of limited relevance because immunotherapy has moved to first line. Planchard D, Reinmuth N, Orlov S et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32201234/
- **PePS2: pembrolizumab in PS2 NSCLC (single-arm phase 2, UK, n=60).** Durable clinical benefit was 38% first line and 36% in later lines, rising with PD-L1 (22%, 47% and 53% for TPS < 1%, 1–49% and ≥ 50%). Toxicity leading to dose delay or discontinuation occurred in 28%, with no grade 5 events. It supports pembrolizumab in carefully assessed PS2 patients, especially with PD-L1 ≥ 50%. It is non-randomised, and DCB at 18 weeks is a soft endpoint. Middleton G, Brock K, Savage J et al. *Lancet Respir Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32199466/
- **JCOG1210/WJOG7813L: carboplatin-pemetrexed vs docetaxel in patients ≥ 75 years (phase 3 non-inferiority, n=433).** Median OS was 18.7 vs 15.5 months (HR 0.850, 95% CI 0.684–1.056; non-inferiority met) and PFS was longer (HR 0.739). The haematological toxicity profile differed:
  - Carboplatin-pemetrexed had less grade 3–4 neutropenia (46% vs 86%) and febrile neutropenia (4% vs 18%).
  - It had more thrombocytopenia (26% vs 1%) and anaemia (29% vs 2%).

  Rated high, not practice-changing: the comparator predates first-line immunotherapy, and the trial was Japanese only. Okamoto I, Nokihara H, Nomura S et al. *JAMA Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32163097/
- **Vx-001 TERT vaccine maintenance (randomised double-blind phase 2b, n=221).** The primary endpoint was not met: median OS 14.3 vs 11.3 months (p = 0.86). Vaccinated patients with a TERT-specific immune response (29%) lived longer, but this is a non-randomised comparison. It is a negative vaccine trial. Gridelli C, Ciuleanu T, Domine M et al. *Br J Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32210365/
- **IASLC expert panel: immune checkpoint inhibitors in thoracic malignancies.** This is a review of the evidence across NSCLC, SCLC, thymic carcinoma and mesothelioma, with recommendations on resistance, progression patterns, biomarkers, treatment duration and special populations. The abstract does not state specific recommendation changes. Remon J, Passiglia F, Ahn MJ et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32179179/

### lung-local-therapy
- **PET-Plan: PET-based target volume reduction in chemoradiotherapy for stage III NSCLC (randomised, n=205).** The trial ran in 24 centres in Austria, Germany and Switzerland. It randomised target volumes defined by FDG PET alone against conventional PET/CT volumes plus elective nodal irradiation. Locoregional progression was non-inferior and numerically lower:
  - per-protocol: 1-year 14% vs 29% (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.30–1.06)
  - ITT: 17% vs 30% (HR 0.64)

  Acute toxicity was similar (grade ≥ 3 oesophagitis 16% in both arms). Treatment-related deaths numbered 13 vs 7 (PET vs conventional). Rated high rather than practice-changing for three reasons: many centres had already dropped elective nodal irradiation, the abstract does not report OS, and the death imbalance is unexplained. Nestle U, Schimek-Jasch T, Kremp S et al. *Lancet Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32171429/
