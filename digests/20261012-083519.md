### lung-key-evidence / sclc / nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-local-therapy
- **VIOLET: VATS vs open lobectomy for early-stage lung cancer (multicentre randomised trial, n=503, UK, NEJM Evid).** Physical function at 5 weeks was better with VATS (median 73 vs 67; mean difference 4.65, 95% CI 1.69–7.61, against an MCID of 5). Serious adverse events after discharge were 30.7% vs 37.8% (RR 0.81). One-year PFS (HR 0.74) and OS (HR 0.67) did not differ significantly. It supports VATS as the default; long-term oncological follow-up continues. Lim E, Batchelor TJP, Dunning J et al. NEJM Evid 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38319202/
- **CASPIAN 3-year OS update: durvalumab (with or without tremelimumab) plus EP vs EP first line in ES-SCLC (phase III, n=805, ESMO Open).** At a median follow-up of 39.4 months, durvalumab-EP OS HR was 0.71 (95% CI 0.60–0.86), with 36-month OS 17.6% vs 5.8%. The tremelimumab arm had HR 0.81, not formally significant. It confirms an established standard. Paz-Ares L, Chen Y, Reinmuth N et al. ESMO Open 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35279527/
- **PACIFIC post hoc analysis in unresectable stage IIIA-N2 (n=287 of 713, ESMO Open).** Durvalumab benefit held in IIIA-N2 disease (PFS HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.33–0.65; OS HR 0.56, 0.39–0.79). It is exploratory; whether surgery adds benefit in operable N2 remains open. Senan S, Özgüroğlu M, Daniel D et al. ESMO Open 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35247871/
- **Dinutuximab plus irinotecan vs irinotecan or topotecan in relapsed SCLC (randomised phase 3, n=471, Lung Cancer).** OS was 6.9 vs 7.0 vs 7.4 months (p=0.31) and ORR 17.1% vs 18.9% vs 20.2%. The trial is negative for anti-GD2. Irinotecan every 3 weeks performed like topotecan, a useful practical finding. Edelman MJ, Dvorkin M, Laktionov K et al. Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35278766/
- **Veliparib plus carboplatin-paclitaxel vs standard chemotherapy in advanced non-squamous NSCLC (phase III, n=595, Clin Lung Cancer).** The primary OS endpoint in LP52-positive patients (13%) was not met (11.2 vs 9.2 months, HR 0.644, p=0.113). OS was identical overall (12.1 months both arms, HR 0.986). The trial is negative and pre-immunotherapy. Govindan R, Lind M, Insa A et al. Clin Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35331641/
- **SEOM-GEINO guideline (2021): systemic therapy and management of CNS metastases (breast, melanoma, lung; Clin Transl Oncol).** It covers targeted and immunotherapies with CNS activity. It is a national guideline, but the abstract does not name a changed recommendation. Martínez-García M, Servitja Tormo S, Vilariño Quintela N et al. Clin Transl Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35258806/

### lung-screening / lung-nordic
- **One-off LDCT screening in China (multicentre population-based prospective cohort, 223,302 high-risk participants, Lancet Respir Med).** After inverse probability weighting, the screened group (35.6%) had higher lung cancer incidence (HR 1.47) and lower lung cancer mortality (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.53–0.92). All-cause mortality was also lower (HR 0.68, 0.57–0.82), which points to healthy-screenee bias. Median follow-up was 3.6 years, and screening was self-selected, not randomised. Li N, Tan F, Chen W et al. Lancet Respir Med 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35276087/
- **DELUGE: lung cancer found through LDCT screening, nodule programmes or neither (prospective observational, n=22,886, Mississippi Delta, JCO).** Screening- and nodule-detected cancers were more often stage I–II (61%, 60% vs 44%). 5-year OS was 76%, 60% and 44%. Only 54% of all lung cancers met USPSTF 2021 eligibility, which supports nodule programmes as a complement. It is observational, with lead-time bias. Osarogiagbon RU, Liao W, Faris NR et al. J Clin Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35258994/
- **SELEPREBB: intensive vs standard bronchoscopic surveillance of low-grade preinvasive bronchial lesions (randomised, n=364, France, Eur Respir J).** Six-monthly autofluorescence bronchoscopy did not reduce invasive cancer or CIS (OR 0.63, 95% CI 0.20–1.96). Baseline dysplasia predicted cancer (OR 5.9), and smoking cessation was associated with clearance of dysplasia and lower 5-year cancer risk. Guisier F, Deslee G, Birembaut P et al. Eur Respir J 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35236723/
- **Cancer patient pathway compliance in Norway (Cancer Registry of Norway linked to NPR and Statistics Norway, n=20,668, BMC Cancer).** 76% of lung cancer patients starting systemic therapy were treated within the recommended timeframes. Treatment decision to start of treatment was the main delay. Travelling outside one's own health trust prolonged waits and lowered the odds of surgery and radiotherapy for lung cancer. The data are from 2015–2016. Nilssen Y, Brustugun OT, Eriksen MT et al. BMC Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35227226/

### lung-ctdna-mrd
- **Residual ctDNA after curative treatment of early-stage NSCLC using the tumour-informed RaDaR assay (n=88, 363 samples, Ann Oncol).** Pre-treatment detection was 24%, 77% and 87% in stage I, II and III. Post-treatment ctDNA detected 64.3% of patients who relapsed, with specificity >98.5% and a median lead time of 212.5 days. It is a small cohort without an intervention. Gale D, Heider K, Ruiz-Valdepenas A et al. Ann Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35306155/

### car-t-solid
- **PSMA-targeting CAR-T armoured with a dominant-negative TGF-β receptor in mCRPC (phase 1, 13 treated, Nat Med).** The prespecified safety and feasibility endpoints were met. Five had grade ≥2 CRS, including one death after grade 4 CRS with concurrent sepsis in a patient with >98% PSA reduction. Three others had a PSA fall ≥30%. CAR-T expansion and tumour trafficking were shown, but toxicity is substantial. Narayan V, Barber-Rotenberg JS, Jung IY et al. Nat Med 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35314843/
