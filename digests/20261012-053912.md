## Practice-changing

### SEOM clinical guidelines for the treatment of small-cell lung cancer (2019)
This is the Spanish Society of Medical Oncology (SEOM) national guideline for SCLC, 2019 version, published February 2020. The changed recommendation is for extensive-stage disease: adding immunotherapy to platinum-etoposide is now the recommended first-line standard of care, because it gave a significant overall-survival gain. The underlying trials (IMpower133 with atezolizumab and CASPIAN with durvalumab) are not named or quantified in the abstract. Other recommendations are unchanged:
- **Stage I–IIA:** surgery followed by adjuvant platinum-etoposide.
- **Stage IIB–IIIC:** early concurrent platinum-etoposide plus thoracic radiotherapy, then PCI if there is no progression.
- **Second line:** topotecan remains the standard. Platinum-etoposide rechallenge is recommended for sensitive relapse (≥ 3 months), and lurbinectedin and immunotherapy are named as new options.

The abstract gives no effect sizes, toxicity data or grading of evidence. The guideline is consensus-based and does not address reimbursement. In Norway the question is which chemo-immunotherapy regimens Nye Metoder has approved for ES-SCLC.
Dómine M, Moran T, Isla D et al. *Clin Transl Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32040815/ (doi:10.1007/s12094-020-02295-w)

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen / nsclc-drivers
- **TATTON: osimertinib plus savolitinib in MET-amplified EGFR-mutant NSCLC after EGFR TKI (phase 1b).** This interim analysis covers two expansion cohorts. In part B (n=138; prior third-generation TKI or T790M-negative/positive) the objective response rate was 48% (95% CI 39–56). In part D (n=42; no prior third-generation TKI, T790M-negative, savolitinib 300 mg) it was 64% (95% CI 46–79). Grade ≥ 3 adverse events occurred in 57% and 38%, and two deaths were possibly treatment-related. This is the first substantial dataset for an EGFR + MET combination as a post-osimertinib strategy. It is open-label and single-arm, with no PFS/OS comparison. Sequist LV, Han JY, Ahn MJ et al. *Lancet Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32027846/
- **INCREASE: high- vs routine-dose icotinib in L858R NSCLC (randomised phase II).** 176 patients with L858R were randomised to icotinib 250 mg or 125 mg three times daily, and 77 patients with exon 19 deletions received the routine dose. Median PFS was 12.9 vs 9.2 months with high vs routine dose (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.53–1.05). The authors call this significant, but the CI crosses 1. ORR was 73% vs 48%, and grade 3/4 toxicity was similar. It addresses the poorer outcome of L858R on first-generation TKIs, but icotinib is not used in Europe and osimertinib has replaced first-generation TKIs as standard. Li X, Zhang L, Jiang D et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32060099/
- **Alflutinib (furmonertinib, AST2818) in T790M-positive NSCLC: phase 1/expansion.** 130 patients were treated (14 in escalation, 116 in expansion at 40–240 mg). Independently reviewed ORR in the expansion phase was 76.7%, and 70.6% in patients with CNS metastases (12/17). Treatment-related grade ≥ 3 adverse events occurred in 8%. There was no clear dose–response. It is a new third-generation EGFR TKI with a favourable early profile, but the data are single-arm and from Chinese patients only. Shi Y, Zhang S, Hu X et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32007598/
- **Acquired resistance to MET TKIs in MET exon 14 NSCLC.** In 20 patients sequenced at progression, a resistance mechanism was found in 75%:
  - on-target (MET kinase-domain mutations at H1094, G1163, L1195, D1228, Y1230, or MET exon 14 allele amplification) in 35%
  - off-target (KRAS mutation or amplification, EGFR, HER3, BRAF) in 45%

  Two patients with on-target mutations responded again after switching between type I and type II MET TKIs. This supports re-biopsy and choosing the TKI type by mechanism. It is a small series. Recondo G, Bahcall M, Spurr LF et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32034073/
- **MET alterations as a recurring resistance mechanism in ALK-positive NSCLC.** In 207 post-treatment samples, MET amplification was found in 15% of biopsies after next-generation ALK TKIs (12% after second-generation drugs, 22% after lorlatinib). It was more frequent after first-line second-generation TKIs (P = 0.019). An ST7-MET rearrangement was found twice. Two patients responded rapidly to ALK + MET combination therapy. This is relevant now that alectinib and brigatinib are first-line standard. Evidence for the combination is limited to two patients and cell lines. Dagogo-Jack I, Yoda S, Lennerz JK et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32086345/
- **Lorlatinib: cumulative incidence of CNS vs non-CNS progression (phase II, n=198).** After crizotinib only, the 12-month CNS and non-CNS progression rates were both 22% in patients with baseline brain metastases. Without baseline brain metastases they were 9% CNS vs 43% non-CNS. After ≥ 1 second-generation TKI, non-CNS progression exceeded CNS progression whether or not brain metastases were present (35% vs 23% and 55% vs 12%). This confirms strong intracranial protection with lorlatinib. It is a single-arm competing-risk analysis. Bauer TM, Shaw AT, Johnson ML et al. *Target Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32060867/

### nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-key-evidence / lung-top-journals
- **KEYNOTE-010 long-term outcomes and retreatment.** This is an update of the phase 2/3 trial of pembrolizumab vs docetaxel in previously treated PD-L1-positive NSCLC (n=1,033), with 42.6 months' median follow-up. The OS benefit was maintained:
  - TPS ≥ 50%: HR 0.53 (95% CI 0.42–0.66), 36-month OS 34.5% vs 12.7%
  - TPS ≥ 1%: HR 0.69 (0.60–0.80), 36-month OS 22.9% vs 11.0%

  Grade 3–5 treatment-related AEs occurred in 16% vs 37%. Of the 79 patients who completed 2 years, 98.7% were alive at 12 months after stopping. Six of 14 retreated patients responded. This supports stopping at 2 years. Docetaxel is no longer the relevant comparator now that immunotherapy is first-line. Herbst RS, Garon EB, Kim DW et al. *J Clin Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32078391/
- **KEYNOTE-189 patient-reported outcomes.** These are prespecified exploratory PROs (n=616) of pembrolizumab or placebo plus pemetrexed-platinum. Global health status/QoL was better maintained with pembrolizumab at week 21 (difference 5.3 points, 95% CI 1.1–9.5) but not significantly at week 12. Median time to deterioration in cough, chest pain or dyspnoea was not reached vs 7.0 months. The analysis is exploratory, with nominal p values and declining questionnaire compliance (64–76% at week 21). Garassino MC, Gadgeel S, Esteban E et al. *Lancet Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32035514/
- **Microwave ablation plus chemotherapy vs chemotherapy in advanced NSCLC (randomised phase III).** In 293 treatment-naive Chinese patients with advanced or recurrent NSCLC, median PFS was 10.3 vs 4.9 months (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.28–0.53). Median OS was not reached vs 12.6 months (HR 0.38). Complications of ablation were common but manageable. Rated high, not practice-changing: chemotherapy alone is no longer standard first-line, follow-up is short (about 13 months), and the trial is open-label from one country. Wei Z, Yang X, Ye X et al. *Eur Radiol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32020400/
- **Pembrolizumab concurrent with chemoradiotherapy in stage III NSCLC (phase 1, n=21).** This 3+3 trial tested five timing and dose cohorts. There were no dose-limiting toxicities, but one grade 5 pneumonitis occurred in the safety expansion cohort. Grade ≥ 3 immune-related AEs occurred in 18%. Median PFS was 18.7 months and 12-month PFS 69.7%. It is the safety basis for concurrent rather than consolidative PD-1 inhibition (the KEYNOTE-799 question). It is very small and non-randomised. Jabbour SK, Berman AT, Decker RH et al. *JAMA Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077891/
- **Neoadjuvant sintilimab in resectable NSCLC (phase 1b, n=40).** Patients received two cycles before surgery, and 37 underwent resection. The major pathological response rate was 40.5% and pCR (primary tumour) 16.2%. MPR was much higher in squamous than in adenocarcinoma (48.4% vs 0%). Grade ≥ 3 treatment-related AEs occurred in 10%, including one death. A fall in SUVmax predicted pathological response. This is early support for perioperative PD-1 blockade. It is single-arm with no survival data. Gao S, Li N, Gao S et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32036071/
- **IASLC recommendations for pathological assessment after neoadjuvant therapy.** These are multidisciplinary recommendations defining how to gross and process resection specimens and how to score % viable tumour, necrosis and stroma (summing to 100%). They standardise MPR and pCR for all neoadjuvant modalities. Intended for trials, they will shape how neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy results are read. They are a methodological standard, not a treatment recommendation. Travis WD, Dacic S, Wistuba I et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32004713/

### lung-ctdna-mrd
- **ctDNA dynamics predict benefit from consolidation immunotherapy after chemoradiotherapy.** CAPP-Seq was applied to 218 samples from 65 patients with locally advanced NSCLC, 28 of whom received consolidation ICI. Patients with undetectable ctDNA after chemoradiotherapy did well with or without ICI, and one of them died of pneumonitis. Patients with MRD did significantly better with ICI than without. Early ctDNA response during ICI identified responders. This is the rationale for MRD-guided consolidation. It is retrospective and small, with non-randomised ICI allocation. Moding EJ, Liu Y, Nabet BY et al. *Nat Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34505064/
- **ctDNA surveillance in long-term responders to PD-(L)1 blockade.** In 31 patients with PFS ≥ 12 months, ctDNA was sampled at a median of 26.7 months. Of 27 with undetectable ctDNA, 25 (93%) remained progression-free, while all 4 with detectable ctDNA progressed (PPV 1.0, 95% CI 0.51–1; NPV 0.93). This could inform how long to continue immunotherapy. It is hypothesis-generating, with very few events. Hellmann MD, Nabet BY, Rizvi H et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32046999/

### thoracic-other
- **Lurbinectedin in second/third-line mesothelioma (SAKK 17/16, single-arm phase II).** 42 patients were treated with lurbinectedin 3.2 mg/m² every 3 weeks. The primary endpoint, PFS at 12 weeks, was met at 52.4% (90% CI 38.7–63.5). Median PFS was 4.1 months and OS 11.1 months, and activity was independent of histology or prior immunotherapy. Grade 3–4 toxicity occurred in half the patients, mainly neutropenia (23.8%) and fatigue. It is a promising signal in a setting with few options. It is single-arm, with only two objective responses. Metaxas Y, Früh M, Eboulet EI et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32085891/
