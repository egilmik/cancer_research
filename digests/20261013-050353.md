## High

### lung-key-evidence / nsclc-immunotherapy

- **CHOICE-01 final OS (randomised, double-blind phase 3, first-line advanced NSCLC).** Toripalimab plus chemotherapy vs placebo plus chemotherapy: median OS 23.8 vs 17.0 months (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.57–0.93, nominal P=0.01), with the benefit most marked in non-squamous disease. Biomarker work found that high TMB and FA-PI3K-Akt and IL-7 pathway mutations predicted more benefit, and that an early ctDNA response (cycle 3 day 1) was linked to better outcomes. The comparator is no longer standard and the biomarker findings are exploratory. Zhong J, Fei K, Wu L et al. Signal Transduct Target Ther 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39715755/
- **EDEN / GOIRC 04-2016 (open-label randomised phase III, squamous NSCLC, n=125).** Switch-maintenance nivolumab after first-line platinum chemotherapy did not improve OS compared with nivolumab at second line (median OS 14.9 vs 18.8 months, HR 1.09, 95% CI 0.74–1.62). Accrual stopped at 125 of 388 planned patients once ICIs moved to first line, so the trial is underpowered and the setting is now outdated. Gelsomino F, Boni L, Tiseo M et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39700681/
- **Neoadjuvant atezolizumab plus chemotherapy, 3-year update (single-arm phase II, n=30).** 26 R0 resections, 17 MPR (10 pCR). At a median follow-up of 39.5 months, median OS was 55.8 months, median DFS 34.5 months and 36-month OS 77%. 6 of 14 recurrences were brain metastases, and reduced STK11 copy number was linked to worse response and more brain metastases. The trial is small and single-arm, and the translational associations are hypothesis-generating. Henick BS, Koch PD, Gainor JF et al. J Immunother Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39721753/
- **periSCOPE (single-arm phase II, potentially resectable stage IIIB NSCLC, n=30).** Perioperative sintilimab plus platinum chemotherapy gave an ORR of 63.3% and 90% resection (R0 96.4%). MPR was 44.4% and pCR 22.2%. At a median follow-up of 34.7 months, 36-month EFS was 42.8% and OS 70.1% (ITT). Postoperative atrial fibrillation (22.2%), pneumonitis (18.5%) and heart failure (14.8%) were common. The trial is small, Chinese and single-arm, and stage IIIB resectability is selected. Yu X, Huang C, Du L et al. EClinicalMedicine 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39720604/
- **Adjuvant ICI after pCR/MPR following neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy (multicentre retrospective, n=208).** In IPTW-weighted analysis, adjuvant ICI did not improve 3-year DFS (90.2% vs 93.2%, HR 2.47, 95% CI 0.74–8.22) or OS (89.1% vs 93.9%). 15% stopped adjuvant ICI because of adverse events. These are de-escalation data relevant to treatment duration, but they are retrospective with few events, and three quarters of the patients had squamous histology. Zhao ZR, Yan WP, Yu XY et al. J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39617319/
- **Atezolizumab irAE–efficacy pooled analysis (14 trials, n=9,521).** Using time-varying Cox models to handle immortal-time bias, grade 1–2 irAEs were linked to longer OS (HR 0.65), while grade 3–4 pneumonitis and colitis were linked to shorter OS. Hepatitis showed no association. These are post-hoc associations across NSCLC, SCLC and other cancers, not causal effects. Durán-Pacheco G, Chandler GS, Maiya V et al. J Immunother Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39537212/
- **IBI110 (anti-LAG-3) ± sintilimab (first-in-human phase Ia/Ib, China).** No DLTs; grade ≥3 TRAEs in about 22%. In the combination-with-chemotherapy expansion, the confirmed ORR was 75.0% in squamous NSCLC (n=20) and 70.6% in gastric cancer (n=17). Monotherapy and doublet ORRs were low (3.6% and 14%). The expansion cohorts are small and include chemotherapy, so the LAG-3 contribution cannot be isolated. Mao C, Xiong A, Qian J et al. J Hematol Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39736787/
- **IBI318, a PD-1×PD-L1 bispecific (phase Ia/Ib, n=103).** RP2D 300 mg Q2W; grade ≥3 TRAEs in 9.7%. Confirmed ORR was 45.5% in treatment-naïve NSCLC in phase Ib. These are early, small cohorts without a comparator. Ruan DY, Wei XL, Liu FR et al. J Hematol Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39614368/

### Guidelines and consensus statements

- **AATS clinical practice guideline and companion consensus: high-risk stage I NSCLC.** An AATS expert panel (modified Delphi) issued 14 statements and 5 vignettes. Surgery (sublobar resection) is generally favoured when safe. SABR is the likely next choice in non-surgical patients, and image-guided thermal ablation is reasonable in selected patients. Biopsy before non-surgical treatment and multidisciplinary review are emphasised. The underlying evidence is mostly non-randomised. Pennathur A, Lanuti M, Merritt RE et al. Semin Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39672521/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39662537/
- **Pan-Asian adapted ESMO guideline for oncogene-addicted metastatic NSCLC.** It adapts the January 2023 ESMO guideline with ten Asian oncology societies. Voting was independent of local drug access, and access differences are discussed separately. The record does not list specific changes from the ESMO original. Lee SH, Menis J, Kim TM et al. ESMO Open 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39615406/
- **Japanese Society of CT Screening nodule-management guideline, 6th edition.** It adds recommendations for LDCT screening in Japan and changes the recommended solid-component diameter and follow-up interval for nodules under 15 mm with a solid component under 8 mm. It also adds accreditation criteria. It applies to the Japanese setting. Ashizawa K, Maruyama Y, Kobayashi T et al. Jpn J Radiol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39636528/
- **Chinese expert consensus on ICIs combined with radiotherapy in lung cancer (2025 edition).** It covers mechanisms, application models and safety for NSCLC and SCLC. The English abstract does not state specific recommendations. Chinese Medical Doctor Association / National Quality Control Center for Cancer. Zhonghua Zhong Liu Za Zhi 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39632257/
- **Canadian modified-Delphi recommendations on esophagitis and pneumonitis with chemoradiation ± durvalumab (stage III NSCLC).** 12 questions, 30 recommendations and a risk-stratification guide covering risk factors, mitigation, grade-based management and when to start or restart durvalumab. This is expert opinion rather than trial evidence. Brade AM, Bahig H, Bezjak A et al. Curr Oncol 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39590114/
- **ASCO guideline rapid recommendation updates, clinical-insights summaries: SCLC systemic therapy and stage III NSCLC.** These are JCO Oncology Practice companion pieces to the ASCO rapid updates. The records have no abstract and do not say which recommendations changed, so they are rated high rather than practice-changing. Khurshid H, Ismaila N, Das M et al. JCO Oncol Pract 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565981/ ; Singh N, Früh M, Gubens MA et al. JCO Oncol Pract 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556773/
- **PSOGI multisocietal GRADE consensus: CRS-HIPEC for diffuse malignant peritoneal mesothelioma.** Using GRADE-ADOLOPMENT, the panel unanimously issued a strong recommendation for CRS-HIPEC in carefully selected patients, despite very low certainty of evidence, and stressed centralised care. It concerns peritoneal, not pleural, mesothelioma. Kusamura S, Cinquini M, Morris D et al. J Surg Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39609952/

### nsclc-drivers

- **Fulzerasib: First Approval (drug profile).** The KRAS G12C inhibitor fulzerasib received conditional approval in China on 21 August 2024 for pretreated KRAS G12C-mutated advanced NSCLC, based on a single-arm phase II. This is a journal profile, and fulzerasib was not the first KRAS G12C inhibitor approved anywhere, so it does not meet the regulatory bar. Lamb YN. Drugs 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39587006/
- **Selinexor plus docetaxel in pretreated KRAS-mutant NSCLC (phase I/II, n=40).** MTD selinexor 60 mg weekly plus docetaxel 75 mg/m². PR in 22% and SD in 56% of 32 evaluable patients. Outcomes were much better with wild-type TP53 (median PFS 7.4 vs 1.8 months, HR 0.2). Grade ≥3 neutropenia occurred in 60%. The trial is small and single-arm, and the TP53 finding is exploratory. von Itzstein MS, Burns TF, Dowell JE et al. Clin Cancer Res 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39651955/

### sclc / lung-toxicity

- **JMTO LC 08-01 (randomised phase II, ED-SCLC, n=100).** Amrubicin-irinotecan vs cisplatin-irinotecan: 1-year OS 68.0% vs 59.2% (primary endpoint not met, one-sided P=0.18). Median OS 14.8 vs 13.5 months (HR 0.618, 95% CI 0.398–0.961), PFS 4.8 vs 5.4 months. Grade 2–3 ILD occurred in 5 vs 1 patients. Neither arm contains immunotherapy, which is now first-line standard. Yoshioka H, Ishida T, Atagi S et al. Clin Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39482146/
- **Shengjiang Xiexin decoction for irinotecan diarrhoea in SCLC (multicentre double-blind placebo-controlled RCT, 101 completers).** Diarrhoea incidence was 26.4% vs 52.1%, with the largest difference in UGT1A1 high-risk patients (9.1% vs 66.7%). No serious adverse events. The analysis is of completers only, the sample is small, and this is a traditional Chinese herbal formulation with limited availability. Deng C, Liu Q, Yang M et al. Chin Med 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39497116/

### lung-nonpharm / lung-nordic

- **ProWide (Danish multicentre RCT, advanced lung cancer, n=494).** Remote symptom monitoring did not improve OS vs standard care at a median follow-up of 3.5 years (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.75–1.16), and HRQoL effects were not clinically meaningful (the authors call them modest). Survival benefit appeared in exploratory subgroups only (carboplatin-vinorelbine, departments with prior PRO use). This is a negative Nordic trial for ePRO in this setting. Friis RB, Pappot H, Hjollund NH et al. JCO Oncol Pract 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39657078/
- **Home-based exercise and self-management after NSCLC resection (Australian assessor-blinded RCT, n=116).** No difference in the primary endpoint, EORTC physical function at 3 months (difference 1.0 point, 95% CI −6.0 to 8.0). Six-minute walk distance improved by 39.7 m at 3 months and 50.9 m at 6 months, and global QoL by 7.1 points at 3 months. The trial is small and the primary endpoint was negative. Granger CL, Edbrooke L, Antippa P et al. JAMA Netw Open 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39621348/
- **CareSTEPS (multisite RCT, caregivers of patients with advanced lung cancer, n=174).** A 6-week telephone intervention reduced caregiver depression (d=−0.55), anxiety (d=−0.81) and burden (d=−0.37) at 8 weeks vs usual care. The trial is open-label with short follow-up, and the outcomes are caregiver-reported. Rangel ML, Milbury K, Kayser K et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39583146/
- **Nurse-led, screening-triggered early specialised palliative care (Japanese multicentre RCT, n=204).** No significant improvement in FACT-L TOI at 12 weeks (primary endpoint; difference 2.13), a significant difference at 20 weeks (3.58), and no survival difference (12.1 vs 11.1 months). This is a negative primary endpoint, and the later effect is secondary. Matsumoto Y, Umemura S, Okizaki A et al. Cancer Med 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556485/
- **Norwegian nationwide registry study of first-line pembrolizumab (n=8,416 advanced NSCLC on systemic therapy, 2012–2021).** Median OS with first-line pembrolizumab after 2017 was 13.8 months (monotherapy) and 12.8 months (with chemotherapy), vs 8.0 months with chemotherapy before 2017. That is lower than in KEYNOTE-024/189, but the relative gain is similar. The comparison is historical, so improvements in other care may confound it. It is direct Norwegian real-world evidence. Hektoen HH, Tsuruda KM, Fjellbirkeland L et al. Br J Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39489879/
- **Danish nationwide time-toxicity study (stage IV lung cancer, n=4,384).** In the first year after starting systemic therapy, 22% of days alive involved physical health-care contact (mean 56 contact days vs 198 home days). The share was 16% for targeted therapy and 24% for chemotherapy. One-year survival was 45%. The data are descriptive and registry-based, and they do not capture remote contacts or the reasons for contact. Ording AG, Skjøth F, Poulsen LØ et al. JCO Oncol Pract 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39705615/

### thoracic-other

- **SWOG S1701 (randomised phase 2, advanced thymic carcinoma, n=21).** Carboplatin-paclitaxel with or without ramucirumab: PFS HR 0.51 (80% CI 0.24–1.09, P=0.13) and response 88% vs 40% (P=0.04). The trial closed early for poor accrual, so it is underpowered, and OS is immature. Tsao AS, Hsieh MH, Koczywas M et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39619274/
