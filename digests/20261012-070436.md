## Practice-changing

- **CheckMate 743: first-line nivolumab + ipilimumab vs platinum-pemetrexed in unresectable pleural mesothelioma (open-label randomised phase 3, n=605, 103 hospitals in 21 countries).** Nivolumab 3 mg/kg every 2 weeks plus ipilimumab 1 mg/kg every 6 weeks for up to 2 years was compared with up to six cycles of cisplatin or carboplatin plus pemetrexed. At the prespecified interim analysis (median follow-up 29.7 months), the primary endpoint OS improved: median 18.1 vs 14.1 months (HR 0.74, 96.6% CI 0.60–0.91; p=0.0020), with 2-year OS 41% vs 27%. Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs were similar (30% vs 32%). There were three treatment-related deaths with immunotherapy (pneumonitis, encephalitis, heart failure) and one with chemotherapy. It is the first immunotherapy regimen to beat the platinum-pemetrexed standard on OS in mesothelioma. The FDA approved it in October 2020. Still open: the analysis is interim and open-label, the abstract gives no breakdown by histology, and this item contains no Nye Metoder decision for Norway. Baas P, Scherpereel A, Nowak AK et al. Lancet 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33485464/

### nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-key-evidence / lung-top-journals
- **CheckMate 9LA: nivolumab + ipilimumab + 2 cycles of platinum doublet vs 4 cycles of chemotherapy, first line in stage IV NSCLC (phase 3, n=719).** At the interim analysis, OS improved (median 14.1 vs 10.7 months; HR 0.69, 96.71% CI 0.55–0.87). With longer follow-up, median OS was 15.6 vs 10.9 months (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.55–0.80). Serious treatment-related AEs were 30% vs 18%, with 2% treatment-related deaths in each arm. This is the basis for the approved 9LA regimen. The comparator, chemotherapy alone, was no longer the first-line standard, so the trial does not show superiority over pembrolizumab-chemotherapy. Follow-up was short (median 13.2 months). Paz-Ares L, Ciuleanu TE, Cobo M et al. Lancet Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33476593/
- **KEYNOTE-598: pembrolizumab + ipilimumab vs pembrolizumab + placebo in PD-L1 ≥50% NSCLC (double-blind phase III, n=568).** OS was 21.4 vs 21.9 months (HR 1.08, 95% CI 0.85–1.37) and PFS 8.2 vs 8.4 months (HR 1.06). Grade 3–5 AEs were 62.4% vs 50.2%, and fatal AEs 13.1% vs 7.5%. The trial was stopped for futility. This negative trial supports pembrolizumab monotherapy as standard for PD-L1-high NSCLC and argues against adding CTLA-4 blockade in this group. Boyer M, Şendur MAN, Rodríguez-Abreu D et al. J Clin Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33513313/
- **PACIFIC 4-year update: durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy in unresectable stage III NSCLC (phase 3, n=713).** Updated OS HR was 0.71 (95% CI 0.57–0.88), with median OS 47.5 vs 29.1 months and 4-year OS 49.6% vs 36.3%. PFS HR was 0.55, with 4-year PFS 35.3% vs 19.5%. These are exploratory updates that confirm the established standard, and patients with EGFR/ALK alterations were not analysed separately. Faivre-Finn C, Vicente D, Kurata T et al. J Thorac Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33476803/
- **CheckMate 017/057 pooled 5-year outcomes: nivolumab vs docetaxel in pretreated NSCLC (phase III, n=854).** 5-year OS was 13.4% vs 2.6% and 5-year PFS 8.0% vs 0%. Patients progression-free at 2 years had an 82% chance of being alive at 5 years. No new safety signals emerged. These are long-term data for a setting now mostly treated with first-line immunotherapy. Borghaei H, Gettinger S, Vokes EE et al. J Clin Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33449799/
- **Chinese clinical practice guideline for stage IV primary lung cancer, 2021 version (CACO/CIEPA expert group).** It updates Chinese recommendations for targeted therapy and immunotherapy in stage IV disease. The abstract does not name which recommendations changed. Zhonghua Zhong Liu Za Zhi 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33472316/
- **Canadian consensus recommendations on biomarker testing in advanced EGFR-mutant NSCLC (expert working group).** The panel recommends comprehensive reflex biomarker testing with targeted NGS at diagnosis for all non-squamous NSCLC, regardless of stage. This is national expert consensus, and its relevance for Norway is the reflex-NGS principle. Cheema PK, Gomes M, Banerji S et al. Curr Oncol 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33380864/

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-drivers / lung-local-therapy
- **WJOG8715L: osimertinib + bevacizumab vs osimertinib in T790M-positive NSCLC after prior EGFR-TKI (randomised phase 2, n=81).** ORR was higher (68% vs 54%), but PFS was not longer (median 9.4 vs 13.5 months; adjusted HR 1.44, 80% CI 1.00–2.08). OS did not differ. Grade ≥3 proteinuria (23%) and hypertension (20%) occurred with the combination. The trial argues against adding bevacizumab to osimertinib. It was small and limited to Japan, in the second-line T790M setting. Akamatsu H, Toi Y, Hayashi H et al. JAMA Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33410885/
- **Consolidative SABR to all intrapulmonary lesions in oligometastatic NSCLC (≤5 lesions) after first-line systemic therapy (single-arm phase 2, n=47, Mexico).** Median PFS was 34.3 months (95% CI 31.1–38.8), and median OS was not reached. Patients with a complete metabolic response had longer PFS (53.9 vs 31.9 months). Grade 3 pneumonitis occurred in 7.7%. The trial was single-arm and compared with historical data, and 38% of patients received TKIs, which confounds the comparison. Blake-Cerda M, Lozano-Ruíz F, Maldonado-Magos F et al. Lung Cancer 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33385737/
- **Canadian consensus for biomarker testing and treatment of TRK fusion cancer in adults.** It recommends when and how to test for NTRK fusions in five tumour types including NSCLC, and suggests considering a TRK inhibitor for any TRK fusion cancer without other effective options. This is expert consensus. Bebb DG, Banerji S, Blais N et al. Curr Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33467570/

### sclc
- **IMpower133 updated OS and PD-L1 subgroups: atezolizumab + carboplatin-etoposide in ES-SCLC (phase I/III, n=403).** Median OS was 12.3 vs 10.3 months (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.60–0.95), and 18-month OS 34.0% vs 21.0%. Benefit was seen regardless of PD-L1 IHC or blood TMB, although the biomarker analyses are exploratory. The update confirms the established first-line standard. Liu SV, Reck M, Mansfield AS et al. J Clin Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33439693/
- **Four SCLC subtypes defined by transcription factors and immune activation (Cancer Cell).** Tumour expression data separate SCLC-A, SCLC-N, SCLC-P and an inflamed subtype, SCLC-I. In retrospective analysis, SCLC-I gained most from adding immunotherapy to chemotherapy. The other subtypes show vulnerabilities to PARP, Aurora kinase or BCL-2 inhibitors. Subtype switching towards SCLC-I under cisplatin is proposed as a resistance mechanism. The study is translational, and prospective validation of subtype-guided therapy is needed. Gay CM, Stewart CA, Park EM et al. Cancer Cell 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33482121/
- **Germline predisposition in SCLC (Sci Transl Med; 87 patients plus a validation cohort of 79).** 43.7% of patients carried deleterious germline variants in cancer-predisposition genes, enriched for RAD51D, CHEK1, BRCA2 and MUTYH. Carriers had longer recurrence-free survival after platinum, and one BRIP1 carrier responded to HRD-targeted therapy. The finding influenced management or family testing in 10% of patients. The cohorts are small and from a referral centre. Tlemsani C, Takahashi N, Pongor L et al. Sci Transl Med 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33504652/
- **Durvalumab + tremelimumab with or without SBRT in relapsed SCLC (randomised phase II, n=18).** ORR was 0% without SBRT and 28.6% with SBRT, median PFS 2.1 vs 3.3 months, and median OS 2.8 vs 5.7 months. There was no sufficient efficacy signal. The trial was very small and is negative. Pakkala S, Higgins K, Chen Z et al. J Immunother Cancer 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33428583/
- **ASCO endorsement of the ASTRO guideline on radiotherapy for SCLC.** ASCO endorses the June 2020 ASTRO recommendations, with a few discussion points, on thoracic RT in limited stage, SBRT for stage I–II N0, PCI and thoracic consolidation in extensive stage. The underlying ASTRO guideline was rated practice-changing in run 20261012-054439. Daly ME, Ismaila N, Decker RH et al. J Clin Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33502911/

### thoracic-other
- **NVALT19: switch-maintenance gemcitabine vs best supportive care after first-line platinum-pemetrexed in mesothelioma (randomised phase 2, n=130, Netherlands).** PFS improved (median 6.2 vs 3.2 months; HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.33–0.71), confirmed by masked central review. Grade 3–4 AEs were 52% vs 16%, with one treatment-related death. The OS results are not in the abstract. Its role is uncertain now that nivolumab + ipilimumab is first line. de Gooijer CJ, van der Noort V, Stigt JA et al. Lancet Respir Med 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33515500/
- **MiST1: rucaparib in BAP1- or BRCA1-deficient relapsed mesothelioma (single-arm phase 2a, n=26, Leicester).** Disease control was 58% at 12 weeks (95% CI 37–77) and 23% at 24 weeks. Grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 35% of patients. The prespecified success criterion was met, but the trial was single-arm and single-centre, and disease control is a weak endpoint. Fennell DA, King A, Mohammed S et al. Lancet Respir Med 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33515503/
- **SMART final results: short accelerated hemithoracic IMRT (25 Gy/5 fractions) followed by extrapleural pneumonectomy in resectable cT1–3N0 mesothelioma (single-centre phase 2, n=96).** 49% of patients had 30-day grade 3–4 events, and there was one perioperative death. The 5-year cumulative incidence of distant recurrence was 63.3%, mostly in the contralateral chest and peritoneum. The approach is feasible in an expert centre, but extrapleural pneumonectomy has fallen out of favour after MARS, and morbidity is high. Cho BCJ, Donahoe L, Bradbury PA et al. Lancet Oncol 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33450184/

### lung-repurposed / lung-screening
- **Corticosteroids before chemoimmunotherapy in advanced lung cancer (retrospective cohort with IPW, n=316).** Baseline corticosteroid use was not associated with worse OS or rwPFS on chemo-immunotherapy, compared with non-users. The study is small and retrospective, with residual confounding by indication possible. Sorial MN, Huynh JP, Azzoli CG et al. Eur J Cancer 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33431285/
- **Corticosteroids and ICI outcomes in advanced NSCLC (retrospective, n=196, Karolinska).** Steroids given for irAEs did not affect OS. Only steroids given to palliate cancer symptoms predicted shorter OS (HR 2.7, 95% CI 1.5–4.9), which likely reflects poor prognosis (confounding by indication) rather than a steroid effect. The study is single-centre. Skribek M, Rounis K, Afshar S et al. Eur J Cancer 2021. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33419647/
- **Low-dose aspirin for screen-detected subsolid nodules (randomised phase IIb, n=98).** One year of aspirin 100 mg/day did not change nodule size vs placebo (P=0.33). The trial is negative, with a surrogate endpoint and short duration. Bonanni B, Serrano D, Maisonneuve P et al. JNCI Cancer Spectr 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33409459/
