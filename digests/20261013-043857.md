## Practice-changing

- **Extended pleurectomy decortication and chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone for pleural mesothelioma (MARS 2): a phase 3 randomised controlled trial.** MARS 2 was a UK phase 3, open-label, pragmatic superiority RCT at 26 hospitals. After two cycles of platinum-pemetrexed, 335 patients with resectable pleural mesothelioma (86% epithelioid) were randomised to (extended) pleurectomy/decortication plus 2-4 further cycles of chemotherapy, or to chemotherapy alone. The primary endpoint was OS. At a median follow-up of 22.4 months, surgery gave worse survival: median 19.3 vs 24.8 months, and the restricted mean survival time difference at 2 years was -1.9 months (95% CI -3.4 to -0.3, P=0.019). Serious adverse events (grade >=3) were 318 vs 169 (IRR 3.6, 95% CI 2.3-5.5), driven by cardiac and respiratory complications, infection and additional procedures. What is still open: the comparator was chemotherapy alone, so the trial does not test surgery after first-line nivolumab-ipilimumab, now the usual standard, and follow-up is fairly short. No drug approval or reimbursement question arises; the authors conclude that extended pleurectomy decortication was associated with worse 2-year survival and more serious adverse events. Lim E, Waller D, Lau K et al.. Lancet Respir Med 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38740044/
- Also rated high, not practice-changing: DENIM (randomised phase 2/3 of dendritic-cell maintenance in mesothelioma) was negative, and EORTC 1205 is a non-comparative randomised phase II. The NCCN NSCLC v4.2024 selection, the AATS subsolid-nodule consensus, the WABIP airway-stent guideline, the CACA thymic guideline and the STS thymoma consensus do not say in the abstract which recommendations changed.

### nsclc-drivers

- **FDA Approval Summary: Repotrectinib for Locally Advanced or Metastatic ROS1-Positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer.** FDA Approval Summary: traditional approval of repotrectinib on 15 November 2023 for locally advanced or metastatic ROS1-positive NSCLC, based on the single-arm TRIDENT-1 trial. ORR was 79% (95% CI 68-88) with median duration of response 34.1 months in 71 ROS1-TKI-naive patients, and 38% (25-52) with DoR 14.8 months in 56 patients after a prior ROS1 TKI (no prior chemotherapy); responses were seen in CNS metastases and resistance mutations. Dizziness was the most common adverse reaction. A retrospective journal summary of an earlier decision, so high rather than practice-changing. Barbato MI, Bradford D, Ren Y et al.. Clin Cancer Res 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38875108/

### sclc

- **Small Cell Lung Cancer in Norway: Patterns of Care by Health Region and Survival Trends.** Cancer Registry of Norway (8,374 SCLC patients, 2002-2022): stage distribution did not change, while 5-year OS in limited stage rose from 7.7% to 22.8%. Diagnostic workup and treatment varied by health region (MDT use 62.5-85.7%, PET-CT 70.4-86.2%, death without registered treatment 1.2-10.9%). For limited stage in 2018-2022, 5-year OS ranged from 18.7% to 28.7% across regions (P=.019). Points to unwarranted regional variation in Norwegian SCLC care; registry data, residual case-mix confounding possible. Nilssen Y, Brustugun OT, Fjellbirkeland L et al.. Clin Lung Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38692990/

### lung-screening / lung-nonpharm / lung-nordic

- **Impact of lung cancer screening on stage migration and mortality among the national Veterans Health Administration population with lung cancer.** US Veterans Health Administration cohort (57,919 lung cancers 2011-2018, 2,167 screen-detected): prior LDCT screening was linked to more stage I diagnoses (52% vs 27%) and better 5-year OS (50.2% vs 27.9%) and lung cancer-specific survival (59.0% vs 29.7%). With lead-time adjustment and propensity weighting, all-cause mortality was lower (aHR 0.79) among screening-eligible patients treated per guidelines. Real-world support for screening benefit; observational, with residual healthy-user and overdiagnosis bias. Edwards DM, Pirzadeh M, Van T et al.. Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38853532/
- **In-hospital thromboprophylaxis variation and the risk of venous thromboembolism after lung cancer surgery: a nationwide cohort study.** Danish nationwide cohort (9,615 NSCLC resections 2010-2021, four hospitals): 6-month VTE risk after lung cancer surgery was 2.2% and did not differ between hospitals with and without routine in-hospital LMWH thromboprophylaxis (1.7-3.1% vs 2.2%). Suggests in-hospital prophylaxis alone is insufficient and extended prophylaxis deserves study; observational, hospital-level comparison. Christensen TD, Ording AG, Skjøth F et al.. Interdiscip Cardiovasc Thorac Surg 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38702829/
- **Impact of single round of low dose CT lung cancer screening on cause of mortality in different socio-economic groups: a post-hoc analysis of long-term follow-up of the UKLS trial.** UKLS trial post-hoc analysis (single round of LDCT; median follow-up 9.1-9.9 years): screening benefited lung cancer survival equally in more deprived (HR 1.89, 95% CI 1.16-3.08) and less deprived groups (HR 1.87, 1.07-3.26). More deprived people were less likely to respond to the invitation but more often selected by the LLP risk model, and screening had a larger impact on COPD/emphysema deaths in deprived groups. Relevant to equity in screening programme design; post-hoc subgroup analysis. Davies MPA, Vulkan D, Gabe R et al.. Lancet Reg Health Eur 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38813535/
- **Real-world outcomes with durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy in patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC: interim analysis of overall survival from PACIFIC-R.** PACIFIC-R first planned OS analysis (retrospective observational cohort, 1,154 patients in 10 countries starting durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy for unresectable stage III NSCLC in the 2017-2018 early-access programme; median follow-up 38.7 months): median OS not reached, 3-year OS 63.2% (95% CI 60.3-65.9). 3-year OS was numerically higher with PD-L1 >=1% vs <1% (67.0% vs 54.4%) and after concurrent vs sequential CRT (64.8% vs 57.9%). Real-world confirmation of PACIFIC; selected early-access population, no comparator. Filippi AR, Bar J, Chouaid C et al.. ESMO Open 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38833971/
- **Trends in lung cancer survival in the Nordic countries 1990-2016: The NORDCAN survival studies.** NORDCAN survival study (lung cancers diagnosed 1990-2016 in Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden): 1- and 5-year relative survival improved continuously in all countries, across age groups and subtypes, most in Denmark and least in Finland. In the most recent period, age-standardised 5-year relative survival ranged from 13% to 26% between countries; survival was lower with age, in men and in small-cell carcinoma. A Nordic benchmark for Norwegian outcomes; registry data without treatment detail. Lundberg FE, Ekman S, Johansson ALV et al.. Lung Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38795460/

Very small, mixed tumour types; early oncolytic-virus signal only. He Y, Huang X, Li X et al.. J Immunother Cancer 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38719544/
