## Practice-changing

### Reduced lung-cancer mortality with volume CT screening in a randomised trial (NELSON)
NELSON was a randomised trial in the Netherlands and Belgium of 13,195 men (primary analysis) and 2,594 women aged 50–74 at high risk from smoking. Participants were assigned to volume-based low-dose CT at baseline, year 1, year 3 and year 5.5, or to no screening (the comparator). The primary endpoint was lung-cancer mortality, ascertained by registry linkage with at least 10 years of follow-up. At 10 years the cumulative rate ratio for lung-cancer death in men was 0.76 (95% CI 0.61–0.94; P = 0.01): 2.50 vs 3.30 deaths per 1000 person-years. In women the rate ratio was 0.67 (95% CI 0.38–1.14), which is not statistically significant given the small subgroup. On the harms side, screening adherence was 90%, 9.2% of screened men needed at least one extra CT for an indeterminate result, and 2.1% were referred for suspicious nodules. Lung-cancer incidence was higher in the screened arm (5.58 vs 4.91 per 1000 person-years), which raises the possibility of some overdiagnosis. The abstract does not report an all-cause mortality benefit. Volume-based nodule management with low referral rates is the main methodological point for programme design. Whether and how this applies to a Norwegian programme (eligibility criteria, intervals, capacity) is not addressed by the trial.
de Koning HJ, van der Aalst CM, de Jong PA et al. *N Engl J Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31995683/ (doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1911793)

### Therapy for stage IV NSCLC without driver alterations: ASCO and OH (CCO) joint guideline update
This is a guideline update from ASCO and Ontario Health (Cancer Care Ontario), published January 2020. It replaces the 2017 ASCO recommendations on first-line systemic therapy for stage IV NSCLC without EGFR or ALK alterations. It rests on a systematic review of randomised trials from December 2015 to 2019, with five RCTs as the evidence base. Changed recommendations, by PD-L1 level and histology:
- **Non-squamous, PD-L1 TPS ≥ 50%:** single-agent pembrolizumab.
- **Non-squamous, PD-L1 0–49%:** pembrolizumab/carboplatin/pemetrexed for most patients. Alternatives are atezolizumab-based combinations or chemotherapy.
- **Squamous, TPS ≥ 50%:** single-agent pembrolizumab.
- **Squamous, TPS 0–49%:** pembrolizumab/carboplatin/(nab-)paclitaxel or chemotherapy.

The recommendations are conditional, depending on histology, PD-L1 status and contraindications to immunotherapy. A separate update for driver-positive disease was announced. The abstract does not give overall survival or toxicity data. Applicability in Norway depends on Nye Metoder decisions for each regimen, which the guideline does not address.
Hanna NH, Schneider BJ, Temin S et al. *J Clin Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31990617/ (doi:10.1200/JCO.19.03022)

### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen
- **Nazartinib (EGF816) phase 1 in EGFR-mutant NSCLC.** This was the dose-escalation part of a phase 1/2 study, with 180 patients treated at seven dose levels between 75 and 350 mg. The maximum tolerated dose was not reached, and the recommended phase 2 dose is 150 mg once daily. Rash occurred in 62% (mostly maculopapular and low grade), and any-cause grade 3–4 adverse events in 55%. It adds a third-generation TKI with a distinct skin-toxicity profile, but efficacy is uncontrolled and the comparison with osimertinib is unknown. Tan DS, Leighl NB, Riely GJ et al. *Lancet Respir Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31954624/
- **Afatinib for uncommon EGFR mutations: a database of 693 cases.** This pooled analysis drew on trials, compassionate-use programmes and case series. In TKI-naive patients (n=315):
  - major uncommon mutations (G719X/L861Q/S768I): ORR 60.0%, median TTF 10.8 months (95% CI 8.1–16.6)
  - compound mutations: ORR 77.1%, median TTF 14.7 months
  - exon 20 insertions: ORR 24.3%, median TTF 4.2 months

  It supports afatinib for major uncommon and compound mutations. The data are heterogeneous, mostly non-randomised and partly retrospective. Yang JC, Schuler M, Popat S et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31931137/
- **Resistance to first-line osimertinib (tissue-based).** In 62 MSK patients with paired tissue NGS, histologic transformation (mainly squamous) was found in 15% of first-line cases. Off-target alterations (MET amplification, KRAS, RET/BRAF fusions) were found in 19% (5/27), and an acquired EGFR mutation in only 4%. Transformation was associated with shorter time on osimertinib and shorter survival. This supports re-biopsy at progression rather than plasma testing alone. It is a small single-centre series. Schoenfeld AJ, Chan JM, Kubota D et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31911548/
- **Rapid non-uniform adaptation to KRAS G12C inhibition.** Single-cell work showed that some cells escape G12C inhibitors by making new KRAS G12C, which EGFR and aurora kinase signalling keep in the active, drug-insensitive state. Blocking these pathways restored sensitivity. This is the mechanistic rationale for KRAS G12C plus EGFR or aurora kinase combinations. The evidence is preclinical only. Xue JY, Zhao Y, Aronowitz J et al. *Nature*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31915379/
- **RET solvent-front mutations mediate resistance to selpercatinib.** Mutations at RET G810 (G810R/S/C) emerged in ctDNA and tissue of patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC and RET-mutant MTC who progressed on selpercatinib, and were confirmed in a PDX model. This is the first recurrent on-target resistance mechanism to selective RET inhibition and points to the need for next-generation RET inhibitors. It is based on a few patients. Solomon BJ, Tan L, Lin JJ et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31988000/

### nsclc-drivers / lung-top-journals
- **Crizotinib in MET exon 14-altered NSCLC (PROFILE 1001 expansion).** In 69 patients, ORR was 32% (95% CI 21–45) among the 65 evaluable. Median duration of response was 9.1 months and median PFS 7.3 months (95% CI 5.4–9.1). Responses did not depend on the type of splice-site alteration or on concurrent MET copy number. It is single-arm, with modest durability compared with selective MET inhibitors. Drilon A, Clark JW, Weiss J et al. *Nat Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31932802/
- **Vemurafenib in BRAF V600 and non-V600 NSCLC (AcSé).** This was the NSCLC cohort of a French basket phase 2 (n=118). In BRAF V600 patients, 43/96 responded (Bayesian ORR estimate 44.9%, 95% CI 35.2–54.8), with median PFS 5.2 months and OS 10 months. No responses were seen with non-V600 mutations (n=17), and that cohort was stopped. The trial confirms that only V600 mutations respond. Vemurafenib monotherapy is inferior to the dabrafenib-trametinib standard. Mazieres J, Cropet C, Montané L et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31959346/

### lung-key-evidence / sclc
- **IMpower133 safety and patient-reported outcomes.** This is a secondary analysis of the phase 1/3 trial in ES-SCLC (394 patients assessable for safety). Overall and grade 3–4 adverse events were similar between arms. Immune-related adverse events were more frequent with atezolizumab (induction 28% vs 17%), most often rash and hypothyroidism. HRQoL improved more, and for longer, with atezolizumab. It supports the benefit-risk profile of the regimen. As an open-label PRO analysis it is prone to missing-data bias. Mansfield AS, Każarnowicz A, Karaseva N et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31959349/
- **preOVNI: preoperative non-invasive ventilation before lung cancer surgery.** In this multicentre RCT (n=300) in patients with cardiac or respiratory comorbidity, at least 7 days of NIV did not reduce postoperative complications (42.6% vs 44.8%; P = .75). Pneumonia was numerically lower with NIV (28.0% vs 37.7%; P = .08). This is a negative perioperative trial, and the trial was open-label. Paleiron N, Grassin F, Lancelin C et al. *J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31924365/
- **Pan-Asian adapted ESMO guidelines for locally advanced unresectable NSCLC.** This is the KSMO-ESMO consensus adapting the ESMO 2017 guidelines for Asian patients, endorsed by CSCO, ISMPO, JSMO, MOS, SSO and TOS. The abstract does not specify which recommendations changed, so it is rated high rather than practice-changing. Park K, Vansteenkiste J, Lee KH et al. *Ann Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31959336/
- **AIOM 2019 Italian guidelines for advanced NSCLC.** This is a national guideline summary of recommendations. The abstract states no specific change in recommendation. Passiglia F, Pilotto S, Facchinetti F et al. *Crit Rev Oncol Hematol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31918343/

### nsclc-immunotherapy
- **Immunotherapy retreatment after nivolumab: French national database.** Of 10,452 patients starting nivolumab in 2015–2016, 1,517 received a second course of PD-1 inhibitor. In 1,127 this was resumption after a treatment-free interval (median OS 15.0 months after discontinuation). In 390 it was rechallenge after intervening chemotherapy (median OS 18.4 months). OS was longer when the first course lasted 3 months or more. These are real-world data on retreatment, but the groups are strongly selected (immortal-time and selection bias), so no causal benefit can be inferred. Giaj Levra M, Cotté FE, Corre R et al. *Lung Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31911324/

### car-t-solid
- **CARVac: an RNA vaccine drives claudin-6 CAR-T expansion.** A nanoparticle RNA vaccine delivers the CAR antigen (CLDN6) to lymphoid antigen-presenting cells, expanding the transferred CAR-T cells. This improved engraftment and led to regression of large tumours in mice at subtherapeutic CAR-T doses. It introduces CLDN6 as a solid-tumour CAR target and a vaccine-boost strategy. The evidence is preclinical. Reinhard K, Rengstl B, Oehm P et al. *Science*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31896660/
- **GPC3 CAR-T cells armoured with IL-15 and IL-21 in HCC.** A 4-1BB GPC3 CAR co-expressing IL-15 and IL-21 had superior expansion, persistence and antitumour activity in xenografts, linked to TCF-1-dependent stem-like memory. The data are preclinical and support clinical evaluation. Batra SA, Rathi P, Guo L et al. *Cancer Immunol Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31953246/

### thoracic-other
- **IMRT plus etoposide/cisplatin for unresectable thymic epithelial tumours (prospective phase 2).** The trial enrolled 56 patients: 22 thymomas, 34 thymic carcinomas, 75% stage IVB. ORR was 85.7% (95% CI 76.3–95.2). At a median follow-up of 46 months, 5-year PFS was 29.5% and 5-year OS 56.2%. Grade 3–4 leukopenia occurred in 42.9% and pulmonary fibrosis in 5.3%. It is single-arm and single-country, with mixed histologies. Fan XW, Yang Y, Wang HB et al. *Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31987968/
- **AZD4547 (FGFR1–3 inhibitor) in relapsed mesothelioma (phase 2).** Only 3 of 24 patients (12%) were progression-free at 6 months, so the trial met its stopping criteria. BAP1 loss was not associated with outcome. This is a negative single-centre trial. Lam WS, Creaney J, Chen FK et al. *Lung Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31901768/
