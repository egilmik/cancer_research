## High

### lung-key-evidence / nsclc-immunotherapy (trials)

- **DOMAJOR (randomised phase III, n=292, first-line non-squamous NSCLC).** Prolgolimab, an Fc-silenced anti-PD-1, plus pemetrexed-platinum vs placebo plus pemetrexed-platinum. At a median follow-up of 18 months, median OS was not reached vs 14.6 months (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.35–0.73) and PFS was 7.7 vs 5.5 months (HR 0.65), with benefit regardless of PD-L1. The trial is positive but against a comparator that is no longer standard. Prolgolimab is a Russian product without EMA approval, and follow-up is short. Laktionov K, Smolin A, Stroyakovskiy D et al. Eur J Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39879779/
- **SQUINT (randomised, non-comparative phase II, squamous NSCLC, n=91, Italy).** One-year OS was 62% with nivolumab-ipilimumab and 50% with nivolumab-chemotherapy. In 74 patients with biomarker data, MAPK-pathway-activating alterations were linked to higher ICI response (43% vs 15%) and longer PFS and OS. These alterations had no prognostic value in an ICI-naive validation cohort. The trial is small and non-comparative, and the biomarker finding needs validation. Cappuzzo F, Ricciuti B, Delmonte A et al. Clin Cancer Res 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39836372/
- **PD-L1-guided anti-PD-1 induction then surgery for unresectable stage III NSCLC (single-centre phase II umbrella, n=100, China).** Arms were anti-PD-1 alone, anti-PD-1 plus anti-angiogenic, or anti-PD-1 plus chemotherapy. MPR was 61.7% among the 47 who had surgery (primary endpoint met), ORR 54.0% and median EFS 29.9 months. The trial is single-centre and single-arm per stratum, and fewer than half went to surgery. Yi C, Bian D, Wang J et al. Nat Commun 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39994201/
- **Induction toripalimab-chemotherapy for initially unresectable stage IIIA-IIIB NSCLC (phase 2, n=40).** 72.5% had surgery, all R0, with MPR 58.6% and pCR 34.5%. Median EFS and OS were not reached at 31.8 months. Baseline TLS signatures were linked to pCR. The trial is small and single-arm, and patient selection drives the resectability results. Zeng L, Yan H, Jiang W et al. Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39892382/
- **Perioperative pembrolizumab with standard adjuvant chemotherapy, stage IB–IIIA (single-arm phase II, n=30).** Two neoadjuvant doses, then surgery, adjuvant chemotherapy and four adjuvant doses. 25 of 30 were resected, MPR was 28% (pCR 2 patients), 5-year PFS 72.0% and 5-year OS 75.8% at 59 months' median follow-up. The trial is very small, and the PCSK9 biomarker finding is exploratory. Wood C, Lyniv L, Isaacs JM et al. J Immunother Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39904561/

### Guidelines and consensus statements

- **NCCN Guidelines Insights: Lung Cancer Screening v1.2025.** It summarises recent updates on eligibility and nodule management. The abstract does not say which recommendations changed. Wood DE, Kazerooni EA, Aberle DR et al. J Natl Compr Canc Netw 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39819601/
- **CAR/CSTR practice guideline on CT screening (Canada, update of 2016).** It draws on NELSON and provincial programmes. It recommends LDCT screening with eligibility set by risk models such as PLCOm2012, with training, standardised protocols, QA and AI for nodule risk, and it flags biennial screening as an open question. It is an imaging-society guideline, and the record does not say how eligibility differs from 2016. Taylor JL, Adams SJ, Dennie C et al. Can Assoc Radiol J 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40016863/
- **CSCO 2024 NSCLC guideline updates (two editorials: early/locally advanced and advanced disease).** These records have no abstract, so the changed recommendations are not stated. Chen S, Wang Z, Sun B. Cancer Biol Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39995198/ ; Wang J, Qiu T, Ren S. Cancer Biol Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39995197/
- **Chinese expert consensus on immunotherapy for SCLC (2025 edition, CSCO SCLC committee).** It is based on domestic and international guidelines and evidence, with several ICIs approved in China. The English abstract gives no specific recommendations. Zhonghua Zhong Liu Za Zhi 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39828584/
- **ATORG expert consensus on perioperative management of NSCLC (Asia-Pacific).** 19 experts from 11 countries cover diagnostic testing through post-neoadjuvant decisions. It is expert opinion tailored to Asian health systems. Saw SPL, Zhong WZ, Fu R et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39799810/
- **STS clinical practice guideline: surgical management of oligometastatic NSCLC.** The panel addressed 7 areas of controversy and found strong support for resection of the primary as local consolidative therapy in stage IV disease. The extent of resection and nodal dissection remain in equipoise. Antonoff MB, Mitchell KG, Kim SS et al. Ann Thorac Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39797869/
- **ThORN consensus defining a high-quality wedge resection for early-stage lung cancer.** No abstract. It is relevant as sublobar resection spreads. Krantz SB, Mitzman B, Antonoff MB et al. Ann Thorac Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39793687/
- **Chilean expert consensus (RAND/UCLA Delphi, 18 experts): 25 recommendations for a first lung cancer screening programme.** It covers target population, frequency and implementation, and flags feasibility, awareness and cost barriers. Suárez V F, Saavedra V MP, Roberti J et al. Rev Med Chil 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39760579/
- **ASCO guideline update: treatment of pleural mesothelioma.** It is built on 110 additional studies from 2016–2024 and gives recommendations on surgical cytoreduction, immunotherapy, chemotherapy, pathology and germline testing. The abstract does not say which recommendations changed. Kindler HL, Ismaila N, Bazhenova L et al. J Clin Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39778125/

### nsclc-drivers

- **Brigatinib first line, integrated ALTA-1L/J-ALTA analysis (n=169).** Median PFS was 29.3 months by independent review, ORR 79% and intracranial ORR 66% (70% with measurable brain metastases), with 3-year OS 74%. Grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 74% (CPK, hypertension, lipase). This is a pooled analysis of a phase 3 arm and a Japanese phase 2, not a new comparison. Camidge DR, Sugawara S, Kondo M et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39923717/
- **LIBRETTO-321 final update (phase II, Chinese RET-fusion NSCLC, n=47).** Selpercatinib gave ORR 72.3% (100% treatment-naive, 63.9% pretreated), median PFS 27.6 months, 3-year OS 62% and intracranial response in 4 of 5. This is a small bridging study that is consistent with LIBRETTO-001. Lu S, Cheng Y, Huang D et al. Ther Adv Med Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39759832/
- **HA121-28, a RET multikinase inhibitor (phase 1/2, 162 + 48 patients).** In 97 RET-fusion NSCLC patients dosed at ≥450 mg, ORR was 26.8% and median PFS 5.5 months. Grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 62.5% in phase 2, with QTc prolongation a concern. Efficacy is clearly below that of selective RET inhibitors. Ruan DY, Huang WW, Li Y et al. Signal Transduct Target Ther 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40016191/
- **Dabrafenib-trametinib in Chinese BRAF V600E NSCLC (single-arm phase II bridging study, n=20).** ORR was 75% (95% CI 50.9–91.3), but follow-up was only 5 months, and the results are consistent with the global study. Fan Y, Zhou J, Zhao Y et al. Transl Lung Cancer Res 2024. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39830765/
- **Pooled sotorasib safety (CodeBreaK 100/101/105/200, n=549).** Grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 26.8%, mainly diarrhoea and transaminase rises, and over 90% of diarrhoea resolved with dose interruption or reduction. Treatment-related hepatotoxicity was 33.3% when immunotherapy had stopped less than 3 months before sotorasib vs 13.8% with a longer gap. Pneumonitis occurred in 1.6%. It gives practical management advice, and the sequencing signal is observational. Skoulidis F, Li BT, Hochmair M et al. Oncologist 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39846981/
- **Ensartinib (Ensacove) and zenocutuzumab (Bizengri): Medical Letter drug reviews after their US approvals.** Ensartinib is for ALK+ NSCLC; zenocutuzumab is for NRG1-fusion NSCLC and pancreatic cancer. These are journal profiles without abstracts, not the regulatory decisions. Med Lett Drugs Ther 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39946703/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39819990/

### sclc

- **Socazolimab plus carboplatin-etoposide in first-line ES-SCLC (randomised, double-blind phase 3, China, n=498).** Median OS was 13.90 vs 11.58 months (HR 0.799, 95% CI 0.652–0.979) and PFS 5.55 vs 4.37 months (HR 0.569). Grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 80.3% vs 75.7%. The OS gain is modest, the trial was in China only, and the comparator (chemotherapy alone) is no longer standard. Chen Z, Chen J, Huang D et al. Signal Transduct Target Ther 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39800716/

### lung-local-therapy / lung-nordic

- **REPAINT (randomised, n=50, LA-NSCLC).** PET-adjusted, de-intensified dose-painted RT (55 Gy to large lesions, 44–48 Gy to others, 20 fractions) vs standard 60 Gy/30, both with concurrent chemotherapy. PFS and OS were similar (median 18 and 42 months overall), and grade 3–4 lymphopenia was lower (48% vs 81%). The primary endpoint was PRO toxicity, so the trial is too small to establish non-inferiority. Ohri N, Bodner WR, Kabarriti R et al. Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39862898/
- **Nab-paclitaxel vs solvent-based paclitaxel with carboplatin and thoracic RT (randomised phase 1/2, 92 in phase 2, stage III NSCLC).** Nab-paclitaxel gave more grade ≥3 AEs (56% vs 30%), fewer completing consolidation (26% vs 58%), and numerically lower 2-year OS (56% vs 67%) and PFS (27% vs 44%). This is a negative signal for nab-paclitaxel in this setting, from a pre-durvalumab-era design. Zhang Y, Laine AM, Iyengar P et al. Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39922319/
- **CURE-OLIGO / TORG1529 (single-arm phase II, n=19, synchronous oligometastatic NSCLC).** Thoracic chemoradiotherapy plus LCT to all oligometastases gave 2-year OS 68.4% (80% CI 52.6–79.9), median PFS 8.6 months and median OS 42.1 months. 74% progressed with new lesions. It is very small and pre-immunotherapy. Tokito T, Yamada K, Ishii H et al. Radiat Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39755666/
- **Danish RCT of VATS vs thoracotomy lobectomy, long-term survival (n=206 randomised, 192 analysed).** At a median follow-up of 12.8 years, OS, DFS and cancer-specific survival did not differ. The trial was not powered for survival, and the authors suggest pooling with similar trials. It is reassuring Nordic evidence that VATS does not compromise survival. Dittberner FA, Bendixen M, Licht PB. Eur J Cardiothorac Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39873729/
- **Norwegian registry emulation of KEYNOTE-042 (first-line pembrolizumab vs chemotherapy, PD-L1 ≥50%).** Norwegian health registries were used to emulate the trial's inclusion criteria, and an observational intention-to-treat analogue gave results similar to KEYNOTE-042. It is direct Norwegian evidence that trial benefit carries over to practice, but it is observational and its numbers are not in the abstract. Brant SB, Børø S, Jonasson C et al. Clin Pharmacol Ther 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39916352/

### lung-screening / lung-ctdna-mrd

- **ECLS 5-year mortality (RCT, n=12,208, Scotland).** Using the EarlyCDT-Lung autoantibody test to select high-risk people for LDCT vs usual care gave lung cancer deaths of 73 vs 90 (adjusted HR 0.789, 95% CI 0.636–0.978). For cancers diagnosed within 2 years, lung cancer mortality HR was 0.598. This is a secondary, registry-ascertained analysis of a trial whose primary endpoint was stage at diagnosis, and the absolute difference is small. Sullivan FM, Mair FS, Anderson W et al. PLoS One 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39774508/
- **Never-smoker LDCT screening in Korea (multicentre cohort, n=21,062, 2009–2021).** 0.8–0.9% had screen-detected cancer at baseline, over 89% at stage 0–I and nearly all adenocarcinoma. There were 11 lung cancer deaths over a mean of 7 years, with no sex differences. There is no unscreened comparator, so overdiagnosis cannot be separated from benefit. Kim YW, Joo DH, Kim SY et al. JAMA Netw Open 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39813033/
- **BMJ analysis: screening never-smokers, lessons from East Asia.** The authors argue that high detection and survival among screened never-smokers in East Asia are more likely signs of overdiagnosis than of benefit. It is opinion and analysis, but directly relevant to proposals to screen never-smokers. Welch HG, Gao W, Wilder FG et al. BMJ 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39914848/
- **PM2.5 and lung cancer in never-smokers (Chinese prospective cohort).** Lung cancer incidence HR was 1.10 and mortality HR 1.17 per 10 μg/m³. High exposure combined with high polygenic risk gave incidence HR 2.01, with an additive interaction. An estimated 33.6% of never-smoker cases could be avoided at WHO guideline levels. It is observational, and exposure is modelled. Zhu M, Han Y, Mou Y et al. Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39918842/
- **Integrated tobacco treatment in the screening setting (RCT, n=630, Houston).** At 6 months, self-reported abstinence was 32.4% with integrated care vs 20.5% with quitline referral plus NRT (OR 1.86) and 27.6% with quitline-plus. The primary endpoint was switched from biochemical verification to self-report during COVID, and the trial was single-centre. Cinciripini PM, Minnix JA, Kypriotakis G et al. JAMA Intern Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39804633/
- **Postoperative MRD, updated prospective cohort (n=261, resected stage I–III NSCLC, median follow-up 43.4 months).** Longitudinal MRD had PPV 92.8% and NPV 93.2%, with a median lead time of 5.2 months. Landmark MRD-negative patients did not benefit from adjuvant therapy (P=0.529), and half of recurrences in persistently MRD-negative patients were brain-only. It is observational, and the adjuvant comparison is not randomised. Zhang JT, Liu SY, Gao X et al. Clin Cancer Res 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39853318/

### lung-repurposed

- **Corticosteroids and first-line pembrolizumab-chemotherapy (multicentre French cohort, n=193).** Steroids at ≥10 mg/day were not linked to PFS overall (HR 1.25, 95% CI 0.77–2.03), but each 10 mg/day increment was (HR 1.08), especially at ≥60 mg/day. Steroids started ≥4 weeks before therapy were also linked to shorter PFS. 58% received steroids for brain metastases, so confounding by indication is likely. Roboubi A, Wasielewski E, Bordier S et al. Ther Adv Med Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39935765/
- **Antipsychotics during chemoradiotherapy (Korean nationwide cohort, n=23,099).** 11.5% used antipsychotics, mostly quetiapine and chlorpromazine. Use was linked to worse 2-year OS (20.4% vs 36.4%) and remained significant in multivariable analysis, with no dose or timing gradient. Antipsychotic use is likely a marker of frailty, delirium or end of life, so causal inference is not possible. Kim DY, Hwang IG, Kim SM et al. Cancer Res Treat 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39930853/

### thoracic-other

- **BEAT-meso / ETOP 13-18 (randomised phase III, n=400, first-line pleural mesothelioma).** Adding atezolizumab to bevacizumab plus carboplatin-pemetrexed gave median OS 20.5 vs 18.1 months (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.66–1.06, P=0.14), so the primary endpoint was not met. PFS was 9.2 vs 7.6 months (HR 0.72). In non-epithelioid disease OS HR was 0.51 (interaction P=0.012). Grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 55% vs 47%. It does not displace ipilimumab-nivolumab, and the non-epithelioid signal is a subgroup finding. Felip E, Popat S, Dafni U et al. Ann Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39814199/
- **Atezolizumab in pretreated advanced thymic carcinoma (single-arm phase 2, China, n=34).** ORR was 14.7%, DCR 58.8%, median PFS 3.2 months and 24-month OS 63.2%. ORR was 40% when PD-L1-positive vs 0% when negative, and immune-related AEs occurred in 29.4%. Activity is modest, and the PD-L1 subgroup is small. Lu S, Ma X, Liu L et al. Clin Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39986946/
