### Guidelines and consensus statements

- **TSANZ practical guide to managing screen-detected and incidental pulmonary nodules (Australia/New Zealand).** It covers risk stratification by size and morphology, diagnostic work-up, follow-up, and dedicated nodule evaluation teams and MDMs, with Australia's national screening programme starting in July 2025. It is a practical guide, and the abstract does not single out changed recommendations. Brims F, McWilliams A, Williamson J et al. Respirology 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40438947/
- **ACR Appropriateness Criteria: imaging surveillance after lung cancer therapy.** Evidence-based imaging recommendations for routine surveillance after treatment of stage I-III NSCLC and SCLC, and for suspected recurrence. Annual update; the abstract does not say which ratings changed. Expert Panel on Thoracic Imaging, Madan R, El Alam RH et al. J Am Coll Radiol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409885/
- **Consensus guideline for management of peritoneal mesothelioma (modified Delphi).** About 100 experts reached over 90% consensus across six management pathway blocks over two rounds. It is the first consensus pathway for this rare disease, but consensus-level evidence. Brown LM, Wilkins SG, Bansal VV et al. Ann Surg Oncol 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40560500/
- **Commentary on the US national consensus guideline for peritoneal mesothelioma (Cancer).** A companion summary of the Delphi guideline above, setting out its rationale and limitations. Brown LM, Wilkins SG, Bansal VV et al. Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40558081/
- **NCCN Guidelines: thymomas and thymic carcinomas, version 2.2025.** Diagnosis, treatment and surveillance recommendations stressing multidisciplinary care. The published abstract does not list which recommendations changed in this version, so it is rated high, not practice-changing. Riely GJ, Wood DE, Loo BW et al. J Natl Compr Canc Netw 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40499586/

### lung-key-evidence (trials and pooled analyses)

- **Time to benefit for LDCT screening (pooled analysis of 4 trials, n=64,105, Chest).** In NLST, preventing one lung cancer death per 1,000 screened took 2.87 years, and per 200 screened 8.87 years. It supports limiting screening to people with enough life expectancy. Time-to-benefit modelling depends on trial populations. Xie Y, Zhang Y, Zhang P et al. Chest 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40541736/
- **CALGB 140503 (Alliance): extent of node dissection and DFS (secondary analysis of the randomised lobar vs sublobar trial).** 5-year DFS was 62.3% after complete dissection, 65.7% after systematic sampling and 61.2% after simple sampling, with no difference after lobar or sublobar resection. It suggests less extensive nodal evaluation is acceptable in small peripheral cN0 tumours, but node strategy was not randomised. Altorki N, Damman B, Wang X et al. J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40532782/
- **Time to benefit for lung cancer screening (survival meta-analysis of 8 RCTs, n=88,526).** It took 3.4 years (95% CI 2.2-5.1) to prevent one lung cancer death per 1,000 screened. The authors conclude screening suits people with life expectancy over 3.4 years. A complementary estimate to the Chest analysis above; both rest on heterogeneous trials. Kim EE, Cenzer I, Graham FJ et al. Am J Prev Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40447235/
- **Finotonlimab + docetaxel vs placebo + docetaxel in previously treated squamous NSCLC (randomised double-blind phase 3, n=188, China).** OS improved (17.1 vs 10.4 months, HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.45-0.96), as did PFS (4.2 vs 2.9 months) and ORR (27.0% vs 3.2%). The trial is positive, but docetaxel alone is no longer the second-line standard for patients who have not had immunotherapy, and the trial is small, so it is rated high. Han B, Wu L, Yang R et al. Transl Lung Cancer Res 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40386729/
- **Anlotinib + docetaxel vs docetaxel after platinum (randomised phase 2 part of a phase 1/2, n=88).** PFS improved (5.39 vs 2.56 months, HR 0.36, 95% CI 0.21-0.63), but OS did not (16.8 vs 9.9 months, HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.47-1.66). Small and not tested after chemo-immunotherapy. Shou J, Chen J, Guo Q et al. Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40366884/
- **EMPOWER-Lung 1: cemiplimab vs chemotherapy in PD-L1 ≥50% NSCLC with brain metastases (post-hoc subgroup).** Median OS was 52.4 vs 20.7 months (HR 0.40) and PFS 12.5 vs 5.3 months (HR 0.33) in patients with treated, stable brain metastases. Supports single-agent PD-1 in this subgroup, but post-hoc and small. Kilickap S, Özgüroğlu M, Sezer A et al. Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40323717/

### nsclc-drivers

- **TUXEDO-3 cohort 3: patritumab deruxtecan for leptomeningeal metastatic disease from solid tumours (phase 2, n=20, Nat Med).** 3-month survival was 65% (primary endpoint met), with 6-month OS 58.9% and intracranial ORR 11.1%. Only 30% had lung primaries, and numbers are very small. Preusser M, Garde-Noguera J, García-Mosquera JJ et al. Nat Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40447851/
- **ALEK-B: alectinib + bevacizumab first line in ALK+ NSCLC (single-arm phase 2, n=41, Mexico).** 12-month PFS was 97.1%, 36-month PFS 64.2% and OS 87.9%, with grade 3-4 AEs in 46.3% (proteinuria, hepatotoxicity). Single-centre with no comparator; needs a randomised test against alectinib alone. Arrieta O, Lara-Mejía L, Rios-Garcia E et al. Nat Commun 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40379690/
- **LIBRETTO-431 patient-reported outcomes: first-line selpercatinib vs chemotherapy ± pembrolizumab in RET fusion-positive NSCLC.** Selpercatinib delayed deterioration of all lung cancer symptoms and of physical (HR 0.54) and role functioning (HR 0.59). Secondary endpoints of an open-label phase 3. Zhou C, Novello S, Garrido P et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40567260/

### sclc

- **Turtle (LOGiK 2003): durvalumab + carboplatin-etoposide in elderly ES-SCLC (phase 2, n=40).** ORR was 89.5%, median PFS 5.4 and OS 16.1 months, with grade 3+ AEs in 94.6% (neutropenia 76.3%, febrile neutropenia 15.8%) and quality of life maintained. Single-arm, but supports chemo-immunotherapy in fit older patients. Ishii H, Azuma K, Yamanaka Y et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40534995/
- **ECOG-ACRIN EA5161: platinum-etoposide with or without nivolumab first line in ES-SCLC (randomised phase 2, n=160).** PFS (HR 0.78, p=0.083) and OS (11.2 vs 8.1 months, HR 0.71, p=0.059) favoured nivolumab, with no new safety signals. It is consistent with the PD-(L)1 benefit established in phase 3 trials, but it is a phase 2. Leal TA, Wang Y, Dowlati A et al. Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40526078/
- **ASTRUM-005 updated analysis: serplulimab + chemotherapy first line in ES-SCLC (phase 3).** Median OS was 15.8 vs 11.1 months (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.50-0.76) vs placebo + chemotherapy, and an exploratory 15-protein signature and RB1/Notch mutations were linked to benefit. The comparator is no longer standard now that PD-(L)1 chemo-immunotherapy is the norm, and serplulimab is not approved in Norway. Cheng Y, Zhang S, Han L et al. Cancer Commun (Lond) 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40440184/
- **CeLEBrATE (GOIRC-01-2019): carboplatin-etoposide + atezolizumab + bevacizumab first line in ES-SCLC (phase 2, n=53).** 1-year OS was 61.8% (met endpoint), median OS 12.9 and PFS 6.2 months, ORR 83.3%, with grade 3-4 AEs in 64.2%. Single-arm; adding bevacizumab has not shown OS benefit in randomised SCLC trials. Lamberti G, Rihawi K, Mazzoni F et al. J Immunother Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40341031/
- **Anlotinib + irinotecan second line in ES-SCLC relapsing within 6 months (single-arm phase 2, n=37).** ORR 62.2%, median PFS 4.5 and OS 7.2 months. Small, single-arm, and anlotinib is mainly available in China. Zhang M, Tang Y, Liang J et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40555062/
- **News highlight: DeLLphi-304 shows second-line tarlatamab improves OS vs chemotherapy in SCLC (Nat Rev Clin Oncol).** A news summary of the phase 3 trial, not the primary report; the trial publication itself is the item that would qualify as practice-changing. Killock D. Nat Rev Clin Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40528046/
- **News highlight: IMforte shows adding lurbinectedin to atezolizumab maintenance improves outcomes in ES-SCLC (Nat Rev Clin Oncol).** A summary of the phase 3 trial, not the primary report. Romero D. Nat Rev Clin Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40506590/
- **Review of the regulatory path to tarlatamab approval.** Describes the FDA accelerated approval of 16 May 2024 for ES-SCLC progressing after platinum, based on DeLLphi-301, and the global regulatory strategy. A retrospective review, not a new decision. Pathak V, Pimentel CA, Cooney E et al. Ther Innov Regul Sci 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40419854/

### nsclc-immunotherapy

- **NeoTAP01 4-year outcomes: perioperative toripalimab + chemotherapy in resectable stage III NSCLC (single-arm phase 2).** 4-year EFS was 66.7% and OS 83.3% per protocol, with better EFS after pCR (92.9% vs 46.7%) and grade 3 pneumonitis in 11.1%. Small single-arm study supporting the randomised Neotorch results. Zhou Y, Zhao Z, Zhai W et al. J Thorac Dis 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40529778/
- **News highlight: CheckMate 816 final OS with neoadjuvant nivolumab + chemotherapy (Nat Rev Clin Oncol).** Summary of the OS result of the phase 3 trial; not the primary report. Sidaway P. Nat Rev Clin Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40506587/

### lung-nonpharm / lung-toxicity

- **Long-term safety of tislelizumab (pooled trial analysis, n=2,636, of whom 1,221 had lung cancer).** In lung cancer, any-grade immune-mediated AEs occurred in 39.6% and grade 3+ in 8.2%, mostly skin, thyroid and pneumonitis, with slightly more in Asian patients. Median follow-up 15.4 months, no comparator arm. Zhou C, Huang R, Yuan Y et al. Oncologist 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40578840/
- **Gegen Qinlian tablets to prevent irAEs in NSCLC on ICIs (multicentre open-label RCT, n=94, China).** irAEs occurred in 31.9% vs 59.6% (p=0.007), and ORR was not reduced (48.9% vs 36.2%). The trial is small and open-label, uses a traditional Chinese formulation, and needs independent replication. Lu X, Wang A, Chen D et al. Phytomedicine 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40561860/
- **NEXTAC-TWO: nutrition and exercise for elderly patients with NSCLC or pancreatic cancer on chemotherapy (randomised phase 2, n=131, Japan).** Adherence was high (98.4%), but median disability-free survival did not improve (478 vs 499 days). A negative trial for this multimodal programme in older patients. Mitsunaga S, Naito T, Imai H et al. J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40522817/
- **Pulmonary rehabilitation during thoracic radiotherapy (RCT, Korea).** 6MWT improved in the rehabilitation arm (+16.9 vs −26.1 m at end of RT, with gains to 7 months), and steroid-requiring radiation pneumonitis fell from 54.5% to 9.4%. The pneumonitis effect is striking but comes from a small single-country trial. Lee JH, Cha S, Ko EJ et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40306134/
- **Safety of durvalumab vs durvalumab + tremelimumab across trials (pooled analysis, J Immunother Cancer).** Grade 3-4 treatment-related AEs were 22.1% vs 11.5% and grade 3-4 immune-mediated AEs 11.0% vs 4.3% for the combination vs monotherapy, mainly from colitis and rash. Fatal imAEs were rare in both (0.4% vs 0.3%). The comparison is across trials, not randomised. Morgan C, Kurland J, Patel M et al. J Immunother Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40447320/

### lung-screening

- **Finnish randomised lung cancer screening pilot (Acta Oncol).** Uptake of LDCT was 96.7% over two rounds, six cancers were found (five stage I, PPV 75%), and a smoking-cessation app gave 18.3% vs 12.8% quit rates at 1 year (OR 1.53, 95% CI 0.69-3.41). A small feasibility trial bearing on Nordic screening decisions. Wichmann V, Iivanainen S, Mattila L et al. Acta Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40497290/
- **Lithuanian national lung screening programme pilot (n=1,014).** Lithuania screens everyone aged 50-70 every 3 years regardless of smoking history. Uptake was 76.1%, Lung-RADS 4 nodules were found in 1.4% (8 of 14 in non-smokers), and significant incidental findings in 25.9%. A notable European policy that includes never-smokers, but these are early pilot data with no outcomes. Danila E, Krynke L, Ciesiūnienė A et al. Cancers (Basel) 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40563607/
- **Inflammatory diseases and lung cancer risk in never-smokers (nested case-control, UK CPRD, Nat Commun).** Conditions such as COPD/emphysema, GERD and bronchitis 1-10 years before diagnosis were linked to lung cancer in never-smokers (1,581 cases, validated in 2,188 more). Proposed as markers for never-smoker screening; observational and open to detection bias. D'Arcy ME, Pfeiffer RM, Bradley MC et al. Nat Commun 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40456733/

### lung-ctdna-mrd

- **LIQUIK: combined ctDNA/ctRNA liquid biopsy in metastatic NSCLC (prospective multicentre).** The ctDNA assay found fewer biomarker-positive patients than tissue NGS (48.2% vs 59.6%; non-inferiority not met) but was non-inferior to Guardant360 and turned around faster (9.7 vs 21.7 days). Adding ctRNA increased detection of fusions by 28.6%. Supports plasma-first testing with tissue reflex. Samol J, Ng D, Poh J et al. JCO Precis Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40570258/

### lung-local-therapy

- **PACIFIC-6 final analysis: durvalumab after sequential chemoradiotherapy in stage III NSCLC (phase 2, n=117).** Median OS 39.0 months, 3-year OS 56.5%, median PFS 13.1 months, grade 3/4 pneumonitis 1.7%. Supports durvalumab when concurrent CRT is not possible; single-arm, frail population. Garassino MC, Khalifa J, Reck M et al. ESMO Open 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40435873/
- **ETOP CHESS: systemic + local therapy for synchronous oligometastatic NSCLC (phase 2, n=49).** Durvalumab + chemotherapy, SBRT to metastases and surgery or RT to the primary missed the primary endpoint (1-year PFS in 14 of 42, 33%; 17 required). 1-year OS was 74.9%, and grade 3+ AEs occurred in 34%. A negative signal for this multimodal strategy. Guckenberger M, Opitz I, Dellaporta T et al. Lung Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40311307/

### lung-nordic

- **Second-line atezolizumab vs nivolumab vs pembrolizumab in Norway (registry comparative-effectiveness study, n=447).** No OS differences: atezolizumab vs pembrolizumab HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.65-1.12), atezolizumab vs nivolumab HR 0.97, nivolumab vs pembrolizumab HR 0.99. Supports cost-based choice between agents; observational with short follow-up (8.4 months). Pagui ECK, Brant SB, Svendsen K et al. Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40552731/
- **Danish national MDT consensus meetings for complex lung cancer cases.** Regional MDTs fully agreed on stage, intent and treatment in only 20% of 15 complex cases. Plenary national discussion reduced discrepancies, mainly about multifocal tumours, nodal status and pleural fluid. Health-services study with few cases. Gouliaev A, Szejniuk WM, Fledelius J et al. Acta Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40524342/
- **Lung cancer care and survival across the Nordic countries (Acta Oncol).** Finland's poorer 5-year survival was linked to less CT access from primary care, longer primary-care waits, slower access to novel treatments (e.g. neoadjuvant immunotherapy) and no hospital outcome tracking. Largely qualitative comparison. Khalife G, Waris J, Bødtger U et al. Acta Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40468524/
- **Lymph node sampling and survival after NSCLC resection (Danish 10-year cohort, n=6,615).** After PSM (n=5,670), adherence to NCCN nodal sampling did not affect OS (p=0.31) or nodal upstaging. Observational; the result may reflect high baseline quality. Arnarsson LB, Stenger M. Eur J Cardiothorac Surg 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40341438/
- **NIELS (Nordic trial of inhomogeneous dose-escalated thoracic RT in limited-stage SCLC): design and pre-trial dose-planning QA.** Escalation to a median mean dose of about 80 Gy to the primary was feasible while respecting organ constraints (3 breaches in 35 plans). A Nordic randomised trial in progress; no outcome data yet. Linde S, Møller DS, Linaa MB et al. Radiother Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40409366/

### lung-vaccines-cell

- **UCPVax telomerase-derived CD4 helper peptide vaccine (phase 1/2, 60 patients with advanced lung cancer, Cell Rep Med).** It selectively activated polyfunctional Th1 CD4 cells across HLA-DR types, with antibody responses and epitope spreading associated with antitumour immunity. Immunological analysis; clinical efficacy data are limited. Laheurte C, Boullerot L, Ndao B et al. Cell Rep Med 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40543509/
- **TEIPP24 peptide vaccine in NSCLC progressing on checkpoint blockade (first-in-human phase 1/2, Nat Commun).** A synthetic long peptide targeting TAP-deficiency-associated antigens, given to HLA-A2+ patients in dose escalation with an extension cohort. Early safety and immunogenicity; efficacy unknown. Emmers M, Welters MJP, Dietz MV et al. Nat Commun 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40436854/
- **Resistance to TIL therapy in lung cancer (time-serial analysis of a TIL trial, Nat Cancer).** In non-responders, tumour-reactive clonotypes had a dysfunctional programme and were poorly retained, with loss of neoantigen-specific T cells. Mechanistic insight for TIL development, from a small trial. Wang C, Yu X, Teer JK et al. Nat Cancer 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40341231/

### thoracic-other

- **Maintenance gemcitabine vs supportive care in unresectable pleural mesothelioma (randomised, Egypt).** PFS improved (8.9 vs 5.2 months, p=0.022), but not significantly on multivariate analysis, and OS was not significantly better (16.2 vs 13.4 months, p=0.138). Small trial of chemotherapy maintenance, not tested with first-line immunotherapy. Karam AS, Abdelwahab S, Ezz El Din MMA et al. Future Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40501447/
- **Cytoreductive surgery + hyperthermic intrathoracic chemotherapy for thymic epithelial tumours with pleural spread (prospective phase 2, n=45, Nat Commun).** Grade 3+ treatment-related AEs occurred in 17.8%, and short-term outcomes were reported. Single-arm with short follow-up. Wang S, Yang X, Jiang J et al. Nat Commun 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40467556/
- **Neoadjuvant concurrent chemoradiotherapy for locally advanced high-grade thymic tumours (phase 2, n=33).** ORR was 48.5% (thymomas 63.6%, carcinomas 40.9%), grade 3-4 toxicity 39.4%, and 69.7% went on to surgery, with 82.6% complete resections. Single-arm. Xu N, Wang C, Zhang Q et al. JTO Clin Res Rep 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40458540/
- **Perioperative efgartigimod for thymoma-associated myasthenia gravis (prospective multicentre phase 2, n=40, J Thorac Oncol).** All patients responded on MG scales around thymectomy, with improved ocular, bulbar, limb and respiratory strength. Single-arm; may reduce perioperative myasthenic crisis risk. Wang S, Zhu M, Dong J et al. J Thorac Oncol 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40320172/
