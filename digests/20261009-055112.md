### nsclc-immunotherapy: publications
- **Fianlimab (anti-LAG-3) + cemiplimab: tumour-specific expansion cohorts of the first-in-human phase 1.** Patients got fianlimab 1600 mg plus cemiplimab 350 mg every 3 weeks for up to 24 months. In NSCLC, investigator-assessed ORR was 27% in anti-PD-(L)1-naive patients (four partial responses) and 7% in PD-(L)1-experienced patients (one PR). The commonest treatment-related AEs across cohorts were fatigue (15%), rash (12%) and adrenal insufficiency (10%). The authors call the efficacy modest; the cohorts are small, single-arm and give no PFS data, and this is clinical data for a LAG-3 combination rather than evidence of added benefit over PD-1 alone. Kim TM, Williamson SK, Papadopoulos KP et al. *Cancer* 2026. [PMID 42028885](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42028885/)
- **Corticosteroid peak dose for irAEs and survival in stage IV NSCLC.** The study pooled 419 patients from 17 hospitals in eight countries who got systemic immunosuppression for irAEs during first- or second-line ICI. Each 80 mg rise in peak prednisolone-equivalent dose was linked to worse OS (adjusted HR 1.63, 95% CI 1.27–2.08) and cancer-specific survival (HR 1.54, 1.13–2.08), but not to worse PFS (HR 1.05, 0.75–1.48). Cumulative dose and second-line immunosuppressants (19% of patients) were not linked to survival. This supports using the lowest effective peak steroid dose for irAEs, but it is retrospective, and confounding by irAE severity cannot be excluded. van Dijk EJ, Smeenk MM, Bensch F et al. *ESMO Open* 2026. [PMID 41564819](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41564819/)
- **NRG-LU004: chemotherapy-free durvalumab with accelerated (60 Gy/15 fx) or standard (60 Gy/30 fx) RT, PD-L1 >50% stage II–III NSCLC.** In this phase 1 trial (24 evaluable, 12 per arm), there was one DLT (standard-fractionation arm). Durvalumab feasibility was 85% with accelerated and 75% with standard RT, and each arm had one grade 5 event judged unrelated. The authors conclude that concurrent durvalumab-RT without chemotherapy is safe, which supports a de-escalated alternative for patients unfit for concurrent chemoradiotherapy. It is small and reports no efficacy, so it gives no comparison with chemoradiotherapy followed by durvalumab. Lin SH, Pugh SL, Valle DMD et al. *Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys* 2026. [PMID 42731785](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42731785/)
- **Low-dose SBRT then pembrolizumab-chemotherapy for potentially resectable stage III NSCLC (phase 1b).** 17 patients got 24 Gy (peripheral) or 12 Gy (central) in 3 fractions, then two cycles of pembrolizumab 100 mg plus platinum chemotherapy. There were no DLTs, and ORR was 94.1%. 11 patients (64.7%) had surgery, 10 with R0 resection, and 6 of the 11 had pCR. Grade 3–5 treatment-related AEs occurred in 29.4%, including one fatal pulmonary artery haemorrhage at surgery. Median PFS was 29.0 months and OS 37.9 months at 22.8 months' follow-up. It is a small single-arm study with a non-standard pembrolizumab dose, so the conversion rate needs a randomised test. Zhu X, Zhao L, Zhang S et al. *Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys* 2026. [PMID 42264097](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42264097/)

### lung-ctdna-mrd: publications
- **Landmark ctDNA molecular response predicts immunotherapy outcome in metastatic NSCLC.** In a prospective protocol (NCT05995821), 109 patients on anti-PD-(L)1 alone or with chemotherapy had targeted error-correction sequencing of ctDNA (328 samples) with matched white-cell DNA to remove clonal haematopoiesis variants. Undetectable ctDNA at 3–9 weeks (landmark molecular response) was linked to longer PFS (p=1.6e-06) and OS (p=2.5e-05). A single on-treatment timepoint performed as well as baseline-to-landmark dynamics. Pre-treatment ctDNA burden predicted survival, but blood TMB did not. This supports ctDNA as an early response endpoint, but the cohort is single-protocol and the abstract gives no hazard ratios; the authors note it is not validated for regulatory use. Niknafs N, Sivapalan L, Balan A et al. *Clin Cancer Res* 2026. [PMID 42489696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42489696/)
- **ctDNA tumour fraction to choose ICB alone vs ICB + chemotherapy in advanced NSCLC.** In a US clinicogenomic database (n=965), high ctDNA tumour fraction was prognostic on ICB. At ≥5% it predicted benefit from adding chemotherapy (real-world PFS HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.41–0.82), including in PD-L1 ≥50%. The 5% cut-off was validated in an independent Gustave Roussy cohort, where TF correlated with metabolic tumour volume (rho 0.46, n=283) and with TP53/RB1 mutations. It could help decide when to intensify treatment in PD-L1-high disease, but both cohorts are observational and treatment was not randomised, so a prospective trial is needed. Dall'Olio FG, Zrafi W, Vasseur D et al. *Clin Cancer Res* 2026. [PMID 42440365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42440365/)
- **Tumour-informed vs tumour-naive ctDNA assays head to head, early-stage NSCLC.** The two approaches were run on the same samples and platform: pretreatment plasma of 42 patients plus 50 healthy donors, and 176 postoperative samples from 76 patients. Sensitivity/specificity for ctDNA detection was 66.7%/99.3% tumour-informed and 52.6%/95.7% tumour-naive. Both postoperative calls predicted recurrence (HR >100), with recurrence sensitivity 86.7% vs 80.0%. A fixed 500-hotspot panel rescued cases with poor tissue, and the tumour-naive arm relied on non-mutation genome-wide features. It is useful where tissue is limited, but it is retrospective, single-centre (Vietnam) and has few recurrences. Nguyen Hoang VA, Nguyen N, Nguyen T et al. *JTO Clin Res Rep* 2026. [PMID 41496778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41496778/)
- **RESIDUAL: tissue-free methylation ctDNA (Guardant Reveal) MRD after resection of stage I–III NSCLC.** In this prospective cohort, 48 patients were followed for a median 41.8 months, and 19 (39.6%) relapsed. Pre-surgical and landmark (10 days post-op) ctDNA detection were each linked to worse DFS (p=0.022 and p=0.024). Serial sampling anticipated radiological recurrence by a median 2.6 months. Tumour volume >26,378 mm³ added risk information. However, overall ctDNA detection was only 15.2% across timepoints, the cohort is small and mostly stage I, and the abstract gives no sensitivity for relapse. Verzè M, Minari R, Bordi P et al. *J Liq Biopsy* 2026. [PMID 42828040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42828040/)
- **REMODEL protocol: ctDNA MRD-guided postoperative management, stage IA–IIIA NSCLC.** This 10-centre Chinese prospective study (target n=916, recruiting since April 2025) tests whether ctDNA-guided management is non-inferior to standard care. Patients with undetected MRD 1 month after surgery (plus major pathological response if they had neoadjuvant therapy) may skip adjuvant therapy, and treatment then follows MRD status every 3–6 months for 3 years. Arms are assigned by patient preference, not randomisation, which limits how far a non-inferiority result could be trusted. No results yet. Yuan X, Jin R, Wei Y et al. *J Transl Int Med* 2026. [PMID 42389426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42389426/)
