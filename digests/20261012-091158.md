### egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen / nsclc-drivers

- **EVAN updated OS (randomised phase 2).** 102 patients with resected stage IIIA EGFR+ NSCLC were randomised to adjuvant erlotinib or vinorelbine-cisplatin. Median follow-up was 55–64 months. Median OS was 84.2 vs 61.1 months (HR 0.318, 95% CI 0.151–0.670) and 5-year OS 84.8% vs 51.1%. 5-year DFS with erlotinib was 48.2%. This is a small open-label phase 2 with a first-generation TKI, so it supports adjuvant EGFR-TKI rather than changing today's osimertinib-based standard. Yue D, Xu S, Wang Q et al. J Clin Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36027483/
- **NEJ009 updated analysis (phase 3).** Gefitinib plus carboplatin-pemetrexed kept a PFS2 advantage over gefitinib alone (HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.62–0.97). The OS difference that was significant in the first report is no longer significant (median 49.0 vs 38.5 months, HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.64–1.06, P=0.127). This tempers the case for adding chemotherapy to a first-generation TKI. The comparator predates osimertinib. Miyauchi E, Morita S, Nakamura A et al. J Clin Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35960896/
- **FURLONG CNS analysis (phase 3, secondary).** This analysis covers the 133 of 358 patients with CNS lesions, comparing first-line furmonertinib with gefitinib. CNS PFS was 20.8 vs 9.8 months (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.23–0.71). CNS ORR was 91% vs 65% in the 60 patients with measurable lesions. As with FLAURA, a third-generation TKI beats gefitinib in the brain. The analysis is a subgroup, the trial was China-only, and furmonertinib is not available in Europe. Shi Y, Chen G, Wang X et al. J Thorac Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35932953/
- **ORIENT-31 first interim (phase 3).** 444 Chinese patients with EGFR-mutant non-squamous NSCLC progressing on an EGFR-TKI were randomised 1:1:1 to sintilimab+IBI305 (bevacizumab biosimilar)+cisplatin-pemetrexed, sintilimab+chemo or chemo alone. Median follow-up was 9.8 months. PFS was 6.9 vs 4.3 months for the quadruplet vs chemo (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.34–0.64). Grade 3–4 toxicity was similar across arms, mostly haematological. The sintilimab+chemo comparison and OS are immature, the trial was China-only, and the patients were largely treated before osimertinib. Lu S, Wu L, Jian H et al. Lancet Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35908558/
- **FDA accelerated approval of trastuzumab deruxtecan for HER2-mutant NSCLC (journal news).** Cancer Discovery reports the August 2022 accelerated approval for HER2-mutant NSCLC after platinum-based therapy. This is a news report, not the agency's own announcement, so it is rated high. A regulatory record from the FDA itself would qualify for the practice-changing tier. Cancer Discov 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35984233/
- **PATHER2 (single-arm phase 2).** 33 patients with HER2-mutant or -amplified metastatic NSCLC (48.5% after ≥2 prior lines) received pyrotinib plus apatinib. ORR was 51.5%, median PFS 6.9 months and OS 14.8 months. Grade ≥3 hypertension occurred in 9.1%. The trial is small, single-arm and single-country. Yang G, Xu H, Yang Y et al. BMC Med 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36031613/
- **ALTA-1L final results by ethnicity (phase 3 subgroup).** Brigatinib beat crizotinib on BIRC-assessed PFS in both the 108 Asian patients (HR 0.35, median 24.0 vs 11.1 months) and the non-Asian patients (HR 0.56). Toxicity was similar between the groups. This is a subgroup analysis against crizotinib, which is no longer a preferred first-line ALK TKI. Ahn MJ, Kim HR, Yang JCH et al. Clin Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36038416/
- **J-ALTA TKI-naive cohort (phase 2).** 32 Japanese patients received first-line brigatinib. 12-month PFS was 93.0% and ORR 96.9%. Grade ≥3 AEs occurred in 91% and pneumonitis in 9%. The cohort is small with short follow-up (14 months). Sugawara S, Kondo M, Yokoyama T et al. Int J Clin Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36036294/
- **ETOP ALERT-lung (phase 2, closed early).** Alectinib in 14 patients with pretreated RET-rearranged NSCLC gave no objective responses and a median PFS of 3.7 months. This confirms that alectinib is not a useful RET option now that selective RET inhibitors exist. Felip E, Smit EF, Molina-Vila MA et al. Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36030612/
- **ARROW update, NSCLC (phase 1/2).** 281 patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC received pralsetinib. ORR was 72% treatment-naive (n=75) and 59% after platinum (n=136), with median PFS 13.0 and 16.5 months. Intracranial ORR was 70% (7/10). Neutropenia (18%) and hypertension (10%) were the main grade 3–4 toxicities. The trial is single-arm. Griesinger F, Curigliano G, Thomas M et al. Ann Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35973665/
- **LIBRETTO-321 (phase 2, China).** 47 Chinese patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC received selpercatinib. ORR was 69.2% in the primary analysis set (n=26), and 4 of 5 with measurable CNS disease responded intracranially. Grade ≥3 hypertension occurred in 19.5%. Follow-up is short (9.7 months) and n is small. Lu S, Cheng Y, Huang D et al. Ther Adv Med Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35923928/
- **TQ-B3139 (CT-711) first-in-human (phase 1).** This is a new ALK/ROS1 TKI, tested in 63 patients. In TKI-naive patients at ≥200 mg BID, ORR was 76.7% and median PFS 25.2 months. After prior TKI, ORR was 37.5% and PFS 5.4 months. GI toxicity was very common (vomiting 79%). The evidence is early-phase only. Ma Y, Zhao H, Xue J et al. Eur J Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35940055/
- **Chinese expert consensus on lorlatinib adverse effects.** This is practical guidance on hyperlipidaemia and CNS effects of lorlatinib: baseline assessment, monitoring and dose modification. It is consensus-based and changes no treatment recommendation. Zhou Q, Lu S, Li Y et al. Zhongguo Fei Ai Za Zhi 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36002192/
- **Pooled T-DXd ILD analysis (nine trials).** Across 1,150 heavily pretreated patients (17.7% lung cancer), adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis occurred in 15.4%, and 2.2% were grade 5. 87% of first events came within 12 months. The analysis names candidate risk factors: age <65, enrolment in Japan, dose >6.4 mg/kg, SpO2 <95%, renal impairment and lung comorbidity. The data are pooled from single-arm studies with mixed tumour types. Powell CA, Modi S, Iwata H et al. ESMO Open 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35963179/

### lung-key-evidence / nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-local-therapy

- **EMPOWER-Lung 3 part 2 (phase 3).** 466 patients with advanced NSCLC without EGFR/ALK/ROS1 alterations were randomised 2:1 to cemiplimab+platinum doublet or placebo+chemo. The trial stopped early for OS: median 21.9 vs 13.0 months (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.53–0.93). Grade ≥3 AEs occurred in 43.6% vs 31.4%. Chemotherapy alone is no longer the first-line standard, so this adds another PD-1 option rather than changing practice. Gogishvili M, Melkadze T, Makharadze T et al. Nat Med 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36008722/
- **ORIENT-11 final OS (phase 3).** 397 patients with non-squamous NSCLC received first-line sintilimab or placebo with pemetrexed-platinum. Median OS was 24.2 vs 16.8 months (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.50–0.85; crossover-adjusted HR 0.52). The comparator is no longer standard and the trial was China-only. Zhang L, Wang Z, Fang J et al. Lung Cancer 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35917647/
- **BFAST cohort C (phase 3, negative).** 471 patients with blood TMB ≥10 were randomised to first-line atezolizumab or chemotherapy. The trial missed its primary PFS endpoint in bTMB ≥16 (HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.59–1.00, P=0.053). Treatment discontinuation for AEs was 10% vs 20%. Blood TMB is not ready to select patients for ICI monotherapy. Peters S, Dziadziuszko R, Morabito A et al. Nat Med 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35995953/
- **TORG1630 (randomised phase 2/3).** 128 patients with previously treated, ICI-naive NSCLC received nivolumab plus docetaxel or nivolumab alone. Median OS was 23.1 vs 14.7 months (HR 0.63, 90% CI 0.42–0.95) and ORR 41.8% vs 14.0%, with more haematological and GI toxicity. The question has little relevance now that most patients receive ICI first line. The trial is small and Japanese. Taniguchi Y, Shimokawa T, Takiguchi Y et al. Clin Cancer Res 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35980349/
- **PACIFIC-6 (phase 2).** 117 frailer patients (60% PS >0) received durvalumab after sequential chemoradiotherapy. Grade 3–4 possibly-related AEs within 6 months occurred in 4.3%. Median PFS was 10.9 months and 12-month OS 84.1%. The trial supports durvalumab when concurrent CRT is not possible, but it is single-arm and survival data are immature. Garassino MC, Mazieres J, Reck M et al. J Thorac Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35961520/
- **Neoadjuvant durvalumab ± sub-ablative radiation, surgical outcomes (randomised phase 2).** 60 patients were randomised, and 26 per arm had resection. There was no 30- or 90-day mortality, and grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 38% in both arms. This report covers feasibility and safety only. Lee B, Mynard N, Nasar A et al. J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36028357/
- **Expert consensus on perioperative treatment for NSCLC (Chinese).** This item has no abstract, so the recommendations cannot be summarised from it. Duan J, Tan F, Bi N et al. Transl Lung Cancer Res 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35958323/
- **IFCT-0302 (phase 3).** 1,775 patients with resected NSCLC were randomised to CT-based follow-up or x-ray-based follow-up. Median OS was 10.3 vs 8.5 years, a non-significant difference (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.83–1.10). CT detected more recurrences and second primaries (4.5% vs 3.0%) without a survival gain. The trial does not show that CT surveillance saves lives. Accrual ran 2005–2012. Westeel V, Foucher P, Scherpereel A et al. Lancet Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35964621/
- **Cadonilimab: First Approval (drug profile).** Cadonilimab is a PD-1/CTLA-4 bispecific, approved in China in June 2022 for cervical cancer. It is being developed in lung cancer among other indications. This is a journal profile, not a lung indication. Keam SJ. Drugs 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35986837/
- **ctDNA change and ICI benefit, pooled across five trials.** On-treatment ctDNA reductions were associated with OS (HR 2.28, 95% CI 1.62–3.20) and PFS (HR 1.76). This supports ctDNA as an early response marker. The analysis is associative and assay methods were heterogeneous. Vega DM, Nishimura KK, Zariffa N et al. JCO Precis Oncol 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35952319/

### lung-vaccines-cell / car-t-solid / lung-repurposed

- **NEO-PV-01 personalised neoantigen vaccine (phase 1b).** 38 patients received the vaccine with pemetrexed-carboplatin-pembrolizumab first line for non-squamous NSCLC. There were no treatment-related serious AEs. De novo neoantigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ responses appeared, with epitope spread including to KRAS G12C/G12V. The study measured immunogenicity only and had no control arm, so it says nothing about efficacy. Awad MM, Govindan R, Balogh KN et al. Cancer Cell 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36027916/
- **Anti-mesothelin CAR-T in ovarian cancer.** The paper reports preclinical in vivo activity plus three patients in an investigator-initiated trial. There was no CRS above grade 2, two had stable disease (PFS 5.8 and 4.6 months) and CAR expression was transient. With n=3, no efficacy conclusion is possible. Chen J, Hu J, Gu L et al. Cancer Immunol Immunother 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35925286/
- **ROR1 CAR-T, results posted (NCT02706392, phase 1, Fred Hutchinson, n=21).** The trial covers ROR1+ CLL, MCL, ALL, stage IV NSCLC and TNBC. Results were posted to ClinicalTrials.gov, and the record carries no efficacy summary. https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02706392
- **Statins and ICB in NSCLC (preclinical with clinical correlation).** Statins turned immune-cold NSCLC inflamed in vivo and enhanced ICB response. In-house data and a meta-analysis are cited as supporting benefit in patients. The patient data are observational and prone to confounding by indication and immortal-time bias. Mao W, Cai Y, Chen D et al. JCI Insight 2022. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35943796/
