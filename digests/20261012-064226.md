## High

### nsclc-drivers / egfr-nsclc / nsclc-resistance-nextgen
- **CROWN interim analysis: lorlatinib vs crizotinib first-line in advanced ALK-positive NSCLC (phase 3, NEJM, n=296).** At 12 months, 78% vs 39% were alive and progression-free by BICR (HR 0.28, 95% CI 0.19–0.41). ORR was 76% vs 58%, and intracranial response 82% vs 23% (71% intracranial CR with lorlatinib). Grade 3–4 AEs were more frequent (72% vs 56%), mainly hyperlipidaemia, along with oedema, weight gain, neuropathy and cognitive effects. It is a planned interim analysis with immature OS. The comparator, crizotinib, is no longer the first-line standard, so the result does not show superiority over alectinib or brigatinib. Shaw AT, Bauer TM, de Marinis F et al. *N Engl J Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33207094/
- **JUNIPER: abemaciclib vs erlotinib in pretreated KRAS-mutant NSCLC (phase 3, n=453).** OS, the primary endpoint, was not improved (7.4 vs 7.8 months, HR 0.97). PFS (3.6 vs 1.9 months) and ORR (8.9% vs 2.7%) favoured abemaciclib. It is a negative trial, and the comparator, erlotinib, is not standard in KRAS-mutant disease. Goldman JW, Mazieres J, Barlesi F et al. *Front Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33194700/
- **ADJUVANT/CTONG1104 quality of life: adjuvant gefitinib vs vinorelbine-cisplatin in resected stage II–IIIA (N1–N2) EGFR-mutant NSCLC (phase 3).** Time to HRQoL deterioration was longer with gefitinib (FACT-L median 69 vs 6 weeks, HR 0.62; TOI HR 0.51). This was a secondary endpoint in an open-label design. It is overtaken by ADAURA and osimertinib. Zeng J, Mao WM, Chen QX et al. *Lung Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33186858/
- **Erlotinib + RT vs etoposide-cisplatin + RT in unresectable stage III EGFR-mutant NSCLC (randomised phase 2, China, n=40).** PFS was 24.5 vs 9.0 months (HR 0.10, 0.03–0.39), ORR 70% vs 62%, and AEs similar. The trial is very small, accrual was slow, and the authors say a phase 3 trial is not feasible. It does not establish a standard. Xing L, Wu G, Wang L et al. *Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33220395/
- **FDA Approval Summary: selpercatinib.** It describes the accelerated approval of 8 May 2020 for metastatic RET fusion-positive NSCLC and RET-altered thyroid cancers, based on LIBRETTO-001 ORR (64%, 95% CI 54–73, in platinum-pretreated NSCLC). Label warnings cover hepatotoxicity, hypertension, QT prolongation and haemorrhage. It is a journal summary of a decision already made, so it is rated high. Bradford D, Larkins E, Mushti SL et al. *Clin Cancer Res*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33239432/
- **"Pralsetinib: First Approval" (drug profile).** It reports FDA accelerated approval for metastatic RET fusion-positive NSCLC, with thyroid indications under US review and NSCLC under EU review. Markham A. *Drugs*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33136236/

### nsclc-immunotherapy / lung-key-evidence / lung-top-journals
- **KEYNOTE-042 China: pembrolizumab vs chemotherapy first-line in PD-L1-positive NSCLC (n=262).** OS HR was 0.63 (0.43–0.94) for TPS ≥50%, 0.66 for TPS ≥20% and 0.67 (0.50–0.89) for TPS ≥1%. Grade 3–5 TRAEs were 19.5% vs 68.8%. It is consistent with the global trial. Chemotherapy-immunotherapy, not chemotherapy, is now the usual comparator for TPS 1–49%. Wu YL, Zhang L, Fan Y et al. *Int J Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33231285/
- **POPLAR and OAK final OS: atezolizumab vs docetaxel in pretreated NSCLC (n=287 and n=1,225).** In OAK, median OS was 13.3 vs 9.8 months (HR 0.78, 0.68–0.89), and 4-year OS 15.5% vs 8.7%. The benefit held across PD-L1 and histology groups. It confirms an established second-line standard, which now applies mainly to immunotherapy-naive patients. Mazieres J, Rittmeyer A, Gadgeel S et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33166718/
- **CYPRESS 1 and 2: pegilodecakin added to pembrolizumab (PD-L1 ≥50%, n=101) or nivolumab (PD-L1 0–49%, n=52) (randomised phase 2).** There was no gain in ORR (47% vs 44%) or PFS (HR 0.94), and OS trended worse (HR 1.51). Grade ≥3 TRAEs were 62% vs 19%. Both trials are negative. Spigel D, Jotte R, Nemunaitis J et al. *J Thorac Oncol*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33166722/

### lung-screening
- **Meta-analysis of 8 LDCT screening RCTs (n=90,475).** Lung cancer mortality fell (RR 0.81, 0.74–0.89; ARR 0.4%, NNS 250). The reduction in all-cause mortality was not significant (RR 0.96, 0.92–1.01). With the longest follow-up, about 20% of cancers were overdiagnosed. This is relevant to the pending Norwegian screening decision. Trial heterogeneity and differing comparators (usual care vs CXR) are limitations. Ebell MH, Bentivegna M, Hulme C. *Ann Fam Med*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33168683/

### lung-nordic
- **Swedish-Norwegian cohort of resected NSCLC: PD-L1 (22C3) expression (n=875: 538 Norway, 337 Sweden).** TPS ≥50% was found in only 9.5%, lower in stage I, and higher with EGFR wild-type and KRAS-mutant tumours. PD-L1 was not independently prognostic. It indicates how many Nordic patients would be eligible for PD-L1-restricted perioperative immunotherapy. It is retrospective and single-assay. Yu H, Brustugun OT, Ekman S et al. *Clin Lung Cancer*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33214079/
- **Sex and survival after lung cancer surgery: Swedish nationwide cohort (n=6,356).** Women had lower mortality after weighting (HR 0.73, 0.67–0.79), with an absolute 10-year survival difference of 12%. The difference held across age, comorbidity, socioeconomic status, surgery type and stage. It is observational, and residual confounding is possible. Sachs E, Sartipy U, Jackson V. *Chest*, 2020. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33217414/
